Choose a dental PPC manager on four things: they work in an account your practice owns, they track booked patients rather than clicks, they understand health advertising rules, and they can explain what they changed and why. Whether that is a freelancer, an agency or someone in-house matters less than those four.

Be careful with arrangements where you never see the account, or where leads are shared with other practices.

For general hiring advice, see [how to hire a Google Ads expert](/learn/how-to-hire-a-google-ads-expert/). This article covers what is specific to dental practices.

## The options

**In-house.** A team member runs the account. It keeps knowledge in the practice, but Google Ads changes often, and an account that gets an hour a month tends to drift.

**Freelance specialist.** One person who manages the account directly. You talk to whoever makes the changes, and the fee goes into the work rather than overheads. [Google Ads freelancer vs agency](/learn/google-ads-freelancer-vs-agency/) covers the trade-offs.

**General agency.** Useful if you also want design, SEO and social media from one supplier. Check who actually works on your account and how often.

**Dental marketing company.** Many specialise in dentistry and offer packages. Some are excellent. Check carefully who owns the account, the data and the website pages the ads send people to.

## What matters most in a dental account

### 1. The account belongs to your practice

The Google Ads account should be registered to the practice and paid from your own payment method, with the manager given access. If you part ways, you keep the history, the conversion data and everything that was learned. Ask this before anything else.

### 2. Tracking measures patients, not clicks

A good manager will ask how calls and bookings are recorded, connect ads to your booking or practice management system where possible, and report new patients and accepted treatment. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) explains what proper tracking looks like.

### 3. They understand health advertising rules

Dental ads fall under Google's healthcare policies and your country's rules. Your manager should avoid unsupported claims, check trademark rules for brand names such as aligner systems, and handle remarketing and patient privacy carefully.

### 4. Campaigns follow your priorities

Implants, emergencies and check-ups need different campaigns and budgets. A manager who puts everything in one campaign will spend most of the budget on whatever is cheapest to click, not on the treatment you want more of.

## Questions to ask before you hire

1. **Will the account be in my practice's name,** with my payment method?
2. **How will you track new patients and accepted treatment,** not just calls?
3. **What will you check in my account first?** The right answer usually starts with tracking
4. **How will you structure campaigns** for emergencies, general patients and high-value treatment?
5. **How do you handle health ad rules and patient privacy?**
6. **What will I see each month,** and can I speak to the person who makes the changes?
7. **What are the contract terms** and notice period?

## Red flags

- **You cannot see the account,** or the manager owns it
- **Leads are shared** between several practices, or sold to you per enquiry from a system you do not control
- **Reports show only clicks, impressions and calls,** with no link to booked patients
- **Guaranteed results** before anyone has looked at your account and your diary
- **Long contracts** with no way out if results are poor
- **Ad spend is invoiced through the manager** with an unclear markup, rather than paid directly to Google

[How to tell if your agency is doing anything](/learn/is-your-google-ads-agency-doing-anything/) lists more warning signs.

## What good management looks like month to month

- Search terms reviewed and negative keywords added. See [negative keywords for dental practices](/learn/dental-negative-keywords/)
- Budgets moved towards the campaigns producing patients at the best cost
- Ads and landing pages tested, one change at a time
- Tracking checked after any change to your website or booking system
- A short report you can understand: new patients, cost per patient, what changed and what is next

## What it costs

Fees are usually a fixed monthly amount, a percentage of ad spend, or a project fee for setup and audits. Compare like with like, and remember the ad spend itself is separate. [How much do Google Ads cost for dentists?](/learn/dental-google-ads-cost/) covers both.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a dental practice use a dental marketing company or a Google Ads specialist?

Either can work. What matters is that the account is in your name, tracking measures booked patients, and the person running it understands both Google Ads and health ad rules. Ask the same questions of both.

### Who should own the Google Ads account?

The practice. The account should be registered to your business and paid from your own payment method, with the manager given access. That keeps your data and history if you change provider.

### How do I know if my dental PPC manager is doing a good job?

Look at new patients and cost per new patient over time, not clicks. Ask what changed last month and why. A good manager can explain both clearly.

### Can I switch PPC managers without losing results?

Yes, if the account is in your name. The new manager inherits the history and data. If the old provider owns the account, you may have to start again, which is why ownership matters.

### How often should a dental Google Ads account be managed?

Most accounts need attention every week or two: search terms, budgets and tracking. New or high-spend accounts need more frequent checks in the first months.
