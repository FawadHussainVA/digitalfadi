Choose the bid strategy that matches how much reliable conversion data you have and what you are trying to grow. With little or untrustworthy conversion data, keep control with Manual CPC or Maximise clicks with a bid limit. With a steady flow of genuine conversions, use Maximise conversions, then add a target CPA. If your conversions carry real revenue values, use Maximise conversion value, then add a target ROAS.

The strategy matters less than the data it learns from. Most bidding problems are tracking problems in disguise.

## The short answer, by situation

1. **New account, or conversion tracking you do not fully trust:** Manual CPC, or Maximise clicks with a maximum bid limit
2. **Tracking is right and conversions arrive every week:** Maximise conversions
3. **Maximise conversions is working and you know your affordable cost per lead:** add a target CPA
4. **Online shop with accurate order values:** Maximise conversion value
5. **That is working and you know your break-even ROAS:** add a target ROAS
6. **Brand campaign, where you just want to appear for your own name:** target impression share, or Manual CPC

## The strategies in plain English

- **Manual CPC.** You set the most you will pay per click. Full control, and full responsibility for adjusting bids by hand
- **Maximise clicks.** Google buys as many clicks as the budget allows. Useful for gathering data early, but it chases cheap clicks, not good ones, so set a maximum bid limit
- **Maximise conversions.** Google spends the budget trying to get the most conversions it can
- **Target CPA.** An optional target inside Maximise conversions: get conversions at around this average cost
- **Maximise conversion value.** Google aims for the most total conversion value, such as revenue, rather than the most conversions
- **Target ROAS.** An optional target inside Maximise conversion value: get value at around this return on ad spend
- **Target impression share.** Google bids to appear for a chosen share of searches, often used for brand terms

## The question that decides everything: what are you counting?

Automated bidding optimises towards whatever the account counts as a conversion. If that includes page views, button clicks or a duplicate tag firing twice, the strategy will get very good at producing those, and the reports will look excellent while enquiries stay flat.

Before switching to any conversion-based strategy, check that every conversion action counted is a real enquiry, call or sale, and that each is counted once. [Why are my Google Ads not converting?](/learn/why-are-my-google-ads-not-converting/) covers the usual faults.

## How much data is enough

Conversion-based strategies learn from patterns, and patterns need volume. A handful of conversions a month gives the system very little to work with, and a target CPA on that little data behaves more like a setting than a strategy.

If conversions arrive every week, Maximise conversions has something to learn from. If they arrive a few times a month, keep the strategy simple, concentrate the budget on fewer campaigns, and let the data build before asking the algorithm to hit a number.

## How to set a target without breaking the campaign

Start the target near what the campaign has actually achieved recently, not where you would like it to be.

A target CPA set far below reality often makes things worse rather than better. The strategy narrows spend onto the few auctions it is most confident about, which tend to be the expensive ones, so volume drops and cost per click rises. [Why your Google Ads cost per click went up](/learn/why-your-cost-per-click-went-up/) explains that loop. The same happens with a target ROAS set above anything the account has ever delivered.

Then tighten it gradually. Google says a target change does not put the campaign back into Learning status or wipe what the strategy has already learned, but the campaign still needs time to show what the new target does. Google's own advice is to avoid several target changes within a single conversion cycle, so make one change and let the numbers settle before the next.

To find a sensible target in the first place:

- For leads, work out the most you can pay per enquiry and still make money. The "Work backwards" section of the [metrics calculator](/tools/metrics-calculator/) turns that into a maximum cost per click
- For online shops, the [break-even ROAS calculator](/tools/break-even-roas-calculator/) gives the floor below which every sale loses money, and the target ROAS that leaves the profit you want

## The learning period

A campaign shows a Learning status for three reasons only: a new or reactivated bid strategy, campaigns, ad groups or keywords added to or removed from the strategy, or a significant change to the conversion goals it optimises for. Google says the status never lasts more than seven days. Changes to budgets, targets, ads or targeting do not trigger it.

Performance can still take longer than that to settle, especially with few conversions or a long sales cycle. The most common mistake is reacting to early swings by changing the target again and again. That no longer restarts learning, but it makes it impossible to see what any single change did. Make one change, then leave it alone long enough to judge. [Does changing your budget reset Google Ads learning?](/learn/does-changing-budget-reset-google-ads-learning/) covers what does and does not trigger learning.

## When leads are not all equal

Maximise conversions treats every enquiry as equal. If some leads are worth far more than others, or many never become customers, the strategy will happily find more of the cheap, poor ones.

The fix is to tell Google which leads turned into customers, by importing that outcome back into the account, and to give conversion actions values that reflect what they are worth. Without that, judge lead campaigns on booked work rather than on form fills. [Why your Google Ads calls are not becoming jobs](/learn/why-google-ads-calls-are-not-becoming-jobs/) covers this for local businesses.

## Common bidding mistakes

1. **Switching to a target strategy before tracking is right.** The algorithm learns the wrong lesson quickly
2. **Setting an aspirational target.** Volume collapses and cost per click rises
3. **Changing targets every few days.** Each change needs time to show its effect, so constant edits leave you unable to judge any of them
4. **Judging a new strategy in its first week.** The numbers are not settled yet
5. **Running Maximise clicks without a bid limit.** It buys cheap, irrelevant clicks to hit volume

## When to change strategy

Move to a more automated strategy when conversion tracking is verified and conversions arrive steadily. Change one campaign at a time, compare against a similar period before the change, and keep the old settings written down so you can go back.

If you are not sure whether the account's conversions can be trusted, that is the first thing a [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/) checks, before anything about bids.
