Dental Google Ads waste money in predictable places: job and training searches, DIY and home remedy searches, product searches, free or low-cost care you do not offer, and areas patients will not travel from. A starter list of negative keywords blocks most of this before it costs anything. Then review the search terms report every week or two, because every practice attracts its own waste. The list below is a starting point to adapt, not a list to paste in whole.

## How to use this list

- **Only add what does not fit your practice.** If you offer payment plans, do not block "finance". If you treat children, do not block "kids"
- **Use phrase or exact negatives** for words that could be part of a good search. Use broad negatives only for words that never fit
- **Apply lists at the right level.** A shared list for terms that are wrong for every campaign, campaign-level negatives for terms that only matter in one place
- **Check for conflicts.** A negative that blocks a keyword you bid on stops that keyword showing. The [negative keyword generator](/tools/negative-keyword-generator/) checks for this

[How to find negative keywords](/learn/how-to-find-negative-keywords/) explains the method in detail.

## Jobs, training and education

People looking to work in dentistry search with many of the same words as patients.

- jobs, job, vacancies, careers, hiring, salary
- dental nurse jobs, hygienist jobs, receptionist jobs
- course, courses, training, school, degree, apprenticeship, qualification
- dental school, dental student, exam, CPD

These are almost always safe to block across the whole account.

## DIY, home remedies and information

- how to, at home, home remedy, natural remedy, DIY
- pain relief at home, tooth pain remedies
- symptoms, causes, meaning, pictures, diagram
- wikipedia, definition

Be careful with information searches. Someone reading about a problem may become a patient. Block clear DIY intent, and judge information searches by results rather than blocking them all.

## Products

- toothpaste, mouthwash, floss, toothbrush, electric toothbrush
- whitening kit, whitening strips, at home whitening, whitening pen
- aligner kit, at home aligners, retainer case
- denture adhesive, denture cleaner

Block these unless you sell the products or offer the treatment they refer to.

## Free and low-cost care you do not offer

- free dental, free dentist, charity, voucher, grant, government
- cheap dentist, low cost dentist, discount dental plan
- dental school clinic, student clinic, community clinic

Only block these where they do not fit. If your practice offers a free consultation or low-cost care, keep the matching terms. Searches for government-funded care, such as NHS dentists in the UK or public dental programmes elsewhere, should be blocked only if you do not take those patients.

## Other professions and wrong services

- vet, veterinary, dog, cat (people searching for pet dental care)
- dental lab, dental technician, dental supplies, dental equipment
- dental insurance quotes, if you are not an insurer

Also block services you do not provide, such as orthodontics, oral surgery or paediatric dentistry, if they are not offered at your practice. That keeps budget on treatments you can actually deliver.

## Location

Block towns, regions and countries that are too far for patients to travel. For specialist treatment such as implants, people travel further, so the location negatives for those campaigns may be lighter than for emergency or routine care. [Google Ads location targeting](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) covers the location setting that causes most out-of-area clicks.

## Emergency campaigns

Emergency dental campaigns attract their own waste:

- home remedies and pain relief searches
- hospital, A&E, emergency room, urgent care centre, if you are not one
- searches for services open at hours you cannot cover

[Emergency dentist Google Ads](/learn/emergency-dentist-google-ads/) covers the rest of that campaign.

## Review the search terms report regularly

Starter lists only go so far. Every practice has its own patterns:

1. Open the search terms report for the last 30 days
2. Sort by cost, and look at searches with spend and no conversions
3. Group them by the word that makes them irrelevant, and block that word
4. Repeat every week or two for the first months, then monthly

The [n-gram analyser](/tools/n-gram-analyser/) groups searches by repeated words, which makes patterns much easier to spot.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which negative keywords should every dental practice use?

Job and training terms, such as jobs, careers and courses, are wrong for almost every practice. Product and DIY terms usually are too. Everything else depends on the treatments you offer and the patients you want.

### Should I block "cheap" or "free" from dental ads?

Only if you do not offer low-cost care or free consultations. If you do, keep the matching terms and block the rest.

### How often should negative keywords be updated?

Every week or two for new campaigns, then monthly once the account is stable. Accounts using broad match need more frequent reviews.

### Can negative keywords block good patients?

Yes, if they are too broad or conflict with your keywords. Check that no negative blocks a keyword you bid on, and use phrase or exact negatives for words that could be part of a relevant search.

### Do negative keywords work in Performance Max?

Performance Max supports negative keywords, though they work differently from Search campaigns. Check the current options in your account, and review its search term reports regularly.
