Google Ads Editor 2.14 changes what happens when you create a new Search campaign: AI Max, text asset automation and Final URL expansion are now switched on by default. Existing campaigns are not changed.

If you build campaigns in Google Ads Editor and do not want Google choosing extra searches, writing extra ad text or sending people to other pages on your site, check those settings on every new Search campaign before you post.

This guide covers what changed in Google Ads Editor 2.14, what each default does, and a short checklist for posting safely.

## What changed in Google Ads Editor 2.14

The headline change is the new default for Search campaigns. When you create one in Google Ads Editor 2.14, three settings start switched on:

- **AI Max:** Google's bundle of Search automation, including search term matching beyond your keyword list
- **Text asset automation:** Google can generate extra headlines and descriptions from your landing page, ads and keywords
- **Final URL expansion:** Google can send people to a different page on your domain if it judges that page more relevant

The same release also adds account-level settings for new customer acquisition and customer retention, a bidding option to focus on lapsed customers, channel prioritisation and budget caps. [Channel prioritisation and budget allocation](/learn/google-ads-channel-prioritisation-budget-allocation/) explains how those channel controls work.

## Why the defaults matter

These are not small switches. Together they can change which searches you appear for, what your ads say and where people land.

- **Pinned headlines stop being guaranteed.** When Final URL expansion is on, pinned responsive search ad assets are not used if Google picks a more relevant URL. If a headline must always show, for legal or brand reasons, Final URL expansion has to be off
- **Landing pages can change.** Final URL expansion can send traffic to pages you did not choose, such as a blog post or an old offer page. Use URL exclusions for pages that should never receive ad traffic
- **Tracking templates need testing.** If you use a tracking template, check that dynamic landing pages from Final URL expansion still track correctly before posting
- **Spend can widen.** Search term matching reaches searches beyond your keywords, which can find new customers or waste money on irrelevant clicks

[AI Max for Search: what it does and when to use it](/learn/ai-max-for-search/) explains each component in more detail.

## Checklist before you post from Google Ads Editor 2.14

For every new Search campaign:

1. **Decide on AI Max deliberately.** If you want it, keep it. If you are building a tightly controlled campaign, switch it off
2. **Check text asset automation** if your industry has strict wording rules, such as legal, healthcare or finance
3. **Check Final URL expansion,** especially if you pin headlines or send traffic to specific landing pages
4. **Add URL exclusions** for pages that should never get ad traffic: careers, blog, checkout or old offers
5. **Confirm bidding.** AI Max features need conversion-based Smart Bidding to work as intended, so check that conversion tracking counts real leads or sales first
6. **Review in the interface after posting,** and watch the search terms report closely for the first weeks

Existing campaigns you edit in Google Ads Editor 2.14 keep their current settings. The defaults only apply to campaigns you create.

## When the new defaults make sense

The defaults suit accounts with reliable conversion tracking, enough conversions for Smart Bidding to learn from, and a website where most pages are good landing pages. They suit tightly controlled accounts less well: regulated industries, accounts with few conversions, or sites with pages that should never receive paid traffic.

If you are unsure, launch with AI Max off and test it later with an [experiment](/learn/google-ads-experiments-ab-testing-guide/), so you can measure the difference instead of guessing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is new in Google Ads Editor 2.14?

The biggest change is that new Search campaigns created in Google Ads Editor 2.14 have AI Max, text asset automation and Final URL expansion switched on by default. The release also adds account-level new customer acquisition and retention settings, a lapsed-customer bidding option, channel prioritisation and budget caps.

### Does Google Ads Editor 2.14 change my existing campaigns?

No. The new defaults apply to Search campaigns you create in Google Ads Editor 2.14. Existing campaigns keep their current settings unless you change them.

### How do I turn off AI Max in Google Ads Editor?

Check the AI Max settings on each new Search campaign before posting, and switch them off if you do not want them. You can also review and change AI Max settings in the Google Ads interface after posting.

### Why are my pinned headlines not showing?

If Final URL expansion or URL inclusions are on, pinned responsive search ad assets are not guaranteed to show. Turn off Final URL expansion if specific headlines must always appear.

### Should I keep AI Max on for new campaigns?

Only if your conversion tracking is reliable and you are ready to review search terms regularly. Otherwise, start without it and test it with an experiment once the campaign has a stable baseline.
