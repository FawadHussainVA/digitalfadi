Channel prioritisation and budget allocation are two new Google Ads controls that let you decide which channels inside a campaign get your budget first and how much the lower-priority ones may spend. In a Search campaign, for example, you can make Google Search high priority and put search partners and the Display Network on low priority with a spending cap. They matter now because of a fixed date: in February 2027, Search campaigns that have the Display Network switched on move to the new system automatically, and their current spending setup goes away.

If none of your Search campaigns use the Display Network, there is no deadline to worry about. If any do, check them before February.

## What the two controls do

- **Channel prioritisation** lets you mark each channel in a campaign as High or Low priority. High-priority channels are funded first, and lower-priority channels receive what is left
- **Budget allocation** lets you set the maximum share of the daily budget a low-priority channel may spend, as a percentage. Google treats this as a best-effort limit rather than an exact cap

The controls can be used whether or not a campaign is limited by budget.

## Which campaigns and channels are covered

According to Google's help page, [About channel prioritisation and budget allocation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/18385152):

- **Search campaigns:** Google Search, search partners and the Google Display Network
- **Performance Max campaigns:** Google channels, Google search partners and the Google Display Network
- **Demand Gen campaigns:** Google channels and the Google Display Network, with prioritisation only. Budget allocation is not supported for Demand Gen
- **Shopping campaigns** are included in the rollout as well

Google is rolling the controls out globally over the coming months, so they may not appear in every account yet. Where they are available, look for the Channel prioritisation card in the settings.

## The February 2027 change for Search campaigns

Today, when a Search campaign has the Display Network switched on (often called Display Expansion), Google already favours search results over Display behind the scenes. That built-in arrangement is being retired.

From February 2027:

1. Search campaigns with the Display Network switched on move automatically to channel prioritisation and budget allocation
2. The current spending arrangement disappears
3. Spend may shift between Google Search, search partners and the Display Network

Google's own advice for keeping today's behaviour is to set the Display Network to **Low priority** with a **50% budget allocation**. If you set channel priorities on a Search campaign before February, the new settings take effect straight away and replace the old behaviour for that campaign.

## Should your Search campaigns use Display at all?

The deadline is a good moment to ask a more useful question than how to keep things as they are.

Display placements reach people browsing websites and apps, not people searching for what you sell. For many lead generation and local service businesses, Display clicks inside a Search campaign convert at a far lower rate than search clicks, and the spend is easy to miss because it sits inside a campaign everyone thinks of as Search.

Check before deciding:

1. In each Search campaign, segment the results by network for the last 90 days
2. Compare cost, conversions and cost per conversion for Google Search, search partners and Display
3. Check that the conversions counted are real enquiries or sales, not page views

Then choose:

- **Display costs more per conversion and brings little:** switch the Display Network off in that Search campaign. That avoids the February change entirely
- **Display performs acceptably:** set it to Low priority with a cap you are comfortable with. Google's 50% keeps today's behaviour; a lower figure limits exposure
- **Display is genuinely strong:** consider a dedicated Display or Demand Gen campaign with its own budget and creative, rather than leaving it inside Search

## What to do with search partners

Search partners are other websites that show Google search ads, such as some search engines and sites with Google-powered search. Quality varies widely. The new controls give you a middle option between on and off: set search partners to Low priority with a small allocation, then compare their cost per conversion with Google Search before deciding whether to raise it, keep it or switch it off.

## What it means for Performance Max

Performance Max has always decided for itself how to split budget across channels. The new controls give you some say: you can keep Google channels at high priority and limit how much of the budget goes to search partners or the Display Network.

Use this carefully. Performance Max is designed to move budget to wherever it finds conversions, so cap a channel because the evidence says it underperforms, not on instinct. [Performance Max vs Search campaigns](/learn/performance-max-vs-search-campaigns/) covers when Performance Max is the right tool in the first place, and [Performance Max: what it hides](/learn/performance-max-what-it-hides/) shows how to see where its spend goes.

## A checklist before February 2027

1. List every Search campaign with the Display Network switched on
2. Pull 90 days of results by network for each one
3. Decide per campaign: Display off, Display at Low priority with a cap, or Display moved to its own campaign
4. Review search partners at the same time
5. Apply the settings now rather than waiting for the automatic move, so any shift in spend happens when you are watching
6. Compare results by network again four weeks after the change

## Quick answers

- **Is this mandatory?** The controls are optional, except that Search campaigns with Display switched on are moved to the new system in February 2027
- **What keeps my current setup?** Display Network at Low priority with a 50% budget allocation, according to Google
- **Does it affect Search campaigns with Display switched off?** No
- **Does Demand Gen get budget caps?** No. It supports prioritisation, but not budget allocation

If you are not sure how much of your Search budget currently goes to Display or search partners, that split is one of the first things a [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/) checks, because it is often money that can be redirected the same day.
