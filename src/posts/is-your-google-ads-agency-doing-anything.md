The hardest thing about hiring a Google Ads agency is that you cannot see the work. A report arrives, numbers go up and down, and there is no obvious way to tell whether anybody opened the account that month.

You do not need to be a specialist to check. Every one of these takes a few minutes inside your own account, and you should have access to it.

## Look at the change history

This is the single most useful screen in Google Ads and almost nobody uses it.

Tools and settings, then Change history. It lists every change made in the account, by whom, and when. Set the date range to the last ninety days.

What you are looking for is not volume. Plenty of changes can mean somebody fiddling for the sake of an activity report, and a well-run mature account may genuinely need few. What you want to see is whether changes cluster the day before each report.

An account with no changes for ninety days is not being managed. An account where every change happens in the two days before invoicing is being managed for the report rather than the result.

## Check the negative keyword list

Search terms arrive constantly, so negatives should grow constantly.

Open the negative keyword lists and look at when terms were added. If the list was built at setup and never touched, nobody has read a search terms report since.

This is the most reliable single indicator of ongoing work, because it is the one task that cannot be automated away or batched.

## Ask for brand and non-brand separately

Ask one question: what is the cost per lead on non-brand?

If the answer takes a week, or comes back as a blended figure, the account is probably not structured to answer it. Brand searches convert cheaply and flatter every average they are included in.

A blended cost per acquisition that includes people searching your own company name is not a measure of what the advertising achieved.

## Look at what Performance Max is actually doing

If the account runs Performance Max, ask which channels the spend went to and whether brand terms are excluded.

Both are answerable. Placement and channel data can be pulled out with reports and scripts. If the answer is that Performance Max is a black box and nobody can see inside, that is a statement about the agency rather than about the campaign.

More detail in [Performance Max: what it hides and how to see it](/learn/performance-max-what-it-hides/).

## Read the report for decisions, not numbers

Most monthly reports are a screenshot of metrics with a paragraph saying performance remained steady.

A useful report answers three things: what changed this month, what it did, and what happens next. If you cannot find those three in the document, it was produced to be sent rather than to be read.

Impressions and click-through rate are not results. Cost per lead, cost per sale and volume are.

## Check who owns the account

Open the account and look at who the administrator is.

If the agency owns the Google Ads account rather than managing yours, you cannot take the history, the conversion data or the learning with you when you leave. That is not a technical detail, it is a retention strategy.

Your account should sit under your business and your billing, with the agency holding access. Ending the relationship should mean removing their access, not starting again from zero.

## What good looks like

- Changes spread across the month, not clustered before invoicing
- Negatives added regularly
- Brand and non-brand reported separately without being asked
- Performance Max explained at channel level
- Reports that name decisions rather than restate metrics
- The account under your ownership
