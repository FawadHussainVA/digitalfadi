Every HVAC account collects irrelevant clicks. Someone searching for a furnace filter size, a job application, or a how-to video is unlikely to call you for a repair, but Google will happily charge you for the click if your keyword matched. Negative keywords stop that leak.

## What a negative keyword does

A negative keyword tells Google not to show your ad when a search contains that word or phrase. Used well, it protects the budget for searches that fit your business. Used badly, it can block valid customers, so it needs regular checking.

Negative keywords can be added at campaign level, at ad group level, or as a shared list applied to several campaigns. For most HVAC companies, a shared list covering universal terms is the efficient choice, with extra terms added to individual campaigns where needed.

## The core list for HVAC

These are the groups that most HVAC accounts should exclude from the start:

- **Jobs and careers:** jobs, career, hiring, salary, apprenticeship, school, training, certification, course
- **DIY and tutorials:** how to, diy, yourself, tutorial, video, youtube, step by step
- **Parts and retail:** parts, part, filter size, wholesale, supplier, capacitor price, replacement part
- **Free or cheap intent:** free, cheap, used, second hand, rebuild
- **Wrong trades:** plumbing, plumber, electrician, roofing, pool heater (unless you do this work)

Adjust this list to your own services. If you do not install commercial systems, exclude "commercial" or "industrial" terms. If you do not offer a particular brand, consider excluding that brand's name, while being careful about policy around brand terms.

## Check the search terms report

The list above is a starting point, not the finish. Each week, open the search terms report and sort by cost. Look at the most expensive searches that produced no calls, forms or bookings. Ask whether the customer could realistically have needed your service. If not, add a negative keyword. If yes, check whether the match type or the keyword itself is too loose.

Be cautious with words that sound negative but can be genuine customer language. "Cheap" can appear in a search from someone who is about to buy a repair, and "replacement" is often exactly what a homeowner needs. Look at the full search, not the single word.

## A common mistake

Some accounts add so many negatives that they block legitimate traffic. A campaign with dozens of blocked terms can end up showing only to a narrow slice of searches, and performance falls. Keep the list focused on terms you have seen in your own data, and review it after any major change to the account.
