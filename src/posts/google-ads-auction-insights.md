Google Ads auction insights shows how often your ads appear in the same auctions as other advertisers, and how you compare with them. It is the clearest view you have of competition.

Use Google Ads auction insights to explain why costs rose, why impressions fell, or whether a new competitor has arrived, but read it alongside your own results, because a higher share of auctions is only good if those auctions make money.

## Where to find auction insights

Open a Search or Shopping campaign, ad group or keyword selection, then go to the Auction insights report in the Insights and reports area. Search campaigns show the full set of metrics; Shopping shows a smaller set. Change the date range to compare periods, such as this month with last month.

## The metrics, in plain terms

- **Impression share:** how often your ads showed out of the auctions you were eligible for. For competitors, how often they showed in the auctions you also took part in
- **Overlap rate:** how often a competitor's ad showed at the same time as yours
- **Position above rate:** when you both showed, how often their ad appeared above yours
- **Top of page rate:** how often an ad showed at the top of the page, above organic results
- **Absolute top of page rate:** how often it showed as the very first ad
- **Outranking share:** how often you ranked higher than a competitor, or showed when they did not

## Questions Google Ads auction insights can answer

**Why did my cost per click go up?** If a competitor's impression share or position above rate rose at the same time, more competition is the likely cause. [Why your cost per click went up](/learn/why-your-cost-per-click-went-up/) covers the other causes.

**Why did my impressions drop?** If your impression share fell while competitors held steady, look at budget, bids and ad quality. A budget-limited campaign loses impression share even without new competition.

**Has a new competitor arrived?** A new name with rising overlap rate means someone new is bidding on your searches.

**Is someone bidding on my brand?** Run auction insights on your brand campaign. Competitors appearing there are bidding on your name. [Should you bid on your own brand name?](/learn/should-you-bid-on-your-own-brand-name/) explains how to respond.

## What to act on, and what not to

**Act on:**
- A competitor's sudden rise in overlap and position above rate on your most profitable searches
- Lost impression share on campaigns that are profitable and limited by budget or rank
- New competitors on your brand searches

**Do not chase:**
- 100% impression share on every search. The last few percent are often the most expensive
- Outranking a competitor on searches that do not convert for you
- A competitor's top of page rate if your own cost per customer is healthy

The goal is profitable visibility, not winning every auction.

## How to use auction insights in a review

1. Compare this month with last month, for your top campaigns
2. Note any competitor whose overlap or position above rate changed sharply
3. Check your own impression share lost to budget and to rank
4. Decide: raise budget, improve ad quality, adjust bids, or leave it if results are healthy

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Google Ads auction insights?

Auction insights is a Google Ads report that compares your performance with other advertisers in the same auctions, using metrics such as impression share, overlap rate, position above rate and outranking share.

### Why can't I see auction insights?

The report needs enough data for the selected campaign, ad group or keywords and date range. Try a longer date range or a larger selection, and use it on Search or Shopping campaigns.

### What is a good impression share?

It depends on profitability. High impression share on your best-converting searches is valuable; chasing 100% on every search usually raises costs without enough extra customers.

### Can auction insights show competitors' budgets or bids?

No. It shows how often you appear in the same auctions and how you rank, not what competitors spend or bid.

### How often should I check auction insights?

Monthly in a normal review, and whenever costs or impressions change suddenly, so you can see whether competition explains the change.
