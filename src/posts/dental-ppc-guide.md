Dental PPC (pay-per-click) means paying Google to show your practice at the top of search results when people search for a dentist or a dental treatment, and paying only when someone clicks. For most practices it means Google Ads: Search campaigns for people actively looking, Local Services Ads where available, and sometimes Performance Max.

It works best for practices that know what a new patient is worth, answer the phone quickly and track booked appointments rather than clicks.

This guide explains how dental PPC works, which campaigns suit which goals, and how to tell whether it is working. For the full approach I use with dental clinics, see [Google Ads for dental clinics](/google-ads-for-dentists/).

## How dental PPC works

Every time someone searches, Google runs an auction among the advertisers who want to appear for that search. Your ad's position depends on:

- **Your bid:** how much you are willing to pay for a click
- **Ad quality:** how relevant your ad and landing page are to the search
- **Expected impact of your assets:** call buttons, locations, sitelinks and other additions

You pay only when someone clicks, and usually less than your maximum bid. The searches you appear for are controlled by your keywords, match types and negative keywords. [Google Ads match types explained](/learn/google-ads-keyword-match-types-explained/) covers how keywords decide which searches you reach.

## Which campaigns dental practices use

**Search campaigns.** Text ads on Google search results. The core of most dental accounts, because they reach people actively looking: "dentist near me", "emergency dentist", "dental implants in [town]". You choose the keywords, write the ads and control the budget.

**Local Services Ads.** In the US and some other markets, dentists can appear in Local Services Ads above the normal results, with a Google verification badge, and pay per lead rather than per click. They suit general and emergency enquiries where available. [Local Services Ads vs Google Ads](/learn/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads/) explains the trade-offs.

**Performance Max.** A campaign type that uses Google's automation across Search, Maps, YouTube and other places. It can work for practices with reliable conversion tracking, but it gives less control over which searches it reaches, so most dental practices start with Search. [Performance Max vs Search](/learn/performance-max-vs-search-campaigns/) explains when each fits.

## Organise campaigns by patient and treatment

Dental searches differ a lot in value and urgency. A well-built account separates them:

- **Emergency dentistry:** urgent, call-driven, won by availability. See [emergency dentist Google Ads](/learn/emergency-dentist-google-ads/)
- **General and new patients:** check-ups, hygiene, family dentistry
- **High-value treatment:** implants, aligners and cosmetic work, each with its own campaign. See [dental implant Google Ads](/learn/google-ads-for-dental-implants/) and [cosmetic dentistry Google Ads](/learn/cosmetic-dentistry-google-ads/)

When everything shares one budget, cheap check-up clicks use up the money that implant searches need.

## How AI search changes dental PPC

Google now shows ads in and around AI Overviews and AI Mode, where people ask longer, conversational questions such as "is it normal for a filling to hurt after a week". Your existing campaigns can appear there, but ads inside the AI answer usually need broad match or AI Max. That makes good negative keywords and accurate conversion tracking more important, because the automation decides which conversational searches to enter based on your data. [How AI Mode and AI Overviews change Google Ads](/learn/how-ai-mode-and-ai-overviews-change-google-ads/) covers this in detail.

## How long dental PPC takes to work

Ads usually start showing within a day of launch once approved. Results take longer: most accounts need several weeks of data before cost per new patient is a fair measure, and high-value treatment such as implants can take longer because patients research before booking. [How long does it take for Google Ads to work?](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/) explains what to expect week by week.

## How to know whether it is working

Clicks and calls are early signals. What matters is:

1. **New patients booked** from ads each month
2. **Cost per new patient,** compared with what a new patient is worth to the practice
3. **Accepted treatment,** for high-value work such as implants

That needs tracking that connects ads to bookings. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) shows the setup.

## When dental PPC is worth it

Dental PPC usually pays off when:

- You can take on more patients or more of a specific treatment
- Someone answers calls promptly during the hours ads run
- You know roughly what a new patient is worth over time
- Your website has pages for the treatments you advertise

It is less likely to pay off if the diary is already full, calls go unanswered, or the website has no clear way to book.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is dental PPC?

Dental PPC is pay-per-click advertising for dental practices, usually through Google Ads. Your ads appear when people search for dental services, and you pay only when someone clicks or, with Local Services Ads, when a qualifying lead comes in.

### Is PPC worth it for dentists?

It can be, when the practice tracks booked appointments, answers calls quickly and knows what a new patient is worth. Judge it on cost per new patient and accepted treatment, not on clicks.

### Which is better for dentists, Google Ads or Local Services Ads?

Where Local Services Ads are available, most practices run both and measure them separately. Local Services Ads suit general and emergency enquiries, while Search gives more control over treatments, keywords and wording.

### How quickly does dental PPC bring new patients?

Ads can show within a day, and first enquiries often come in the first weeks. A fair judgement of cost per new patient usually needs several weeks of data, and longer for high-value treatment.

### Do I need someone to manage dental PPC?

Not always, but dental accounts need regular work: search term reviews, negative keywords, tracking checks and budget changes. Many practices hire a specialist so the account improves rather than drifts.
