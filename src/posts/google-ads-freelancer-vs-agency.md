Hire a Google Ads freelancer if you want direct access to the person managing your account, your advertising is mainly Google Ads, and you would rather pay for senior hands-on time than for an agency's overheads. Hire an agency if you need several channels or services from one supplier, such as design, SEO and social media alongside Google Ads, or if your account needs a team working on it every day.

Both can do excellent work and both can do poor work. The title on the door matters less than who actually works in your account and how they are judged.

## What a Google Ads freelancer is

A Google Ads freelancer is an independent specialist who manages campaigns personally rather than through a team. You may also see the same role described as a Google Ads consultant, a freelance PPC specialist or a Google Ads expert. The names overlap: a consultant usually leans towards strategy and advice, a specialist towards hands-on management, and most freelancers do both.

## What changes when you hire a freelancer

**You talk to the person doing the work.** In many agencies, an account manager handles the client relationship while a specialist you rarely speak to changes the campaigns. With a freelancer, the person answering your questions is the person who made the change.

**Your fee pays for senior time.** An agency fee also covers sales teams, account management and offices. A freelancer's fee goes mostly into the work itself.

**Fewer hand-offs.** Accounts at larger agencies can move between staff as people join and leave. A freelancer's knowledge of your account stays in one place.

**More focus, less breadth.** A good freelancer is usually deep in one platform. If you need landing pages designed, SEO done or social campaigns run as well, you will need other suppliers.

## When an agency is the better choice

An agency usually makes more sense when:

1. **You want one supplier for several channels.** Google Ads, social advertising, SEO and design under one contract
2. **The account needs daily work from several people.** Very large accounts with constant creative production, many markets or many languages
3. **You need cover at all times.** Agencies can absorb holidays and illness inside the team
4. **Your organisation requires it.** Some procurement processes only approve companies of a certain size

There is no shame in any of these. Choosing an agency for the right reasons is a good decision.

## When a freelancer is the better choice

A freelancer usually makes more sense when:

1. **Google Ads is your main paid channel** and you want it run properly rather than as one service among many
2. **You want to understand what is happening** and be able to ask the person doing the work
3. **Your budget is small or medium,** where agency overheads take a large share of what you pay
4. **You have been passed around before** and want one accountable person

## What each typically costs

Pricing varies widely, so compare like with like: what is included, who does the work, and whether the fee changes as your ad spend grows.

Common models are a fixed monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend, an hourly rate, or a one-off project fee for audits and builds. Agencies more often use minimum retainers and longer contracts. Freelancers more often work month to month.

## Questions to ask before you hire either

Whoever you are considering, ask:

1. **Who will actually work in my account,** and can I speak to them directly?
2. **Will the Google Ads account be registered to my business,** under my billing? It should be. If you leave, you keep the history and the data
3. **What will you check first?** The right answer starts with conversion tracking, because every other number depends on it
4. **How will success be measured?** Cost per lead or sale and its quality, not clicks or impressions
5. **What happens if I want to leave?** Notice periods, contract length and handover
6. **Can I see their partner status?** Google's Partners directory shows whether a business holds Partner or Premier Partner status

[How to tell if your Google Ads agency is doing anything](/learn/is-your-google-ads-agency-doing-anything/) lists more checks, and they apply just as much to a freelancer.

## Red flags with either

- Guaranteed results before anyone has seen your account
- The provider wants to own or hold your Google Ads account
- Reports full of clicks and impressions, with no link to enquiries or sales
- Nobody can explain what changed last month and why

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Google Ads freelancer cheaper than an agency?

Often, for the same amount of hands-on senior time, because the fee carries fewer overheads. But compare what is included rather than the headline price, and remember that the cheapest option is expensive if the account is run badly.

### Can a freelancer manage large Google Ads budgets?

Yes, provided the account does not need several people working on it every day. Budget size matters less than the number of campaigns, markets and creative assets that need constant attention.

### What is the difference between a Google Ads freelancer and a Google Ads consultant?

The terms overlap. A consultant tends to advise on strategy, sometimes for an in-house team, while a freelancer or specialist usually manages the account hands-on. Many freelancers do both.

### Does Google Premier Partner status apply to freelancers?

Partner status belongs to the business in Google's Partners programme, whether that business is a large agency or an independent practice. It reflects how that business performs in Google's programme, not the size of its team.
