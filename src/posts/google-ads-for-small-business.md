Google Ads for small business works best when you start narrow: one Search campaign, for one or two services that make money, in the area you actually serve, with conversion tracking set up before you spend.

Small budgets cannot afford Google's broadest automation on day one, so the aim is to buy the most relevant searches first, learn what turns into customers, then grow.

This guide gives a practical starting plan for Google Ads for small business, and the mistakes that waste most small budgets.

## Step 1: Choose what to advertise

Pick the service or product that is profitable, that people search for, and that you can deliver more of. Avoid advertising everything at once. A plumber might start with emergency repairs; a local shop might start with its best-selling category.

## Step 2: Set up conversion tracking first

Before any ad runs, decide what counts as a result: a call, a form, a booking or a sale. Then make sure Google Ads records it. Without tracking, you cannot tell good spend from wasted spend, and Smart Bidding learns from the wrong data. [Why are my Google Ads not converting?](/learn/why-are-my-google-ads-not-converting/) covers the common tracking problems.

## Step 3: Start with one Search campaign

For most Google Ads for small business setups, Search is the right first campaign, because it reaches people who are already looking for what you sell.

- **Keywords:** a short list of specific phrases with clear buying intent, such as "emergency plumber near me" or "wedding cake order", using phrase and exact match. [Google Ads match types explained](/learn/google-ads-keyword-match-types-explained/) explains the options
- **Negative keywords:** block jobs, DIY, free and other searches that never become customers. [How to find negative keywords](/learn/how-to-find-negative-keywords/) shows where they come from
- **Location:** target the area you serve, with the setting for people who are in your area
- **Schedule:** run ads when you can answer calls or reply to enquiries

## Step 4: Set a budget you can learn from

Work backwards from what a customer is worth and what you can afford to pay to win one. The budget needs to buy enough clicks to produce a handful of conversions each month, or you will not learn anything. [How much should you spend on Google Ads?](/learn/how-much-should-you-spend-on-google-ads/) walks through the calculation.

## Step 5: Write ads and pages that match

- Put the service and your area in the headline
- Give a reason to choose you: availability, experience, price, guarantee, where true
- Send people to a page about that service, not your homepage, with a phone number and a clear next step

## Step 6: Review every week

For the first months, spend half an hour a week on:

1. The search terms report: add negatives for irrelevant searches
2. Leads or sales against cost
3. Calls and forms that did not turn into customers, and why

## Google Ads for small business: mistakes that waste budgets

- **Running ads before tracking works**
- **Broad match and automation from day one,** which spread a small budget too thin
- **Targeting too wide an area,** or people merely "interested" in it
- **Advertising every service at once**
- **Judging results on clicks** instead of customers
- **Leaving the account alone** for months

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Ads for small business worth it?

It can be, because it reaches people who are already searching for what you sell. It works best when tracking is set up first, campaigns are narrow, and results are judged on customers rather than clicks.

### How much should a small business spend on Google Ads?

Enough to produce a handful of conversions a month, worked out from what a customer is worth and what you can pay to win one. There is no single right number for every business.

### Which Google Ads campaign should a small business start with?

Usually a Search campaign for one or two profitable services, because it reaches people already looking for them. Add other campaign types once Search is profitable and tracking is reliable.

### Can I run Google Ads for my small business myself?

Yes, if the account stays simple and you give it regular weekly time. Many owners get an account checked first, then run it themselves with a clear plan.

### What is the biggest mistake small businesses make with Google Ads?

Spending before conversion tracking works. Without it, there is no way to know which ads bring customers, and automated bidding learns from the wrong data.
