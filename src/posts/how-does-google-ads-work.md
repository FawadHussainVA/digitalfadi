Google Ads works as an auction that runs every time someone searches. Advertisers choose keywords and set bids, and Google decides which ads to show, and in what order, using Ad Rank: a mix of your bid, ad quality and landing page experience. You usually pay only when someone clicks.

This guide explains how Google Ads works step by step, in plain English.

## Step 1: You choose what to show up for

You tell Google which searches you want to appear for, using keywords such as "emergency plumber" or "running shoes", along with match types that control how closely a search must match. You also set locations, languages, a budget and a bid strategy. [Google Ads match types explained](/learn/google-ads-keyword-match-types-explained/) covers how matching works.

## Step 2: Someone searches

When a person searches, Google finds every advertiser whose keywords and settings fit that search. An auction runs in a fraction of a second.

## Step 3: Google calculates Ad Rank

Ad Rank decides whether your ad shows and in which position. It is based on:

- **Your bid:** the most you are willing to pay for a click
- **Ad quality:** how relevant your ad and landing page are, and how likely people are to click
- **Ad Rank thresholds:** a minimum quality bar an ad must reach
- **Context:** the search, location, device and time
- **Expected impact of assets:** extras such as sitelinks, calls and images

A higher bid does not guarantee the top spot. A more relevant ad can outrank a higher bid. [What is Quality Score in Google Ads?](/learn/what-is-quality-score-in-google-ads/) explains how quality is judged.

## Step 4: You pay when someone clicks

With most Search campaigns you pay per click, and you usually pay less than your maximum bid: roughly the amount needed to keep your position. Display and video campaigns can charge per view or per thousand impressions instead. [Why your cost per click went up](/learn/why-your-cost-per-click-went-up/) explains what pushes prices up.

## Where Google Ads can appear

- **Search:** text ads on Google search results, and increasingly in AI-powered results
- **Shopping:** product ads with images and prices
- **YouTube:** video ads. See [YouTube ads cost](/learn/youtube-ads-cost/)
- **Display:** image ads on websites and apps
- **Discover and Gmail:** through Demand Gen campaigns
- **Maps:** local ads for nearby customers

Performance Max can run across all of these in one campaign.

## How Google Ads works with automation

Most accounts now use Smart Bidding, where Google sets bids for each auction to reach a goal, such as a target cost per lead or return on ad spend. This only works well when conversion tracking records real leads or sales, because the system learns from that data. [Which Google Ads bid strategy should you use?](/learn/which-google-ads-bid-strategy-should-you-use/) compares the options.

## How does Google Ads work for your business?

Understanding how Google Ads works is the start. Results depend on targeting the right searches, blocking the wrong ones, sending people to a relevant page, and measuring real customers rather than clicks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does Google Ads work in simple terms?

Advertisers bid on searches. Each time someone searches, Google runs an auction and ranks ads by Ad Rank, a mix of bid and quality. You usually pay only when someone clicks your ad.

### Does the highest bid always win in Google Ads?

No. Google ranks ads using Ad Rank, which combines bid with ad quality, landing page experience and other factors. A more relevant ad can rank above a higher bid.

### How much do you pay for a Google Ads click?

It depends on competition and quality. You usually pay less than your maximum bid, roughly what is needed to keep your position in that auction.

### Do you pay for Google Ads if nobody clicks?

For most Search campaigns, no, because you pay per click. Some video and display campaigns charge per view or per thousand impressions instead.

### How long does it take for Google Ads to work?

Ads can show within hours of approval, but campaigns usually need weeks of data and adjustments to become efficient. [How long does Google Ads take to work?](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/) explains the timeline.
