Google Ads works for fertility clinics when it respects how personal and long the decision is. People research for months, compare clinics carefully and often speak to several before booking.

Reach them with clear, honest information, offer a consultation or information evening as the first step, present success rates only in the way your regulator allows, and measure consultations and treatment started rather than form fills. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## How people search for fertility care

- **Clinic searches:** "fertility clinic near me", "IVF clinic in [city]". The clearest intent and the core of the budget
- **Treatment searches:** IVF, IUI, egg freezing, donor treatment, fertility testing. Each deserves its own ad group and page
- **Cost searches:** "IVF cost", "egg freezing price". Very common, often early in the decision
- **Information searches:** causes, tests, what to expect. Large volume and mixed intent

Fertility patients often travel to the clinic they trust most, so a wider location can work. Many also consider clinics in other cities or countries, which affects competition.

## Lead with information, not pressure

Fertility is emotional, and urgency-driven advertising can feel wrong to the people you want to reach. What tends to work:

- **A free or low-cost first step:** an information evening, an initial consultation or a fertility test
- **Clear explanations** of treatments, timelines and what each stage involves
- **Transparent pricing,** including what is and is not included, and finance options if offered
- **The team:** who patients will see, with qualifications stated accurately

## Present success rates carefully

Success rates are what many patients look for, and what regulators watch most closely:

- **Follow your regulator's rules.** Some have specific requirements for how fertility success rates are presented, compared and sourced
- **Avoid headline rates in ads** that cannot show the context, such as age group and treatment type
- **Never imply a guaranteed outcome,** directly or through wording such as "make your dream come true"

When in doubt, link to your published outcomes on the landing page rather than putting numbers in the ad.

## Health advertising rules

Fertility treatment falls under Google's healthcare policies. Prescription medicine names used in treatment are restricted in most countries, so advertise the treatment and the consultation, not the drugs. Google also restricts personalised advertising based on health, including reproductive health, which limits remarketing and audience targeting. [What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) covers these rules in more detail.

## Measure the whole journey

The path from first search to treatment can take months, so measure each stage:

1. **Enquiries and information event registrations**
2. **Consultations booked and attended**
3. **Treatment started,** with its value

Record the source of each patient in your patient management system, and send outcomes back to Google Ads where you can. [Enhanced conversions for leads](/learn/google-ads-enhanced-conversions-for-leads-guide/) explains how. Judge campaigns over a long enough window to see treatment start, not just the first month.

## Protect privacy

Fertility enquiries are among the most sensitive health information a clinic receives. Collect only what you need in forms, never ask for medical details on an ad landing page, and agree the tracking approach on booking pages with whoever handles compliance. In some countries, health privacy law, such as HIPAA in the US, affects how tracking tools may be used.

## Where fertility budgets leak

- Fertility supplements, ovulation tests and product searches
- Natural and home remedy searches
- Fertility nursing, embryology and medical job or training searches
- Free public treatment searches, where you do not provide it
- Treatments you do not offer, such as surrogacy services, if not part of your clinic

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for fertility clinics?

Yes, when campaigns respect a long and personal decision. Clear information, a low-pressure first step and careful tracking from enquiry to treatment started make fertility campaigns work.

### Can fertility clinics show success rates in Google Ads?

Be careful. Many regulators have specific rules on presenting fertility success rates. Avoid headline rates in ads without context, and link to properly presented outcomes on your website instead.

### Can fertility clinics use remarketing?

Only in limited ways. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health, including reproductive health, so many remarketing and audience options are not allowed.

### How long does it take for fertility ads to show results?

Often months, because patients research and compare before booking a consultation, and treatment starts later still. Measure consultations and treatment started over a long period.

### What should a fertility clinic track in Google Ads?

Information event registrations, consultations booked and attended, and treatment started with its value. Cost per patient who starts treatment is the measure that matters.
