Google Ads sitelinks are extra links that can appear under your Search ad, sending people straight to specific pages on your site, such as pricing, a service or contact. They make your ad larger and give searchers more ways to act. Add at least four, each pointing to a genuinely useful page that matches what people search for.

This guide explains how Google Ads sitelinks work, which pages to link, and how to set them up properly.

## How Google Ads sitelinks work

Sitelinks are one type of asset, the extras Google can add to a Search ad. Google decides when to show them, how many, and in what format, based on the search, device and expected performance. You cannot force them to show, but good sitelinks improve the chance and can lift click-through rate.

## Character limits

- **Link text:** up to 25 characters
- **Description lines:** two optional lines of up to 35 characters each

Descriptions are optional but recommended, because they give Google more format options.

## Which pages to link

Choose pages a searcher would want next:

- **Services or product categories:** for example, "Emergency repairs", "Boiler servicing"
- **Pricing:** "See our prices"
- **Proof:** "Customer reviews", "Case studies"
- **Action:** "Book a call", "Get a quote"
- **Locations:** for businesses with several branches

Avoid linking to the homepage, your blog index or pages with no clear next step.

## Account, campaign or ad group level

You can add Google Ads sitelinks at three levels:

- **Account level:** general links that suit every campaign, such as reviews or contact
- **Campaign level:** links that fit that campaign's services
- **Ad group level:** the most specific links for a single service

Sitelinks at a lower level replace those at a higher level for that campaign or ad group. Use ad group sitelinks where searches differ a lot, such as separate services.

## Common mistakes

- **Only two sitelinks.** Add at least four so Google has enough to choose from
- **Duplicate destinations.** Each sitelink should lead to a different page
- **Generic text** such as "Click here" or "Learn more"
- **Linking to the same page as the ad,** which wastes the space
- **Outdated offers** left running after a promotion ends

## Measuring sitelinks

In the assets report, compare impressions, clicks and conversions for each sitelink. Replace those that rarely get clicks. Remember that sitelinks also help by making the whole ad more prominent, so judge the ad's overall results too.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are Google Ads sitelinks?

Sitelinks are extra links under a Search ad that send people directly to specific pages, such as pricing, a service or contact, making the ad larger and more useful.

### How many sitelinks should I add?

At least four, each pointing to a different, relevant page. Google decides how many to show for each search.

### What are the character limits for Google Ads sitelinks?

Link text can be up to 25 characters, with two optional description lines of up to 35 characters each.

### Why are my sitelinks not showing?

Google shows sitelinks only when it expects them to help and when the ad ranks high enough. Adding at least four relevant sitelinks with descriptions, and improving ad quality, increases the chance.

### Should sitelinks be at account or campaign level?

Use account level for general links, and campaign or ad group level for links specific to a service. Lower-level sitelinks replace higher-level ones where both exist.
