Keyword choice decides who sees an HVAC company's ads. Bid on terms that match a job someone will pay for, and the calls come in. Bid on broad or informational terms, and the budget goes on people who are researching, comparing, or planning a DIY project.

## Start with the jobs you actually book

Make a list of the work that brings in the most profit, not the most calls. For most HVAC companies that is some mix of emergency repair, system replacement, and recurring maintenance. Each of these has a different search pattern.

Emergency repair searches are short and urgent: "ac repair near me", "furnace not heating", "no cooling today". These usually convert well because the customer needs help now. Replacement searches are longer and more considered: "new furnace cost", "replace central air unit", "heat pump installation". Maintenance searches tend to be lower value per click but bring repeat business: "hvac tune up", "annual furnace inspection".

## Use match types with care

Broad match will show your ads for many related searches, which can be useful for discovering new terms but expensive if unchecked. Phrase match and exact match give more control. A sensible starting point is phrase match for core service terms, with broad match used sparingly and watched closely in the search terms report.

Location matters as well. A keyword like "hvac company" without a service area can attract searches from far outside your coverage. Pair service keywords with the towns or neighbourhoods you actually serve, and set your location targeting to match.

## Terms that usually waste money

Some searches look relevant and rarely lead to a booked job:

- **DIY terms**, such as "how to fix a furnace yourself" or "replace air filter", are research, not buying intent.
- **Job seekers**, such as "hvac technician jobs", "hvac apprenticeship", and "hvac salary", are a common leak in service accounts.
- **Parts and product searches**, such as "hvac capacitor price" or "furnace filter brand", often belong to a retailer.
- **Training and certification searches** are not customers at all.

Add these to your negative keyword list at campaign or account level, and review the search terms report at least every two weeks. The list grows over time, and it is one of the cheapest improvements an HVAC account can make.

## Brand and competitor terms

Your own company name should be in its own campaign, with a low bid, because you want to appear for people already looking for you. Competitor names are a more delicate choice. Check the current advertising policies before using a competitor's trademark in your ad text, and be ready to justify the spend if your conversion data does not support it.
