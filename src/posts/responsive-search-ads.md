Responsive search ads are the standard text ad in Google Search campaigns. You write up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, and Google combines them into different versions for each search. Good responsive search ads give Google varied, specific assets to choose from, while you keep control of anything that must always appear.

This guide explains how responsive search ads work, the limits, ad strength, pinning, and a checklist for setting them up.

## The limits

- **Headlines:** up to 15, each up to 30 characters
- **Descriptions:** up to 4, each up to 90 characters
- **Display path:** two fields of up to 15 characters each, shown after your domain
- **Shown at once:** Google shows up to 3 headlines and 2 descriptions, and on smaller screens often fewer

Characters include spaces and punctuation. The free [ad copy builder](/tools/ad-copy-builder/) counts characters and previews your ad as you type.

## How Google combines your assets

For each search, Google picks a combination of headlines and descriptions it expects to perform best for that person, device and query. Over time it learns which assets work. That is why many genuinely different assets help, and ten versions of the same sentence do not.

## Ad strength

Ad strength rates how many, how varied and how relevant your assets are, from Poor to Excellent. It is a guide to the ad's setup, not a performance score. An ad with Good strength and strong conversion results is better than an Excellent ad that converts poorly. Use it as a prompt, not a target.

## Pinning: use sparingly

Pinning fixes a headline or description to a position. It guarantees that text shows, but removes combinations Google can test.

- Pin only what must always appear, such as a legal disclaimer or a required brand line
- If you pin, pin two or three alternatives to the same position so Google still has a choice
- Note that pinned assets are not guaranteed if AI Max's Final URL expansion is on. [AI Max for Search](/learn/ai-max-for-search/) explains why

## Writing assets that work

- **Use the search term** in a few headlines, because relevance matters
- **Lead with the outcome** for the customer, not your company name
- **Add proof:** a guarantee, a timeframe, a number you can support
- **Include a call to action** in descriptions
- **Vary the angles:** price, speed, trust, location, offer

[How to write Google Ads headlines](/learn/how-to-write-google-ads-headlines/) goes deeper into writing headlines that earn clicks.

## Reading the asset report

Open the ad's asset details to see impressions and performance for each headline and description. Replace assets that rarely show or underperform, one or two at a time, and keep the ones that work.

## Responsive search ads setup checklist

1. One to two responsive search ads per ad group, written for that ad group's theme
2. At least 8 to 10 distinct headlines and all 4 descriptions
3. Search terms included in several headlines
4. Display paths that match the search, not your site structure
5. Minimal pinning
6. Assets such as sitelinks and calls added. [Google Ads sitelinks](/learn/google-ads-sitelinks/) explains how
7. A landing page that matches the ad's promise

## Frequently asked questions

### What are responsive search ads?

Responsive search ads are Google's standard Search text ads. You provide up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, and Google combines them into versions suited to each search.

### How many headlines should a responsive search ad have?

Google allows up to 15. Aim for at least 8 to 10 genuinely different headlines so Google has real variety to test.

### Does ad strength affect performance?

Ad strength rates your asset setup, not results. It can help you add variety, but judge ads on clicks, conversions and cost per conversion.

### Should I pin headlines in responsive search ads?

Only when text must always appear, such as legal or brand requirements. Pinning reduces the combinations Google can test.

### How many responsive search ads can an ad group have?

Google allows a few enabled responsive search ads per ad group, but one or two well-written ads usually perform better than many similar ones.
