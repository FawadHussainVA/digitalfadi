Google Ads works for veterinary clinics when emergency, routine and specialist care are run as separate campaigns. Pet owners searching for an emergency vet need someone who can see them now; owners looking for vaccinations or a new practice compare clinics calmly.

Answer the phone during the hours ads run, track booked appointments rather than clicks, and value new clients by the care they need over years, not by one visit. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Three kinds of vet search

**Emergency and urgent care.** "Emergency vet near me", "dog ate chocolate", "cat not eating". Urgent, often in the evening or at weekends, and won by availability. These searches are only worth paying for when someone can answer and offer help.

**Routine and preventive care.** Vaccinations, neutering, dental care for pets, health checks. Steady demand, lower value per visit, and the start of a long relationship.

**New pet owners and new clients.** People who have just moved or just got a puppy or kitten, searching for "vet near me" or "best vet in [town]". They choose a practice they may use for ten years or more.

Specialist services, such as orthopaedics, exotic animals or equine work, deserve their own campaigns too, because they draw clients from a much wider area.

## Run emergency ads only when you can help

The schedule decides whether emergency ads make money:

- Match the ad schedule to the hours someone answers and can see an animal
- If you are not an out-of-hours practice, do not show ads implying immediate care at night
- Add call assets, because worried owners want to speak to someone
- Track missed calls. A missed emergency call is paid for and goes straight to the next clinic

If your area has a dedicated emergency hospital and you do not provide night cover, block night-time emergency searches instead of competing for them.

## Value clients over their lifetime

A new client is rarely worth one appointment. A puppy brought in for vaccinations may return for years of check-ups, treatment and dental care. When you judge campaigns, look at:

- **New clients registered** from ads each month
- **First appointment booked,** not just a call
- **The value of the first year,** where your practice system can show it

Judging routine campaigns on the value of a single vaccination makes them look unprofitable when they are often the best source of long-term clients.

## Rules that apply to vet ads

Veterinary ads face fewer of the personalised advertising limits that apply to human health, so remarketing is usually possible. A few rules still matter:

- **Animal prescription medicines.** In the US and Canada, targeting keywords for animal prescription drugs that can be misused by people needs Google certification. Avoid medicine names in keywords and ads unless you are sure they are allowed
- **Accurate claims.** Do not promise outcomes for treatments or surgery
- **Professional rules.** Your veterinary regulator may have its own advertising guidance, including for reviews and prices

## Write ads pet owners trust

- Name the service and the area: "Emergency vet in [town]" or "Puppy vaccinations near [area]"
- Show what reassures owners: opening hours, parking, the species you treat, and whether you see new clients
- Offer a clear next step: call, or book online if your system allows it

Landing pages should match the search. An emergency page needs the phone number and directions at the top; a new client page needs registration details, prices or price ranges where you publish them, and the team.

## Where vet budgets leak

- Pet food, toys and product searches
- Home remedy and symptom searches with no intent to visit
- Veterinary nurse and vet jobs, courses and training
- Free or charity animal care, if you do not offer it
- Searches from outside the area owners will travel, except for specialist services

## Measure appointments, not calls

Record how each new client found you in the practice management system, and compare it with Google Ads. Calls and form fills are early signals; booked appointments and registered clients show whether the campaign works. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for veterinary clinics?

Yes, especially for emergency care, new pet owners and specialist services. They work best when emergency and routine care are separate campaigns, calls are answered, and the clinic tracks booked appointments and new clients.

### Can vet clinics use remarketing?

Usually, because veterinary services are not subject to the same personalised advertising limits as human health. Check the current policy for your services and country, and make sure your privacy notice covers it.

### Should a vet clinic advertise emergency care at night?

Only if it can answer and see animals at night. Otherwise night-time emergency ads pay for calls that go nowhere, and the owner is left waiting for help.

### What should a vet clinic measure in Google Ads?

New clients registered, appointments booked, and where possible the value of each client's first year. Calls alone overstate results.

### Can vet ads mention medicine names?

Be careful. Animal prescription medicines that can be misused by people need certification for keyword targeting in the US and Canada, and prescription medicine names are restricted in many countries. Advertise the consultation or treatment rather than the drug.
