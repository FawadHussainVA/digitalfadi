You can run Google Ads yourself if your budget is modest, your account is simple, and you can give it a few focused hours every week. Hire help when the budget is large enough that mistakes are expensive, when results have stalled, or when the time it takes competes with running the business.

Many businesses start in the middle: an expert audits and sets up the account properly, then the owner runs it with occasional check-ins.

## What running Google Ads actually involves

Before deciding, be clear about the work:

- **Every week:** review search terms, add negative keywords, check budgets and spend, look at leads or sales against cost
- **Every month:** test ads and landing pages, adjust bids and structure, review locations, devices and schedules
- **Whenever anything changes:** check conversion tracking after website, form or booking system changes
- **Regularly:** keep up with Google's changes to features, automation and policies

This is ongoing work, not a one-off setup. An account left alone for months drifts.

## When doing it yourself makes sense

- The budget is small enough that the cost of mistakes is limited
- You sell one or two services in one area, so the account stays simple
- You enjoy the work and will make time for it every week
- You are willing to learn tracking properly, not just campaigns

The biggest risk is not knowing what you do not know. Many self-run accounts look fine in the interface while tracking counts the wrong things. [What a Google Ads audit should check](/learn/what-a-google-ads-audit-should-check/) is a good self-check.

## When hiring help makes sense

- Spend is large enough that wasted money adds up quickly
- You run several services, locations or product categories
- Results have stalled or fallen and you cannot find the cause
- You use Performance Max, Shopping or Smart Bidding and want them controlled properly
- The hours it takes are worth more spent on the business

## The in-house option

Training someone on your team keeps knowledge in the business. It works best when that person has real time for it, access to training, and an expert to check the account from time to time. It works badly when Google Ads becomes one task among many.

## The middle route

You do not have to choose between doing everything yourself and handing everything over:

1. **Get an audit or analysis** to find out what is wrong and in what order to fix it
2. **Fix the foundations once:** tracking, structure, negatives and locations
3. **Run the account yourself,** using the analysis as your plan
4. **Book a review** every few months, or when results change

This gives you a properly built account without a monthly management fee, and a clear point at which to hire help if the account grows.

## Compare the real cost

Doing it yourself is not free. Compare:

- **Your time:** hours per week, valued at what they are worth to the business
- **Wasted spend:** irrelevant clicks and poor targeting while you learn
- **Opportunity cost:** leads and sales an optimised account would have produced

Against a management fee, the cheapest option is the one that produces the most profit, not the one with the lowest invoice. [Google Ads management cost](/learn/google-ads-management-cost/) explains how fees are usually structured.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I run Google Ads myself?

Yes, if your account is simple, your budget is modest, and you can give it regular, focused time each week. Learn conversion tracking properly first, because it decides whether the rest of the work is based on real results.

### How many hours a week does Google Ads take?

It depends on the size of the account. A small, simple account needs regular weekly attention for search terms, budgets and results, plus more time for testing and tracking checks each month. Larger accounts need considerably more.

### Is it cheaper to manage Google Ads myself?

It saves the fee, but not always money. Wasted spend while learning and missed leads can cost more than management. Compare the total cost and the results, not just the invoice.

### What is the best first step if I want to run it myself?

Get the account checked, so you start from correct tracking and a sound structure. An audit or analysis gives you a prioritised list of fixes to work through.

### When should I switch from doing it myself to hiring someone?

When spend grows, results stall, the account becomes more complex, or the time it takes competes with the business. Many owners hire help just before increasing their budget.
