Google Ads works for podiatry clinics when campaigns follow the problems people search for: heel pain, ingrown toenails, verrucas, orthotics and diabetic foot care. Each brings a different patient with a different value, so separate campaigns let you put the budget where the clinic earns most.

Show availability, describe treatment accurately, and measure appointments booked and attended rather than clicks. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## How people search for a podiatrist

- **Direct searches:** "podiatrist near me", "chiropodist in [town]", "foot clinic"
- **Problem searches:** heel pain, plantar fasciitis, ingrown toenail, verruca, bunions, corns and hard skin
- **Treatment searches:** nail surgery, custom orthotics, gait analysis, shockwave therapy
- **Specialist care:** diabetic foot care, sports podiatry, children's foot problems

Problem searches bring volume, but some people want home remedies or products. Treatment searches show stronger intent to book.

## Organise campaigns by value

- **Routine care:** nail cutting, hard skin and corns. Steady demand and often regular, repeat visits
- **Painful problems:** ingrown toenails and heel pain. People want help soon, so availability matters
- **Procedures and products:** nail surgery and custom orthotics. Higher value per patient, worth their own campaigns
- **Specialist services:** diabetic and sports podiatry, which draw patients from further away

When routine and high-value work share one budget, cheap routine clicks often use the money that procedures need.

## Describe treatment honestly

Podiatry ads fall under Google's healthcare policies and your professional rules:

- Avoid promising a cure or pain-free results
- Describe what happens: assessment, diagnosis, treatment plan
- Use professional titles accurately, as protected titles differ by country
- Be careful with personalised advertising, as Google restricts targeting based on health conditions

[What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) explains the rules in more detail.

## Landing pages that convert

A good podiatry landing page answers the questions people have before booking:

- The problem or treatment in the headline, matching the search
- What the first appointment involves and how long it takes
- Prices for common treatments, or how insurance works where relevant
- Before-and-after images only where your rules allow them, with consent
- Online booking and a phone number at the top

Ingrown toenail and heel pain pages work best when they say how soon someone can be seen.

## Measure appointments and repeat visits

Track appointments booked and attended by treatment, and the value of procedures such as nail surgery and orthotics. Routine care patients who return regularly are worth more than one visit suggests, so record the source of each new patient and look at their value over time. ## Where podiatry budgets leak

- Foot creams, insoles and product searches
- Home remedies and DIY treatment searches
- Podiatry courses, jobs and training
- Shoe shops and footwear searches
- Free public services, where you do not provide them

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for podiatrists?

Yes, especially for painful problems such as ingrown toenails and heel pain, and for procedures such as nail surgery and custom orthotics. Separate campaigns by treatment and track booked appointments.

### Which podiatry services are best to advertise?

Usually the ones with the highest value or urgency: nail surgery, custom orthotics, heel pain treatment and specialist services. Routine care can work too, especially if patients return regularly.

### Should podiatry ads mention prices?

A starting price for common treatments on the landing page often helps, because people compare clinics. Make sure any price in an ad matches the landing page exactly.

### Can podiatry clinics use remarketing?

With care. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, so condition-based remarketing may not be allowed. Check the current policy.

### What should a podiatry clinic track in Google Ads?

Appointments booked and attended by treatment, the value of procedures and orthotics, and repeat visits. Cost per patient shows profitability better than cost per click.
