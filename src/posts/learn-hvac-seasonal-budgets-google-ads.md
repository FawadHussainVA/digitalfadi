HVAC demand moves with the weather. Cooling calls rise in the hottest months, heating calls rise when temperatures drop, and shoulder seasons are quieter. A flat monthly budget can run out at the wrong moment or sit underspent when demand is weak. Planning around the seasons keeps the account working when it matters most.

## Look at last year's calls, not just the calendar

Start with your own data. Pull the number of booked jobs by month for the last two years, along with the Google Ads spend for each month. This shows when your demand actually peaks in your area, which can differ from the general seasonal pattern because of climate, housing stock and local competition.

Once you know the peaks, plan the budget around them. Increase spend in the weeks before demand usually rises, so your ads are already in place when customers start searching.

## Adjust budgets by campaign

Different services peak at different times. Emergency cooling repair may spike in summer, heating repair in winter, and replacement work often sees a bump in spring and autumn when homeowners plan ahead. Splitting budgets by service type means you can increase spend on the campaigns that are working in each season, rather than raising the whole account evenly.

Shared budgets are useful when several campaigns compete for the same pool of money, but review them regularly. A single campaign with a higher bid can take the whole budget and starve the others.

## Use ad schedules with care

Ad scheduling lets you show ads at certain hours or days. For HVAC, emergency searches often happen outside office hours, especially at night when a system fails. Decide whether you can answer calls at those times. If you cannot, a schedule that stops ads when no one is available wastes less money, but it also gives up business. Make that trade-off on purpose, not by accident.

Bid adjustments by time of day can help too, but change them gradually and watch the results for at least two weeks before judging the change.

## Watch the budget during a surge

A heatwave or cold snap can increase searches sharply within a day or two. Check the account at least twice a week during peak weather, and look at whether campaigns are limited by budget. If they are, decide whether the extra demand is worth paying for, based on your cost per booked job, rather than letting the budget run out silently in the middle of the afternoon.

Set up alerts for sudden changes in spend or cost per click, so you learn about a problem before it has cost you a week of budget.

## Plan the quiet months too

Quiet months are a good time to tidy the account. Review the search terms report, update negative keywords, check call tracking and refresh ad copy. Also look at maintenance campaigns, because booked tune-ups in quiet months fill the calendar for the busy season ahead.
