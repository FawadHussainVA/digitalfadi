Google Ads works for orthodontists when children's and adult treatment are run as separate campaigns. Parents booking a child's assessment and adults considering aligners are different people with different questions, budgets and timelines.

Lead with a consultation, explain treatment options and costs honestly, use aligner brand names only where you are allowed to, and measure consultations that become treatment starts. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Two audiences, two campaigns

**Parents of children and teenagers.** Searches such as "orthodontist for kids", "braces for teenager" and "when does my child need braces". The parent makes the decision, often after a referral from the family dentist, and wants reassurance about timing, comfort and cost.

**Adults.** Searches such as "adult braces", "clear aligners", "invisible braces" and "straighten teeth". Adults often research for weeks, compare at-home aligner kits with in-clinic treatment, and care about appearance during treatment and the total cost.

Separate campaigns let each audience see ads and landing pages written for them.

## Organise by treatment

- **Braces:** metal, ceramic and lingual, each explained in plain language
- **Clear aligners:** treatment with an orthodontist, compared honestly with at-home kits
- **Retainers and retreatment:** for adults whose teeth moved after earlier treatment
- **Brand searches** for the practice name, kept separate

## Be careful with aligner brand names

Several clear aligner names are trademarks. Using them in ad text may be restricted by Google's trademark policy, depending on the brand and whether you are an authorised provider. Check before using a brand name, and use generic terms such as clear aligners or invisible braces where you are unsure. [Cosmetic dentistry Google Ads](/learn/cosmetic-dentistry-google-ads/) covers the same issue for cosmetic dental practices.

## Lead with a consultation

Orthodontic treatment depends on an assessment, so most practices do best by:

- Offering a consultation, free or paid, as the main call to action
- Giving a realistic starting price or range on the landing page, where you can stand behind it
- Explaining monthly payment plans if you offer them, including that approval may be required
- Answering the common questions: how long treatment takes, how often visits are, and what happens after

Health advertising rules apply, so avoid promises of perfect results or fixed timescales for everyone.

## Landing pages for each audience

**For parents:** the right age for a first check, what happens at the consultation, how treatment fits around school, and costs and payment options.

**For adults:** discreet treatment options, how aligners and braces compare, lifestyle questions such as eating and speaking during treatment, and total cost with payment options.

Both need online booking and a phone number at the top. [Dental landing pages that turn ad clicks into bookings](/learn/dental-landing-pages/) explains the principles in more detail.

## Measure consultations and treatment starts

Track consultations booked and attended, and treatment starts with their value, recorded from your practice system. Orthodontic treatment is high value, so a campaign with a high cost per consultation can still be very profitable if enough consultations become treatment starts. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) shows the setup.

## Where orthodontic budgets leak

- At-home aligner kits and product searches, unless you offer them
- DIY teeth straightening searches
- Orthodontic training, courses and jobs
- Free public treatment, where you do not provide it
- Searches from areas too far for regular appointments

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for orthodontists?

Yes, when children's and adult treatment are separate campaigns, ads lead with a consultation, and the practice tracks consultations that become treatment starts.

### Should orthodontists advertise to parents or adults?

Both, in separate campaigns. Parents and adults search differently and need different information, so separate ads and landing pages usually perform better than one campaign for everyone.

### Can orthodontists use aligner brand names in ads?

It depends on the brand, whether you are an authorised provider and Google's trademark policy. Check before using a brand name, and use generic terms where you are unsure.

### Should orthodontic ads show prices?

Usually the ad should offer a consultation, with a realistic starting price or range and payment options on the landing page. Show a price in the ad only if most patients pay close to it.

### What should an orthodontist track in Google Ads?

Consultations booked and attended, and treatment starts with their value. Cost per treatment start is the measure that shows whether campaigns are profitable.
