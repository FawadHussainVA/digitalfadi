A dental landing page converts when it matches the search, makes the next step obvious and gives a worried patient enough confidence to book. Send each campaign to a page about that treatment, not the homepage.

Put the phone number and booking option at the top, show who will treat the patient, answer the main questions on cost and process, and make sure the page loads fast on a phone. Then test one change at a time against booked appointments.

The ads bring the click. The landing page decides whether the click becomes a patient, so it often matters as much as the campaign itself.

## Match the page to the search

The single biggest improvement is usually message match: the page should continue the conversation the search started.

- **Emergency searches** go to a short page about urgent appointments, not a general services page
- **Implant searches** go to an implant page that explains the treatment and the consultation
- **Check-up and new patient searches** go to a new patient page with availability and what to expect
- **Cosmetic searches** go to a page for that specific treatment: aligners, veneers or whitening

If the ad says "same-day emergency appointments", the first thing on the page should say the same.

## What to put above the fold

The part of the page visible without scrolling should answer three questions:

1. **Am I in the right place?** A headline that repeats the treatment and the area
2. **Can you help me soon?** Availability, opening hours or next appointment
3. **How do I book?** A tap-to-call phone number and a clear booking button

On mobile, where most dental searches happen, keep this short. A large image that pushes the phone number off the screen costs bookings.

## Give patients more than one way to book

People choose differently, so offer the options your practice can actually handle:

- **Call,** with a number that works with call tracking
- **Online booking,** if your system allows it
- **A short enquiry form:** name, phone or email, and the treatment. Ask only for what you need, and never for medical details in an ad landing page form

Make sure every option is answered quickly. A booking request that waits until tomorrow often goes to another practice.

## Build trust honestly

Dental patients, especially for implants and cosmetic work, look for reassurance before booking:

- **Who will treat me:** names, photographs and qualifications that can be verified
- **What other patients say:** real reviews, where your regulator allows them in advertising
- **What the treatment involves:** stages, timeline and aftercare in plain language
- **What it costs:** a realistic range or starting point where you can stand behind it, plus finance options if you offer them
- **Real photographs** of your practice and team, rather than stock images

Avoid claims you cannot support, such as guaranteed results. Health advertising rules apply to landing pages as well as ads.

## Speed and mobile

- Keep pages light: compress images, avoid heavy sliders and auto-playing video
- Make buttons large enough to tap and keep text readable without zooming
- Check form fields do not trigger zooming on phones, and that the page works in portrait

Google also uses landing page experience in ad quality, so a faster, more relevant page can lower what you pay per click. [What is Quality Score?](/learn/what-is-quality-score-in-google-ads/) explains how.

## Track the page properly

A landing page can only be improved if you can see what it produces. Track calls, form submissions and online bookings as conversions, and test them after every website change. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) covers the setup.

## Test one change at a time

Good tests for dental landing pages:

- Headline wording, especially availability and the area
- Booking options: a booking button against a phone-first layout
- The amount of information on implant and cosmetic pages
- Adding or moving trust elements such as clinician details and reviews

Run each test long enough to judge it on booked appointments, not clicks. [Google Ads experiments](/learn/google-ads-experiments-ab-testing-guide/) explains how to test fairly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should dental Google Ads send traffic to the homepage?

Usually not. A homepage tries to serve everyone, so it rarely answers a specific search well. Send each campaign to a page about that treatment or need.

### What should a dental landing page include?

A headline matching the search, availability, a tap-to-call number, a booking option, who will treat the patient, what the treatment involves, a realistic idea of cost, and real reviews where allowed.

### How long should a dental landing page be?

Emergency pages should be short, with the phone number at the top. Implant and cosmetic pages can be longer, because patients research before booking, as long as the booking option stays easy to find.

### Do landing pages affect what I pay for Google Ads?

Yes. Landing page experience is part of how Google judges ad quality, so a relevant, fast page can improve your position and lower your cost per click.

### How do I know if my landing page is the problem?

If ads get clicks but few calls or bookings, check the page: does it match the ad, load fast on mobile, and make booking obvious? Also check that tracking still works, because a broken form can look like a weak page.
