Google Ads works for periodontists when it separates three kinds of search: people looking for a gum specialist nearby, people searching for a specific treatment such as gum grafts or dental implants, and people researching symptoms or costs before they choose a clinic. Each one has a different value and needs different ads and landing pages. Measure the campaigns on consultations booked and treatment accepted, because a periodontal case often starts as a referral or a long research process rather than a single click.

If you also run a general dental practice, the [Google Ads for dentists](/google-ads-for-dentists/) page covers the wider approach. This article is for practices where gum and implant specialist care is the main service.

## Who periodontists are trying to reach

Periodontists treat gum disease, gum recession, and the gums and bone around teeth. They also place many dental implants and carry out gum grafts and deep cleaning. Patients come to a specialist in a few ways:

- **Directly, through search,** when they know they have a gum problem or want an implant or graft
- **By referral,** from a general dentist who has found a gum problem that needs specialist treatment
- **After research,** when someone has read about a treatment and is comparing specialists

Google Ads mainly reaches the first and third groups. Referrals are built through relationships with local dentists, which Google Ads does not replace, though a visible, trustworthy website helps when a dentist checks a specialist online.

## Build campaigns around treatment and intent

Most periodontal accounts work best with separate campaigns:

**Local specialist searches.** Phrases such as "periodontist near me" or "gum specialist in [town]". These are the most direct and should have the most budget and the clearest call to action.

**Treatment searches.** Gum graft, gum recession treatment, dental implant placement, deep cleaning for gum disease. Each treatment deserves its own ad group and landing page, because the person searching wants to know about that treatment specifically.

**Research and cost searches.** Phrases such as "scaling and root planing cost" or "gum disease treatment options". These bring many people who are still deciding. They are worth reaching, but at lower bids and with an ad that offers a consultation rather than a price.

Keep brand searches separate as well. If a patient searches for your practice name, that campaign is judged on its own, because brand clicks are cheap and easy to win and can flatter the account.

## Health ad rules come first

Dental and periodontal ads fall under Google's healthcare policies. Before writing or launching anything:

- **Make no claims you cannot back up.** Ads should not promise a cure, guaranteed results or a particular outcome for every patient
- **Describe treatments accurately,** including who they suit and that a consultation is needed to decide
- **Check country rules.** Health advertising is regulated differently in different countries, and Google may apply extra checks to healthcare ads
- **Be careful with personalised advertising.** Google limits some audience targeting around health topics, so remarketing lists may be restricted or unavailable. Check what your account allows before building them
- **Keep privacy in mind on forms.** Patient information collected through ads and landing pages should be handled carefully, with the consent and privacy rules that apply where you work

These rules shape the copy. An ad that says "book your consultation to find out whether a gum graft suits you" is safer and usually performs better than one that promises a result.

## Write ads that match a specialist's audience

Patients looking for a periodontist are often worried, and they compare specialists carefully. Ads and landing pages should do three things clearly:

- **Name the treatment and the specialist** in the first line, so the searcher knows they are in the right place
- **Show credentials and experience** that are true and checkable, such as qualifications and memberships
- **Offer a clear next step,** usually a consultation, with a visible phone number and an online booking option

Avoid fear-based wording. Gum disease is common, and people respond better to calm information and a clear next step than to alarm.

## Watch for waste

Some searches look relevant but rarely lead to a patient. Add these as negatives where they do not fit:

- **Jobs and training:** periodontist jobs, periodontics residency, periodontal programmes
- **Home and free care:** gum disease home remedies, free dental treatment, charity clinics
- **Products:** toothpaste, mouthwash and floss sold for gum health, unless you sell them
- **Areas you do not serve**, especially for specialist care where patients may travel but not indefinitely

## Location is wider than for routine dentistry

Patients often travel further for a specialist than for a routine check-up, so a radius that suits a general practice may be too small for a periodontist. Test a wider area for the treatment campaigns, while keeping the local specialist searches tight. [Google Ads location targeting](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) explains the options.

## Measure consultations and accepted treatment

Calls and form fills are early signals. What matters is the number of consultations booked, how many turn into an accepted treatment plan, and the value of that treatment.

- **Track consultations booked** from ads, using the source on every enquiry
- **Track treatment accepted** from the practice system, and value each treatment type separately, because an implant case is worth far more than a consultation
- **Judge campaigns on cost per accepted treatment,** not cost per enquiry

If the practice is new to Google Ads, start with the local specialist and main treatment campaigns, keep the budget modest, and check the search terms weekly for the first month. ## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google Ads allowed for periodontists?

Yes, dental and periodontal services can be advertised. Ads must follow Google's healthcare policies, avoid unsupported claims, and meet the rules in your country. Check your ads against the policy before launch, because health ads are reviewed more closely.

### Which keywords work best for a periodontist?

Local specialist searches such as "periodontist near me" usually have the clearest intent. Treatment searches for gum grafts or dental implants follow, then research searches for costs and options. Start with the first two and test the third at lower bids.

### Can a periodontist get referrals from Google Ads?

Google Ads mainly reaches patients directly, not dentists. Referrals usually come from relationships with local general dentists. A clear, trustworthy website helps when a referring dentist checks your practice online.

### What should a periodontist track?

Consultations booked, treatment accepted and the value of each treatment type. Cost per accepted treatment is the most useful measure, because it reflects the work that actually turns into patients.

### How much should a periodontist spend on Google Ads?

It depends on your location, the treatments you want to grow and what an accepted treatment is worth to you. Work backwards from the value of a treatment and the share that convert, then set a budget that leaves a profit after costs.
