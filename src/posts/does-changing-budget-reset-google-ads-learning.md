No. Changing a campaign's budget or its target CPA or target ROAS does not reset the Google Ads learning period. In October 2026 Google rewrote its official help page on Smart Bidding learning to say that changes to budgets, targets, ads and targeting settings never trigger the Learning status and never remove what the bidding strategy has already learned. Learning status is now triggered by only three things, and it never lasts more than seven days.

That overturns one of the most repeated rules in Google Ads management, so it is worth knowing exactly what changed and what still deserves care.

## What triggers the Learning status now

According to Google's updated help page, [About Smart Bidding learning](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501), a bid strategy shows Learning for one of three reasons:

1. **New strategy.** The bid strategy was recently created or reactivated
2. **Composition change.** Campaigns, ad groups or keywords were added to or removed from the bid strategy
3. **Goal change.** The conversion goals or actions the strategy optimises for changed significantly

Everything else, including budget changes, target changes, new or edited ads, and changes to location, audience or language targeting, does not trigger Learning status.

## What Google said, and when

Two things happened in the same week. On 15 October 2026, a Google Ads product manager said on Google's Ads Decoded podcast that when you change the target or budget of an established, steady campaign, there is no learning period, because the bidding system scales bids in line with the change using data it already has. A few days later, Google published the rewritten help page, which states the seven-day limit and the three triggers in writing.

## Where the old rule came from

For years, agencies, training courses and blog posts warned that a budget or target change of more than about 15 to 20% would reset learning. That rule never appeared in Google's own documentation as a fixed number. It was a cautious habit built from watching performance wobble after big changes, and the wobble was real, but it was not the Learning status resetting.

Google's new page draws that line clearly: the Learning status is one thing, and the time it takes for performance to settle is another.

## Learning status versus performance settling

This is the part that still matters.

- **Learning status** is a label. It appears for the three triggers above and lasts at most seven days
- **Performance settling** is what actually happens to your results after any change. It can take longer than seven days, especially with few conversions or a long gap between click and sale

So a target change will not show Learning, but results immediately after it are still not a fair test. A campaign that needs ten days for clicks to turn into sales needs at least that long before you can judge what a new target did.

## What this changes in practice

1. **You can adjust budgets when the business needs it.** A budget increase for a busy season or a cut for a quiet one no longer needs to be drip-fed in small steps to protect learning
2. **Target changes are safer than many advertisers assumed.** Google says what the strategy has learned is kept
3. **One change at a time still matters.** Google's own advice is to avoid several target changes within a single conversion cycle. If you change the target on Monday and again on Wednesday, you will never know which change did what
4. **Structure changes deserve the caution.** Moving keywords, ad groups or campaigns into or out of a bid strategy does trigger learning, so batch those changes rather than spreading them over weeks
5. **Goal changes need the most care.** Adding or removing the conversion actions a campaign optimises for changes what it is trying to achieve, which is exactly when learning should happen

## What has not changed

The new guidance does not make bidding strategies work with poor data. Smart Bidding still learns from conversions, so it still needs:

- **Accurate conversion tracking.** If page views or duplicate tags are counted, the strategy optimises towards them. [Why are my Google Ads not converting?](/learn/why-are-my-google-ads-not-converting/) covers the usual faults
- **A steady flow of conversions.** A handful a month gives any strategy little to work with
- **A realistic target.** A target far below what the campaign has achieved still cuts volume and raises cost per click, learning period or not. [Which Google Ads bid strategy should you use?](/learn/which-google-ads-bid-strategy-should-you-use/) explains how to set one

A larger budget also still behaves like a larger budget. More money lets the campaign enter more auctions, including less efficient ones, so cost per conversion can rise as spend grows. That is economics, not learning. [How much should you spend on Google Ads?](/learn/how-much-should-you-spend-on-google-ads/) shows how to check whether more budget is worth it.

## Quick answers

- **Does a budget change reset learning?** No
- **Does a target CPA or target ROAS change reset learning?** No
- **Does pausing and restarting a campaign trigger learning?** It can. Reactivating a bid strategy is one of the listed triggers
- **How long does Learning status last?** Up to seven days, according to Google
- **Does adding keywords trigger learning?** Adding or removing keywords is part of a composition change, so it can
- **Should I still wait after a change before judging it?** Yes, for at least one full conversion cycle

If your campaigns seem to be stuck in Learning, or have been through so many changes that nobody can say what worked, a [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/) starts by checking whether the conversions the strategy learns from are real.
