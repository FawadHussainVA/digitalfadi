Quality Score is Google's rating, from 1 to 10, of how relevant and useful your ads and landing page are for a keyword compared with other advertisers. It is built from three parts: expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. A higher score usually means you pay less per click for the same position, which is why it matters more than its modest place in the interface suggests.

## What Quality Score is made of

Each keyword gets a rating for three components, shown as Above average, Average or Below average:

1. **Expected click-through rate.** How likely your ad is to be clicked when it shows for that keyword, compared with other advertisers
2. **Ad relevance.** How closely your ad matches the intent behind the search
3. **Landing page experience.** How relevant and useful the page is for someone who clicked, including how well it works on a phone

The 1 to 10 score summarises the three. The components are the useful part, because each one points at a different fix.

## How it affects what you pay

Where your ad appears is decided by Ad Rank, which combines your bid with the quality of the ad and landing page at the moment of each search, along with other factors such as the context of the search and the assets you use.

Strictly speaking, the 1 to 10 number itself is a diagnostic tool and is not what the auction uses. The auction uses real-time quality signals, and Quality Score is Google's summary of how those signals have looked for your keyword. In practice they move together, so when Quality Score is low, you are almost always paying more for each click than a competitor with a better ad and page. [Why your Google Ads cost per click went up](/learn/why-your-cost-per-click-went-up/) shows how that plays out.

## How to see your Quality Score

In Google Ads, open the Keywords view and add these columns: Quality Score, Exp. CTR, Ad relevance and Landing page exp. There are also historical versions of each column, which show how the score has changed over time.

Keywords without enough searches show a dash instead of a score. That is normal and not a problem in itself.

## How to improve each component

### Ad relevance: below average

The ad does not match the search closely enough. Usually the ad group holds too many different keywords, so one set of ads tries to answer them all.

- Split ad groups so each holds closely related keywords
- Put the main keyword, or a close version of it, into two or three headlines
- Remove keywords that do not belong with the ads they sit beside

[How to write Google Ads headlines that actually get shown](/learn/how-to-write-google-ads-headlines/) covers relevance headlines in detail.

### Expected click-through rate: below average

People see the ad and choose a competitor. The fix is a better reason to click, and fewer irrelevant searches dragging the rate down.

- Write headlines that state a specific outcome, offer or proof rather than generic claims
- Add assets such as sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets, which make the ad larger and more informative
- Add [negative keywords](/learn/how-to-find-negative-keywords/) for searches the ad was never going to win
- Check that match types are not reaching searches far from the keyword

### Landing page experience: below average

The page does not deliver what the ad promised, or is hard to use.

- Send each ad group to the page that matches it, not to the homepage
- Make sure the words of the search appear on the page, especially near the top
- Check the page loads quickly and works properly on a phone, since that is where much of the traffic arrives
- Make the next step obvious: a visible phone number, a short form, a clear button

## Where to focus first

Not every low score deserves attention. A keyword with a score of 3 that spends almost nothing matters less than a keyword with a score of 6 that takes a third of the budget.

Sort the keywords by cost, then work down the list fixing the components on the keywords that spend the most. That is where an improvement turns into real money.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Deleting low-score keywords that convert.** A keyword with a modest score that produces profitable enquiries is doing its job
2. **Chasing a 10.** A score of 7 or 8 on your main keywords is healthy. The effort to squeeze out the last points is usually better spent elsewhere
3. **Stuffing keywords into every headline.** Relevance improves, click-through rate falls, and the ads read badly
4. **Ignoring the landing page.** It is the component most often left alone because it sits outside Google Ads, and frequently the one holding the score down

## In short

Quality Score is a diagnosis, not a goal. Read the three components, fix the weakest one on the keywords that spend the most, and the lower cost per click follows. A [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/) includes this review alongside tracking and search terms, so the fixes go where the budget actually is.
