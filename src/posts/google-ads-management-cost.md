Google Ads management is usually priced in one of three ways: a flat monthly fee, a percentage of your ad spend, or a one-off project fee for setup or an audit. The fee is separate from your ad spend, which should always be paid directly to Google from your own account.

The right model depends on the size of your budget and how fast it is growing, but what is included matters more than the headline price.

## The three ways management is priced

### Flat monthly fee

You pay the same amount each month, whatever you spend on ads.

- **Good for:** small and medium accounts with a steady budget
- **Why:** the cost is predictable, and the manager has no reason to push your spend up
- **Watch for:** a low fee with very little work behind it. Ask what happens each week

### Percentage of ad spend

You pay a share of your monthly ad spend, often with a minimum fee.

- **Good for:** larger accounts and accounts where spend grows month on month
- **Why:** the fee grows with the size and complexity of the account
- **Watch for:** a manager who benefits from you spending more. Agree how budget increases are decided and measured

### Project fees

You pay once for a defined piece of work: an audit, an analysis, a rebuild or a tracking setup.

- **Good for:** checking an account before hiring someone, fixing a specific problem, or setting up an account you will run yourself
- **Watch for:** setup fees that are really the first month of management under another name

## What good management should include

Compare what you get, not just the price. Ongoing management should normally cover:

- **Conversion tracking** set up, checked and maintained, so the account measures real leads and sales
- **Search terms reviewed** and negative keywords added regularly
- **Bids, budgets and campaign structure** adjusted as results come in
- **Ads and landing pages tested,** one change at a time
- **Reporting you can understand,** with what changed and why, plus a regular review call

If a proposal does not mention tracking, ask about it first. Without reliable tracking, everything else is guesswork. [What a Google Ads audit should check](/learn/what-a-google-ads-audit-should-check/) shows how much depends on it.

## Hidden costs to check

- **Ad spend markups.** Some providers invoice your ad spend and add a margin. Paying Google directly from your own account avoids this
- **Account ownership.** If the provider owns the account, leaving can mean losing your history and data
- **Long contracts** with no way out if results are poor
- **Separate charges** for landing pages, tools, tracking or reports that you assumed were included
- **Setup fees** on top of the first month, without a clear list of what they cover

## Which model suits your account

| Your situation | Usually suits |
|---|---|
| New to Google Ads, or unsure the account is set up properly | A one-off audit or analysis first |
| Small or medium budget, steady month to month | A flat monthly fee |
| Larger budget, or spend growing quickly | A percentage of ad spend |
| You want to run it yourself, with help | Setup or audit project, then do it in-house |

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Google Ads management cost?

It depends on the pricing model and what is included. Common models are a flat monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend or a one-off project fee. Compare what each provider does each month, not just the price.

### Is a flat fee or a percentage of ad spend better?

A flat fee usually suits small and medium accounts with steady budgets, because the cost is predictable. A percentage of spend suits larger or fast-growing accounts. Either can be fair if the work is clear and results are measured on leads or sales.

### Should my ad spend be paid to the manager or to Google?

Directly to Google, from an account registered to your business. That avoids hidden markups and keeps your data if you change provider.

### What should be included in Google Ads management?

Conversion tracking, regular search term and negative keyword reviews, bid and budget changes, ad and landing page testing, and clear reporting with a review call. Ask for this list in writing.

### Is it worth paying for an audit before hiring a manager?

Often, yes. An audit shows what is wrong and how much work the account needs, so you can compare proposals properly and avoid paying monthly for problems that a one-off fix would solve.
