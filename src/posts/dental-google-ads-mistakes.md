The dental Google Ads mistakes that waste the most money are counting calls instead of booked patients, putting every treatment in one campaign, running ads when nobody can answer the phone, sending clicks to the homepage, skipping negative keywords, and letting someone else own the account.

Each one is fixable, and most can be checked in an afternoon. If your ads get clicks but the diary does not fill, start with tracking: it is the mistake that hides all the others.

## 1. Counting calls and clicks as patients

Many accounts count every call over a few seconds as a conversion. Suppliers, existing patients, wrong numbers and price shoppers all count, and automated bidding learns to buy more of them.

**Fix:** count booked appointments and qualified calls, and send accepted treatment back to Google Ads where you can. See [how to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/).

## 2. One campaign for every treatment

When emergencies, check-ups, implants and cosmetic work share one campaign and budget, the money goes to whatever is cheapest to click, usually low-value searches.

**Fix:** separate campaigns for emergency, general and high-value treatment, each with its own budget and ads. [Dental implant Google Ads](/learn/google-ads-for-dental-implants/) shows why implants in particular need their own.

## 3. Ads running when nobody can answer

Evening and weekend emergency searches are valuable, but only if someone answers. Every missed call from an ad is paid for and usually lost to another practice.

**Fix:** match the ad schedule to the hours calls are answered, and track missed calls. See [emergency dentist Google Ads](/learn/emergency-dentist-google-ads/).

## 4. Sending every click to the homepage

A homepage tries to serve everyone, so someone searching for implants or an emergency appointment has to search again on your site.

**Fix:** a landing page for each treatment, with the booking option at the top. See [dental landing pages that turn ad clicks into bookings](/learn/dental-landing-pages/).

## 5. No negative keywords

Dental searches attract job seekers, students, DIY and home remedy searches, product searches and people looking for free care.

**Fix:** a starter negative list, then regular reviews of the search terms report. See [negative keywords for dental practices](/learn/dental-negative-keywords/).

## 6. The wrong location setting

Google's default location option can show ads to people outside your area who are merely interested in it. For a local practice, that often means paying for clicks from people who will never visit.

**Fix:** use the setting that targets people in your area, and check the user locations report. See [the location setting that wastes budget](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/).

## 7. Ignoring health advertising rules

Unsupported claims, brand names you are not allowed to use, or remarketing that breaks Google's personalised advertising rules can get ads disapproved or limited.

**Fix:** factual ad copy, generic treatment names where you are unsure of trademark rights, and checking which audiences your account allows before building remarketing lists.

## 8. Judging too early, or changing too much

Turning campaigns off after a few days, or changing bids, budgets and ads every week, means nothing ever has enough data to show what works.

**Fix:** give changes several weeks before judging, and change one thing at a time. [How long does it take for Google Ads to work?](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/) explains realistic timelines.

## 9. Brand and non-brand mixed together

Searches for your practice name are cheap and convert well. If they are mixed with general searches, they make the whole account look better than it is.

**Fix:** a separate brand campaign, and report brand and non-brand results separately. See [should you bid on your own brand name?](/learn/should-you-bid-on-your-own-brand-name/)

## 10. Not owning your account

If a marketing company owns the Google Ads account, you may lose the history and data when you leave, and you cannot check what is happening.

**Fix:** the account should be registered to the practice and paid from your own payment method. [Dental PPC management: how to choose who runs your ads](/learn/dental-ppc-management/) covers what to ask.

## A quick self-check

1. Do Google Ads conversions roughly match the appointments your team booked from ads last month?
2. Are emergency, general and implant searches in separate campaigns?
3. Do ads stop when nobody can answer the phone?
4. Does each campaign send people to a page about that treatment?
5. Have you reviewed the search terms report in the last two weeks?
6. Is the account in your practice's name?

Any "no" is a good place to start. The [Google Ads audit checklist](/learn/google-ads-audit-checklist/) goes through 51 checks in priority order.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my dental Google Ads not getting patients?

The most common causes are tracking that counts calls instead of bookings, campaigns that mix every treatment, ads that run when nobody answers, and landing pages that do not match the search. Check tracking first, because it hides the other problems.

### Why do my dental ads get clicks but no bookings?

Usually the landing page, the call handling or the targeting. Check that the page matches the ad and makes booking easy on mobile, that calls are answered, and that the search terms are relevant.

### How often should a dental Google Ads account be checked?

Every week or two for search terms, budgets and tracking, with a fuller review every quarter. Check straight away after any website or booking system change.

### Can a bad Google Ads account be fixed, or should I start again?

Most accounts can be fixed without starting again, which keeps their history and data. Start with tracking, then structure, negatives and landing pages.

### What is the first thing to check in a dental Google Ads account?

Conversion tracking. Compare Google Ads conversions with the appointments your team actually booked from ads. If they are far apart, fix that before changing anything else.
