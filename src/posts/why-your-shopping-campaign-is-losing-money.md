Shopping campaigns fail quietly. There is no advert to rewrite and no keyword list to blame, so when results are poor the instinct is to change bids. Bids are rarely the problem.

Shopping is decided by the product feed, the structure around it, and whether the account can tell a profitable product from an unprofitable one. Here is where the money usually goes.

## Your titles are written for a warehouse, not a search

Google matches searches to products largely through the title. Most feeds use whatever the manufacturer or the platform supplied, which is written for inventory systems rather than buyers.

"SKU-4471 Blk" will never match "black running shoes size 10". The product is eligible for nothing.

Titles should carry brand, product type and the attributes people actually type, in roughly the order they type them. This single change moves Shopping performance more than any bid adjustment, and there is a full article on it: [how to write product titles for Google Shopping](/learn/how-to-write-product-titles-for-google-shopping/).

## A chunk of the catalogue is not serving

Open Merchant Center and look at disapprovals and warnings rather than the campaign.

Missing GTINs, mismatched prices between the feed and the landing page, availability saying in stock when the site says otherwise, image policy problems. Each one silently removes products from the auction.

Most merchants discover a third of the catalogue has not served for months. The campaign was never underperforming, it was running on a fraction of the inventory.

## Everything is bid the same

A feed with no segmentation treats a product making 70 percent margin the same as one making 8 percent.

The campaign then optimises towards whatever converts most easily, which is usually the cheap, low-margin item. Return on ad spend looks acceptable while profit falls.

Custom labels fix this. Label products by margin band, by bestseller status, by season. Then the structure can bid differently on each, and the account can be managed to profit rather than revenue.

## Return on ad spend is hiding the real number

A 4x return on a product with a 20 percent margin loses money. A 2x return on a product with a 70 percent margin is excellent.

Return on ad spend is a revenue measure. Used as the only target, it pushes budget towards high-price, low-margin products because they produce the biggest revenue number per click.

If you can supply margin by product or category, bidding can target profit. If you cannot, at least set the target from your own blended margin rather than from an industry benchmark that knows nothing about your costs.

## Performance Max is eating the campaign

Run alongside Shopping without structure, Performance Max absorbs the same traffic and reports it as its own.

Shopping then looks weaker and gets cut, which is exactly backwards. The traffic did not improve, it changed labels.

Brand exclusions on Performance Max and separate reporting are the minimum. More in [Performance Max: what it hides and how to see it](/learn/performance-max-what-it-hides/).

## Revenue tracking is inflated

Shopping bidding is driven by the value you pass back. When that value is wrong, the algorithm efficiently buys the wrong products.

The usual faults: purchase events firing twice, order value including shipping and tax in one place but not another, refunds never subtracted, GA4 and Google Ads disagreeing on the same day's total.

Fix this before touching anything else. Every bid decision rests on it.

## Out of stock products are still spending

Availability in the feed updates on a schedule. If that schedule is slow, you pay for clicks on products nobody can buy, and the landing page tells them so.

Check how often the feed refreshes against how fast stock moves. For fast-moving catalogues, scheduled fetches are not enough and the feed needs updating more directly.

## The order to fix it

1. Clear Merchant Center disapprovals so the catalogue actually serves
2. Verify purchase tracking and the values being passed
3. Rewrite titles around how people search
4. Segment by margin with custom labels
5. Separate Performance Max from Shopping and exclude brand
6. Only then adjust targets

Steps one to three usually account for most of the gap. Almost nobody starts there.
