Clinics can advertise most consultations, treatments and services on Google, but several things are restricted. Prescription medicine names are not allowed in ads, keywords or landing pages in most countries without certification. Online prescribing, addiction services and some other services need Google certification and are limited to certain countries, and speculative or experimental treatments are not allowed.

Google also restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, which limits remarketing.

This guide explains each rule in plain terms. It is not legal advice: always check Google's current healthcare and medicines policy, and your own regulator's rules, before launching. For how I run campaigns within these rules, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## What clinics can usually advertise

- Consultations and appointments with doctors, dentists, vets, physiotherapists and other clinicians
- Treatments and procedures described accurately, without promised outcomes
- Health checks, screenings, vaccinations and travel clinics
- Prices, opening hours, locations and availability
- The clinic's qualifications, memberships and experience, stated accurately

Most ads for these are approved, though health-related ads may be shown with limited reach in some places.

## Prescription medicine names

In most countries, Google does not allow prescription drug terms in ad text, landing pages or keywords. This catches out many clinics, because some common treatments use prescription medicines. Aesthetic clinics are a frequent example: anti-wrinkle injections often involve prescription products, so their brand names can trigger disapprovals.

**What to do:** advertise the consultation or the treatment type, not the medicine. Check your landing pages too, because a restricted term on the page can disapprove an ad even if the ad text is fine.

## Services that need certification

Some healthcare services need Google certification before ads can run, and are only allowed in certain countries:

- **Online pharmacies and online prescribing,** including many telemedicine services
- **Recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services,** which in the US also need third-party certification
- **Abortion services,** where requirements vary by location
- **Pharmaceutical manufacturers,** only in a few countries

If your clinic offers any of these, apply for certification before building campaigns, and check the location rules for every country you target.

## Treatments that cannot be advertised

Google does not allow ads for speculative or experimental medical treatments. Treatments in areas such as regenerative medicine are often affected, with limited exceptions in some places. If a treatment is new, unproven or experimental, assume it may be restricted and check the policy before advertising it.

## Remarketing and audience limits

Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions and treatments. In practice, this means:

- Remarketing lists for health-related pages may not be allowed, or may be limited
- Audiences based on health conditions cannot be used
- Some Customer Match uses may be restricted for health services

Veterinary clinics are usually less affected, because the restrictions concern people's own health. Search campaigns, which respond to what someone types, are generally unaffected.

## Claims and testimonials

Beyond Google's policies, most countries have advertising and professional rules for healthcare:

- **No guaranteed results** and no exaggerated success claims
- **Accurate titles and qualifications,** such as who can call themselves a specialist
- **Testimonials and before-and-after images,** which some regulators restrict or ban
- **Comparative claims,** such as "best clinic", which usually need proof

Chiropractic, aesthetics and weight management attract particular scrutiny. [Google Ads for chiropractors](/learn/google-ads-for-chiropractors/) and [cosmetic dentistry Google Ads](/learn/cosmetic-dentistry-google-ads/) cover two examples.

## Privacy and tracking

Health information is sensitive, and tracking on clinic websites needs care:

- In some countries, health privacy law covers how tracking tools may be used on pages where patients book or share health details, such as HIPAA in the US
- Consent rules for cookies apply in the UK, the EU and elsewhere
- Booking forms should collect only what is needed, and never detailed medical information on an ad landing page

Agree the tracking approach with whoever handles compliance for the clinic before adding new tracking.

## If an ad is disapproved

1. Read the policy reason shown in the ad status
2. Check the ad, keywords and the landing page for restricted terms or claims
3. Remove or reword the problem, then resubmit
4. If the service needs certification, apply for it rather than rewording around the rule

Repeated attempts to get around a healthcare policy can lead to account suspension, so fix the cause rather than the symptom.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can clinics advertise on Google Ads?

Yes. Most consultations, treatments and services can be advertised, as long as the ads are accurate and avoid restricted content such as prescription medicine names, unproven treatments and services that need certification.

### Why was my clinic's ad disapproved?

Common reasons are a prescription drug term in the ad, keywords or landing page, a service that needs certification, a claim that breaks policy, or a treatment Google considers speculative. The ad status shows the policy reason.

### Can clinics use remarketing on Google?

Often only in limited ways. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions and treatments, so many remarketing lists for health services are not allowed. Search campaigns are generally unaffected.

### Can I mention Botox or other medicine brand names in ads?

Usually not. Many aesthetic treatments use prescription medicines, and prescription drug terms are restricted in most countries. Advertise the consultation or treatment type instead.

### Do veterinary clinics face the same rules?

Not all of them. The personalised advertising restrictions concern people's own health, so vet clinics are usually less affected. Animal prescription medicines that can be misused by people still need certification for keyword targeting in the US and Canada.
