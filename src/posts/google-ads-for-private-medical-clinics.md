Google Ads works for private medical clinics when campaigns are built around the services people search for and the speed they need them. A same-day GP appointment, a walk-in visit for a minor injury and a specialist consultation are different searches, so they need different ads, landing pages and budgets.

Keep every ad within Google's healthcare policies, and measure appointments booked and attended rather than clicks. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Separate services by urgency and value

**Same-day and urgent care.** "Private GP same day", "walk-in clinic near me", "urgent care open now". Won by availability and opening hours, and usually decided within minutes.

**Routine private care.** Health checks, screenings, travel clinics, vaccinations. Planned, price-aware searches where people compare clinics.

**Specialist consultations.** Dermatology, gynaecology, cardiology, orthopaedics and other specialist services. Higher value, longer decisions, and a wider area people will travel from.

**Online and video consultations.** Where you offer them, these reach people far beyond your clinic, but they come with extra rules if prescribing is involved.

## Show availability in the ad

For urgent and same-day searches, the most persuasive thing you can say is when you can see someone:

- Opening hours and whether walk-ins are accepted
- Same-day or next-day appointments, only if you can actually offer them
- Location and how easy the clinic is to reach

Run urgent care campaigns only during the hours you can see patients, and track missed calls. A clinic that is closed when the ad shows pays for a click that cannot become an appointment.

## Health advertising rules come first

Medical clinics face more advertising rules than most businesses:

- **Prescription medicines.** In most countries, Google does not allow prescription drug names in ads, keywords or landing pages without certification. Advertise the consultation, not the medicine
- **Online prescribing and telemedicine.** Services that prescribe or dispense medicines online need Google certification, and are only allowed in certain countries
- **Unproven treatments.** Google does not allow ads for speculative or experimental medical treatments
- **Personalised advertising.** Google restricts targeting based on health conditions, so remarketing and some audiences may not be allowed
- **Privacy.** Health privacy laws, such as HIPAA in the US, can affect how tracking tools are used on booking pages

[What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) explains these rules in more detail.

## Price transparency helps

Private patients often compare costs before booking. Where you can, publish clear prices for consultations, and say what is included. If prices depend on assessment, give a starting point and explain why. Ads that mention a price should match the landing page exactly, or they risk disapproval and lose trust.

## Build landing pages for one service each

- The service and location in the headline, matching the search
- Next available appointments, or same-day availability if offered
- What the appointment includes and how long it takes
- Prices or price ranges
- The clinicians, with qualifications stated accurately
- Online booking and a phone number at the top

Collect only what you need in booking forms, and never ask for detailed medical information in an ad landing page form.

## Measure appointments

Track appointments booked and attended, by service, from your booking system. For specialist services, track consultations that lead to further treatment. [Enhanced conversions for leads](/learn/google-ads-enhanced-conversions-for-leads-guide/) explains how to send real outcomes back to Google Ads so bidding learns from them.

## Where medical clinic budgets leak

- Searches for free public healthcare services you do not provide
- Symptom and self-diagnosis searches with no intent to book
- Medical job, training and student searches
- Medicine and pharmacy searches
- Areas too far away for the service being advertised

## Frequently asked questions

### Can private medical clinics advertise on Google?

Yes, within Google's healthcare policies and your country's rules. Clinics can advertise consultations and services, but prescription medicine names, online prescribing and some treatments are restricted or need certification.

### How should urgent care clinics run Google Ads?

Around availability. Run ads during the hours you can see patients, show opening times and walk-in or same-day availability, and keep the location tight. Track booked or attended visits, not calls alone.

### Can medical clinics mention medicines in ads?

Usually not by name. In most countries prescription drug terms are not allowed in ads, keywords or landing pages without certification. Advertise the consultation or service instead.

### Can medical clinics use remarketing?

With care. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, so many remarketing approaches are not allowed for health services. Check the current policy for your services.

### What should a private clinic track in Google Ads?

Appointments booked and attended by service, and for specialist care, consultations that lead to treatment. Record the source of every booking so you can compare cost per patient with the value of each service.
