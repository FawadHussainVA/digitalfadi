Google Shopping sale price rules decide whether your products show a struck-through price and sale badge. Google now says a sale should be a temporary reduction: the sale price ideally should not run longer than 30 days, and the regular price should be the genuine price at other times.

Alongside that, Deal Ends badges are no longer experimental, and Shopping ads can show sold-count badges if you send cart data. Getting these right matters most in the holiday season, when shoppers compare offers side by side.

## The sale price rules

- **Use the sale price attribute,** with sale price start and end dates, rather than changing the main price
- **Keep sales temporary.** Google's guidance says the sale price duration should not exceed 30 days. It uses "should" rather than "must", but long-running sales risk losing the sale annotation
- **The regular price must be real.** It should be the price you actually charge outside the sale
- **Prices must match the website** at the moment Google checks, including during the sale, or products can be disapproved

## Badges that can appear on Shopping ads

**Sale price annotations.** The struck-through regular price and a sale label, when the sale meets Google's rules.

**Deal Ends badges.** A countdown-style badge, such as days left on an offer, under the price. Google has removed the experimental label from this annotation, and it depends on accurate sale dates.

**Purchase signal annotations.** On eligible Shopping ads, Google can automatically show a rounded sales count, such as "100+ sold last 30 days". This needs cart data sent with your conversions, so it is worth checking whether your conversion tracking includes it.

**Promotions.** Merchant Center promotions add offer labels, such as a percentage off or free delivery. Promotion titles should not include redemption dates; set dates in the promotion settings instead.

## Holiday checklist for Google Shopping sale prices

1. Add sale prices with start and end dates in the feed, not by editing the main price
2. Keep each sale to 30 days or less where you can
3. Check the regular price is the genuine everyday price
4. Make sure website prices change at the same moment as the feed
5. Submit promotions early, and keep dates out of promotion titles
6. Check whether your conversion tracking sends cart data, to become eligible for sold-count badges
7. Review Merchant Center diagnostics daily during the sale for disapprovals

## Common mistakes

- **Permanent "sales"** that run for months
- **Inflated regular prices** to make a discount look bigger
- **Feed and website prices out of sync** when a sale starts or ends
- **Dates in promotion titles**
- **Forgetting to remove sale prices** after the offer ends

## Frequently asked questions

### How long can a sale price run in Google Shopping?

Google's guidance says the sale price duration should not exceed 30 days, and the regular price should be the genuine price at other times. Longer sales may not show the sale annotation.

### How do I show a sale price in Google Shopping ads?

Add the sale price attribute with start and end dates in your Merchant Center feed, keep your regular price accurate, and make sure your website shows the same prices.

### What are Deal Ends badges?

They are Shopping ad annotations that show how long an offer has left. Google has made them a standard feature, and they rely on accurate sale dates.

### How do I get the "100+ sold" badge on Shopping ads?

Google can show purchase signal annotations automatically on eligible Shopping ads when you send cart data with your conversions. Eligibility is decided by Google.

### Why is my Shopping sale price not showing?

Common causes are a sale running too long, a regular price that is not genuine, missing sale dates, or feed and website prices that do not match.
