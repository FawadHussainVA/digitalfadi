If your Google Ads account is suspended, your ads stop running until the problem is fixed and Google reinstates the account. Most suspensions come from a small set of reasons: suspicious payment activity, unpaid balances, failed advertiser verification, misrepresentation, or a policy Google calls circumventing systems.

Read the reason in your account, fix the cause on your website and account first, then appeal once, clearly. Do not open a new account to get around a suspended Google Ads account; that usually makes things worse.

## Common reasons a Google Ads account is suspended

- **Suspicious payment activity:** payment details that Google cannot verify or that look unusual
- **Unpaid balance:** a failed payment that has not been settled
- **Advertiser verification:** not completing verification, or details that do not match
- **Misrepresentation:** missing business information, unclear pricing or terms, claims that cannot be supported, or a website that does not show who you are
- **Circumventing systems:** actions Google sees as trying to get around its policies, such as creating new accounts after a suspension or hiding what a website really offers
- **Unacceptable business practices:** for example, impersonation or misleading offers

The exact reason is shown in your account, usually with a link to the policy.

## What to do first

1. **Read the reason and the linked policy** carefully
2. **Stop and check, before you appeal.** Appeals without fixes usually fail
3. **Fix the website:** clear business name and contact details, address where relevant, accurate pricing, terms, privacy policy, and claims you can support
4. **Fix payment issues:** settle any balance and make sure payment details match the business
5. **Complete or correct advertiser verification**

## What not to do

- **Do not open a new Google Ads account** to keep advertising. Google can link accounts, and this is treated as circumventing systems
- **Do not appeal repeatedly** with the same message
- **Do not hide or quickly rebuild the website** to look different. Fix the real issue
- **Do not use someone else's account** to keep ads running

## How to appeal

Appeals are submitted from inside the account, from the suspension notice. A strong appeal:

- States the policy named in the suspension
- Explains what you changed, specifically, with page links
- Confirms the business details, payment information and verification are accurate
- Stays short, factual and polite

Then wait for the review. If the appeal is rejected, read the response, fix anything remaining, and appeal again only when something has genuinely changed.

## Preventing a suspension

- Keep business details consistent across your website, Google Ads, payment profile and verification
- Show clear pricing, terms and contact details on every landing page
- Avoid claims you cannot prove, especially in health, finance and legal advertising. [What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) covers health rules
- Pay invoices on time and keep a working backup payment method

## Frequently asked questions

### Why was my Google Ads account suspended?

Common reasons include suspicious payment activity, unpaid balances, failed advertiser verification, misrepresentation and circumventing systems. The exact reason is shown in your account with a link to the policy.

### How long does a Google Ads suspension appeal take?

Review times vary. Submit one clear appeal after fixing the cause, and wait for a response rather than sending repeated appeals.

### Can I create a new Google Ads account if mine is suspended?

You should not. Google can link accounts, and opening a new one to keep advertising is treated as circumventing systems, which can make the suspension harder to reverse.

### What does circumventing systems mean in Google Ads?

It covers actions Google sees as attempts to get around its policies or reviews, such as creating accounts after a suspension or showing reviewers different content from users.

### Can a suspended Google Ads account be restored?

Often, yes, if the cause is fixed and the appeal clearly explains what changed. Some policy violations are treated more seriously and may not be reversed.
