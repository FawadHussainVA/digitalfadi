Google Ads works for chiropractors when campaigns reach people searching for a chiropractor nearby, the ads make only claims the practice can support, and every enquiry is tracked through to a first visit and a course of care.

Claims are the biggest risk in this field: in some countries, advertising regulators have strict rules on which conditions chiropractors may say they treat, so ad copy needs more care than in most clinics.

For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Start with direct searches

The most valuable searches are people looking for a chiropractor, not for information:

- "chiropractor near me", "chiropractor in [town]"
- "sports chiropractor", "family chiropractor", "chiropractor open Saturday"
- Searches for your practice name, which deserve their own brand campaign

Symptom searches, such as back pain or neck pain, bring more volume but more mixed intent. Some people want a chiropractor; others want exercises, a doctor or a physiotherapist. Test them in separate ad groups, with tighter negatives, and judge them on booked visits.

## Be careful with claims

This is where chiropractic ads differ most from other clinics:

- **Check your regulator's rules** on which conditions you can mention, and how. Rules differ by country and can be strict
- **Avoid promises** of cures, pain relief in one session or guaranteed results
- **Describe what you do:** an assessment, a diagnosis where your rules allow it, a treatment plan
- **Use testimonials only where allowed,** and never edited into claims you could not make yourself

Google's healthcare policies apply too, and Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions. Remarketing lists built around a condition may not be allowed.

## New patient offers

Many practices advertise a first-visit offer, such as a reduced-price initial consultation. Offers can work well, but:

- State exactly what is included and any conditions
- Check that your professional rules allow the offer and how it is worded
- Track whether offer patients return, not just whether they book, because an offer that brings one-off visits can cost more than it earns

## Build landing pages that answer first-visit questions

New patients want to know what will happen. A good landing page explains:

- What the first appointment involves and how long it takes
- The price, or how insurance and payment work where relevant
- Who the chiropractor is, with qualifications stated accurately
- How to book, with online booking and a phone number at the top
- Location, parking and opening hours

Keep the page specific to the search. A sports chiropractic ad should lead to a page about sports care, not the homepage.

## Measure patients who return

A first visit is only the start. Track:

1. **First visits booked** from ads
2. **First visits attended**
3. **Patients who continue care** after the first visit

Cost per first visit can look cheap while cost per returning patient tells the real story. Record the source of every new patient in your practice software and compare it with your Google Ads data. ## Where chiropractic budgets leak

- Stretches, exercises and home remedy searches
- Chiropractic school, courses and job searches
- Searches for other professions, such as osteopaths or physiotherapists, if you do not want to compete for them
- Free or public services you do not provide
- Areas too far away for regular visits

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for chiropractors?

Yes, when campaigns focus on people searching for a chiropractor nearby, ads stay within the rules on claims, and the practice tracks first visits and returning patients. Symptom searches can work too, but need closer management.

### What claims can chiropractors make in Google Ads?

It depends on your country and regulator, and some are strict about which conditions chiropractors can mention. Avoid promises of cures or guaranteed relief, describe your services accurately, and check your regulator's guidance before writing ads.

### Are new patient offers a good idea for chiropractic ads?

They can be, if the offer is clear, allowed by your professional rules, and measured on whether patients continue care. An offer that only brings one-off visits can cost more than it earns.

### Can chiropractors use remarketing?

With care. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, so condition-based remarketing may not be allowed. Check the current policy and your account settings.

### What should a chiropractor track in Google Ads?

First visits booked and attended, and patients who continue care. Cost per returning patient shows whether the campaign is profitable far better than cost per click.
