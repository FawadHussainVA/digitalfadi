Few questions in Google Ads produce more confident opinions and less evidence. One side says brand clicks are free through the organic listing, so paying is waste. The other says a competitor will take the space if you leave it. Both are sometimes right, and which one applies to you is testable.

## Why brand looks so good in reports

Brand campaigns post the best numbers in almost every account. Low cost per click, high click-through rate, high conversion rate, excellent return on ad spend.

This is not performance. It is selection. Somebody typing your company name has already decided to find you. The advert did not persuade them, it intercepted them.

The danger is what this does to everything else. Blended into the account average, brand makes non-brand look better than it is, and hides whether your advertising is finding anybody new. Any report that does not separate the two is answering a question nobody asked.

## The case for bidding on brand

**Competitors can bid on your name.** In most countries this is allowed. Somebody searching for you can be shown a competitor above your organic listing.

**You control the message.** An advert lets you say what the organic listing cannot: current offer, a specific landing page, sitelinks to the pages you want them on.

**You take more of the page.** An advert plus the organic listing pushes competitors further down.

**It is usually cheap.** Quality Score on your own brand is high, so clicks cost little.

## The case against

**Most of those clicks were free.** If you rank first organically and no competitor is bidding, a brand advert largely buys clicks you already had.

**It inflates the picture.** Brand conversions blended into totals make the account look like it is working when new demand may be flat.

**Sometimes nobody is attacking.** Agencies justify brand spend with competitor bidding without checking whether any competitor is actually there.

## How to find out, properly

The argument is settleable. Run a holdout.

1. Note your current brand impression share and the total conversions attributed to brand terms
2. Pause the brand campaign for a defined period, two weeks or four depending on volume
3. Watch total conversions for the business, not just the campaign
4. Watch organic traffic for brand queries in Search Console over the same period

If total conversions hold steady while brand campaign conversions fall to zero, the organic listing absorbed them and you were paying for free clicks.

If total conversions fall, the brand campaign was doing something, and you now know roughly how much.

Run it in a quiet period, not during a promotion or a seasonal peak, and do not change anything else while it runs.

## Before you run it, check who is actually bidding

Open an incognito window and search your company name. Then check auction insights on the brand campaign.

If no competitor appears on either, the defensive argument does not apply to you today. It might apply next quarter, which is why this is worth rechecking rather than deciding once.

## The Performance Max complication

If Performance Max runs without brand exclusions, it is already bidding on your brand whether you decided to or not, and reporting those conversions as its own.

This makes the whole question unanswerable until you exclude brand from Performance Max. Otherwise pausing the brand Search campaign simply hands the same traffic to Performance Max, and the holdout proves nothing.

Details in [Performance Max: what it hides and how to see it](/learn/performance-max-what-it-hides/).

## A reasonable default

For most businesses, once brand is separated and measured:

- Keep a small brand campaign if competitors are visibly bidding on your name
- Keep it if the advert genuinely sends people somewhere better than the organic listing does
- Cut it or cap it tightly if neither applies
- Either way, report brand separately and judge acquisition on non-brand alone

The point is not which answer you reach. It is that the answer comes from a test in your account rather than from an opinion.
