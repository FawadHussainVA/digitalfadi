Google Ads starts producing clicks within a day or so of launch, once the ads are approved. Knowing whether it works takes longer, and the honest measure is data rather than time: you can judge a campaign once it has had enough clicks and conversions to show a pattern. For a campaign with a healthy budget that can be a few weeks. For a small budget, or a business with a long sales cycle, it can be a few months.

Anyone who gives you a fixed number of months without asking about your budget, your cost per click and how long your customers take to decide is guessing.

## What "working" actually means

Before asking how long, decide what you are waiting for. There are three different questions hiding inside "is it working?":

1. **Is it running?** Ads approved, showing, getting clicks. Answered within days
2. **Is it reaching the right people?** Searches that triggered the ads are relevant. Answered within the first week or two
3. **Is it profitable?** Cost per enquiry or sale is below what a customer is worth. Answered once there are enough conversions to trust the average

Most frustration comes from expecting the third answer in the time it takes to get the first.

## Day one to day three: approval and first clicks

New ads are reviewed before they show, and most reviews finish within a business day. Some take longer, particularly in regulated categories such as finance, health or legal services.

Once approved, ads start showing straight away. The first clicks prove the setup works, and nothing more.

## Week one to two: read the searches, not the results

The most useful report in the first fortnight is the search terms report, which shows the actual searches that triggered your ads. This is where you find job seekers, DIY searches and people looking for something you do not sell, and remove them with [negative keywords](/learn/how-to-find-negative-keywords/).

Conversion numbers in this period are too small to mean much. Three enquiries in the first week could be a great start or pure luck, and so could zero.

If the campaign uses a new automated bidding strategy, it will also show a Learning status at this stage, for up to seven days. Performance often wobbles while it settles. Changing the budget or target will not restart that status, but constant changes make it impossible to tell what is working. [Which Google Ads bid strategy should you use?](/learn/which-google-ads-bid-strategy-should-you-use/) covers this in more detail.

## Weeks three to eight: the first fair verdict

By now a campaign with a reasonable budget has enough clicks for the conversion rate to settle, and enough conversions for cost per enquiry to mean something. This is the first point at which "is it profitable?" has a real answer.

It is also when the meaningful improvements happen: pausing keywords that spend without converting, rewriting weak ads, adjusting bids by device, location or time of day, and tightening the landing page.

## Why some accounts take longer

The timeline stretches when data arrives slowly. The usual reasons:

- **A small budget.** If the budget buys a handful of clicks a day, it takes months to collect what a larger account gathers in weeks. [How much should you spend on Google Ads?](/learn/how-much-should-you-spend-on-google-ads/) shows how to check whether your budget can produce a verdict at all
- **Low search volume.** Niche services in small areas simply have fewer searches to buy
- **A long sales cycle.** If customers take a month to decide, a click today may become a sale next month. Judging the campaign on this week's sales undercounts it
- **Conversion delay.** Sales and calls are recorded against the day of the click, so recent days look worse than they are until late conversions arrive
- **Broken tracking.** If conversions are not recorded properly, the account can be working while the report says it is not, or the reverse

## Why it can look like it works when it does not

The opposite problem is just as common. An account that counts page views or button clicks as conversions can show excellent numbers from the first week while producing no real enquiries. Fast results are only good news if the thing being counted is a customer. [Why are my Google Ads not converting?](/learn/why-are-my-google-ads-not-converting/) covers how to check.

## How to speed it up honestly

You cannot rush the data, but you can stop wasting it:

1. **Verify tracking before launch,** so every early conversion counts and nothing false is counted
2. **Concentrate the budget** on your best service and best area, rather than spreading it thin
3. **Add obvious negative keywords on day one,** so the early budget is not spent on job seekers and researchers
4. **Make one change at a time,** then wait for it to show in the numbers
5. **Judge on cost per enquiry or sale,** not on clicks or cost per click

## When to stop waiting

If a campaign has had enough clicks for its conversion rate to settle and cost per enquiry is still well above what a customer is worth, more time alone will not fix it. Something in the keywords, the ads, the landing page or the offer needs to change.

That is the point to look properly rather than wait longer. A [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/) checks tracking first, then where the money is going, so you know whether the campaign needs time, changes or stopping.
