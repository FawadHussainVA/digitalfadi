Google Ads recommendations are suggestions Google makes to change your account, such as raising budgets, adding keywords or switching bid strategies. Some are genuinely useful; others increase spend without improving results. Review each one against your own data, accept the ones that fit your goals, and dismiss the rest. Dismissing a recommendation also counts towards your optimization score.

This guide explains how Google Ads recommendations and optimization score work, which suggestions usually help, and which to be careful with.

## What optimization score measures

Optimization score is a percentage from 0 to 100 that estimates how well your account is set up according to Google's recommendations. Accepting or dismissing recommendations raises it. It does not measure profit, leads or sales. An account can have a high score and poor results, or a lower score and excellent results.

## Why some advertisers chase the score

Google Partner status requires an optimization score of at least 70% across the accounts a partner manages. That gives some agencies a reason to accept recommendations quickly. A good manager reaches a healthy score by reviewing each recommendation and dismissing the ones that do not fit, not by accepting everything.

## Recommendations that often help

- **Fixing problems:** disapproved ads, conversion tracking issues, missing assets
- **Adding assets** such as sitelinks, callouts and images. [Google Ads sitelinks](/learn/google-ads-sitelinks/) explains how
- **Improving ads** with more varied headlines. [Responsive search ads](/learn/responsive-search-ads/) covers this
- **Budget increases on campaigns that are profitable and limited by budget,** checked against your own cost per lead or sale

## Recommendations to review carefully

- **Raise your budget,** when the campaign is not profitable
- **Add broad match keywords** or switch on AI Max, without reliable tracking and regular search term reviews. [AI Max for Search](/learn/ai-max-for-search/) explains the trade-offs
- **Switch bid strategy,** when the account lacks enough conversions for Smart Bidding to learn
- **Remove redundant keywords,** which can remove keywords you deliberately kept
- **Expand to new channels** before your core campaign is working

Each recommendation shows an estimated impact. Treat it as Google's estimate, not a promise.

## Turn off risky auto-apply recommendations

Google Ads can apply some recommendations automatically. Open **Recommendations**, then **Auto-apply**, and check which types are switched on. Keep auto-apply off for anything that changes budgets, bidding, keywords or match types unless you are sure. Changes you did not make are a common reason accounts suddenly spend more.

## A simple review routine

1. Check recommendations weekly or every two weeks
2. Accept fixes for genuine problems straight away
3. Test larger changes, such as bid strategy or broad match, with an [experiment](/learn/google-ads-experiments-ab-testing-guide/)
4. Dismiss the rest, and choose a reason, so Google learns your preferences
5. Judge the account on leads, sales and cost per result, not the score

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I accept all Google Ads recommendations?

No. Review each one against your goals and data. Accept fixes and useful improvements, test bigger changes, and dismiss suggestions that would raise spend without improving results.

### What is a good Google Ads optimization score?

There is no score that guarantees good results. A healthy score from reviewed recommendations is fine; chasing 100% by accepting everything can hurt performance.

### Does dismissing recommendations lower my optimization score?

No. Dismissing a recommendation also counts towards your optimization score, so you can raise the score without applying changes that do not suit your account.

### What are auto-apply recommendations in Google Ads?

They are recommendation types Google can apply automatically. Check them under Recommendations and Auto-apply, and switch off any that change budgets, bids or keywords without your review.

### Why does my agency keep accepting recommendations?

Google Partner status includes an optimization score requirement, so some agencies accept recommendations to keep their score up. Ask how each change helps your results.
