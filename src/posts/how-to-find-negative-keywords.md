Negative keywords come from three places: the searches you can predict before a campaign launches, the search terms report once it is running, and the patterns in that report that only show up when you look at it in aggregate. Most accounts do the first, skim the second and never do the third, which is where the steady, invisible waste lives.

## What a negative keyword does

A negative keyword stops your ad from showing on any search that contains it. If you repair boilers and add jobs as a negative, your ad will not appear for boiler engineer jobs.

Positive keywords stretch to cover plurals, misspellings and close variants. Negative keywords do not. Adding job will not block jobs, so the singular, the plural and common misspellings each need adding.

## The three negative match types

- **Negative broad** blocks a search containing all of the negative's words, in any order. A negative of free quote blocks quote free online
- **Negative phrase** blocks a search containing the words in that exact order, with anything around them. A negative of how to blocks how to fix a boiler
- **Negative exact** blocks only that exact search, with nothing added. A negative of boiler blocks the search boiler and nothing else

Phrase match is the safest default. It is precise without being as narrow as exact, and it avoids the surprises of broad negatives built from common words.

## Source 1: the searches you can predict

Before a campaign spends anything, some waste is obvious. Nearly every business attracts the same groups of searchers who will never buy:

1. Job seekers: jobs, careers, salary, apprenticeship
2. Learners: course, training, certification
3. Do-it-yourselfers: how to, DIY, tutorial
4. Researchers: what is, meaning, definition
5. People wanting it for nothing: free, torrent, download
6. Searches for places you do not serve, and products you do not sell

Read every word before you add it. Free is waste for most businesses, yet free quote is a buying search for a tradesperson. Cheap blocks bargain hunters and also blocks a budget service that is exactly what you sell.

The [negative keyword generator](/tools/negative-keyword-generator/) has ready lists for each of these groups, with the risky words switched off by default, and checks the list against your own keywords before you paste it in.

## Source 2: the search terms report

Once a campaign runs, the search terms report shows the actual searches that triggered your ads. In Google Ads it sits under Insights and reports, then Search terms.

Look for three things:

1. **Spend with no conversions** on searches that are clearly the wrong intent
2. **Searches that are close but wrong**, such as a product you do not stock with a name like one you do
3. **Searches that belong in another campaign**, which become negatives here so the right campaign gets them

Google does not show every search. Lower-volume searches are grouped out of view, so the report always shows less than you paid for. That is a reason to act on the patterns you can see, not to ignore the report.

## Source 3: patterns across the whole report

Read line by line, a search terms report rarely looks alarming. Each wrong search has one or two clicks. The waste only shows when you add those clicks up by the words they share.

That is what n-gram analysis does. It splits every search into single words and short phrases, then totals the cost and conversions of every search containing each one. A word that appears in sixty different searches, none of which converted, becomes one line with one total.

The [search terms n-gram analyser](/tools/n-gram-analyser/) does this from a downloaded report, in your browser, and exports the phrases you tick as a negative list.

## Where to add each negative

- **Shared negative keyword lists** for words that are wrong everywhere, such as jobs and training. Apply the list to every relevant campaign
- **Campaign negatives** for words wrong in one campaign but right in another
- **Ad group negatives** for directing searches between ad groups inside the same campaign
- **Account-level negatives** for terms you never want to appear for anywhere in the account

Negatives also steer traffic. Adding emergency as a negative on a planned-maintenance campaign sends those searches to the emergency campaign, where the ad and the landing page actually match.

## The mistakes that cost the most

1. **Blocking your own keywords.** One careless negative can switch off your best keyword without any warning. Check every new list against what you bid on
2. **Forgetting variants.** Job and jobs are two separate negatives, and so are career and careers
3. **Single-word negatives that are too broad.** One word blocks every search containing it, good ones included
4. **Judging on one click.** A search with a single click and no conversion has told you very little. Wait for a pattern unless the intent is plainly wrong
5. **Set and forget.** Search behaviour changes, and broad match keeps finding new searches

## How often to check

Weekly while a campaign is new, because the first weeks surface the most waste. Monthly once it settles. If wasted spend keeps returning however many negatives you add, the cause is usually match types or account structure rather than missing negatives, which is covered in [why your Google Ads cost per click went up](/learn/why-your-cost-per-click-went-up/) and is part of every [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/).
