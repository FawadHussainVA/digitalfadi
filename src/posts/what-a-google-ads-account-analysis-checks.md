When an account is underperforming, the instinct is to change something quickly: raise the budget, cut the keywords, rewrite the adverts, switch bidding. Each of those changes is only worth making if the numbers behind them are right. A proper account analysis starts by checking that they are.

## Why the order matters

Google Ads makes decisions from the data it receives. If conversions are counted twice, counted at the wrong value, or not counted at all, the bidding system optimises towards the wrong outcome. Changing bids on top of broken tracking just makes the wrong outcome more expensive.

That is why the analysis checks the foundations first and only then looks at the campaigns.

## Conversion tracking

The first stage confirms that the actions you care about are recorded: enquiries, purchases, booked calls, whatever the business counts as a result. It checks for duplicates, for missing values, and for conversions that fire on pages they should not. It also looks at whether the primary conversion is the one bidding is actually using, because a campaign can be optimised for a minor action such as a page view without anyone noticing.

## Search terms

Next, the search terms report. This is what people typed before clicking. It shows whether the account is spending on relevant searches, on searches that belong to someone else, or on broad queries that drift far from what the business sells. Irrelevant terms are noted with the spend they have consumed, and the analysis separates them into terms to exclude and terms that deserve their own tighter campaign.

## Structure and settings

The third stage looks at how the account is organised. Are related products grouped together, or is one campaign carrying everything? Do the match types and bidding settings fit the goal? Are there overlapping campaigns competing for the same searches? Structural problems rarely show up in a single report, but they explain why an account can look busy and still underperform.

## Adverts and landing pages

Finally, the adverts and the pages they point to are reviewed together. Headlines and descriptions are checked for character limits, duplicates and relevance to the search. The landing page is checked for whether it answers the question the advert raised, because a strong advert sending people to a weak page wastes its own clicks.

## What you receive

The output is a written list of what was found, ordered by how much it is likely to matter, with the reasoning behind each point. It is an analysis, not a sales deck, and it does not change anything in your account. Any changes are a separate decision, and they are easier to judge once the foundations are known to be sound.
