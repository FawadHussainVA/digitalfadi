Google Ads works for cosmetic surgery clinics when each procedure has its own campaign, ads lead with a consultation with a named surgeon, and every claim and image follows advertising rules. Patients research for months and compare surgeons carefully, so trust and clear information matter more than offers.

Keep ads away from under-18s where your rules require it, and measure consultations that become booked procedures, not enquiries. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## One campaign per procedure

Cosmetic surgery searches are specific, and each procedure differs in price, patient and decision time:

- **Breast procedures:** augmentation, reduction, uplift
- **Body procedures:** tummy tuck, liposuction
- **Facial procedures:** rhinoplasty, eyelid surgery, facelift
- **Non-surgical treatments,** where offered, kept separate because they follow different rules and price points

Separate campaigns let each procedure have its own budget, ads and landing page. A single "cosmetic surgery" page for every search converts poorly and wastes budget on low-value searches.

## Lead with the surgeon and a consultation

Patients choose a surgeon as much as a clinic. Ads and landing pages should show:

- **Who performs the procedure,** with qualifications and registrations stated accurately
- **A consultation as the first step,** free or paid, with what it involves
- **Realistic information:** recovery time, risks and aftercare
- **Price as a starting point or range** where you can stand behind it, plus finance options if offered

Avoid time-limited discounts and pressure tactics. Many regulators discourage them for surgery, and they can attract the wrong patients.

## Age and audience rules

- **Under-18s.** In some countries, such as the UK, cosmetic intervention ads must not be directed at under-18s through the choice of media or context. In Google Ads, that means excluding younger age groups where possible, avoiding placements aimed at young people, and keeping Display and YouTube placements under control
- **Personalised advertising.** Google restricts targeting based on health, which can limit remarketing
- **Other countries** have their own rules, so check the advertising code and professional guidance where you operate

## Claims and images

- **No guaranteed results** or promises of a perfect outcome
- **Before-and-after images** only of your own patients, with consent, and only where your regulator and Google's policies allow them
- **Body imagery** can trigger Google's adult content policies even when it is clinical, which can limit where ads show. Keep images respectful and relevant
- **Prescription medicine names,** such as brand names of injectables, are restricted in most countries

[What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) explains these rules in more detail.

## Landing pages for a long decision

- The procedure and surgeon in the headline
- Who the procedure suits, and that suitability is decided at consultation
- What happens before, during and after surgery
- Price range, finance and what is included
- Real reviews where your rules allow them
- Booking for a consultation, with a phone number and a short enquiry form

## Measure consultations and procedures

Track:

1. **Consultation requests and bookings**
2. **Consultations attended**
3. **Procedures booked,** with their value

Because the decision is long, judge campaigns over several months. Record the source of each patient in your practice system and send outcomes back to Google Ads where you can. [Enhanced conversions for leads](/learn/google-ads-enhanced-conversions-for-leads-guide/) explains how.

## Where cosmetic surgery budgets leak

- Surgery abroad and medical tourism searches, unless you want them
- Cheap or discount procedure searches
- Searches for complications and revision of surgery done elsewhere, unless you offer revision
- Nursing and surgical training, jobs and courses
- Procedures you do not perform

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for cosmetic surgery clinics?

Yes, when each procedure has its own campaign, ads lead with a consultation and a named surgeon, and the clinic tracks procedures booked. Patients research for a long time, so judge results over months.

### Can cosmetic surgery ads target under-18s?

In some countries, such as the UK, they must not be directed at under-18s. Exclude younger age groups where possible, avoid placements aimed at young people, and check the rules in every country you target.

### Are before-and-after photos allowed in cosmetic surgery ads?

Only where your regulator and Google's policies allow them, using your own patients with consent. Body imagery can also limit where ads show under Google's adult content policies.

### Should cosmetic surgery ads show prices?

A starting price or range on the landing page usually helps, with finance options if offered. Avoid time-limited discounts, which can encourage rushed decisions and may break professional guidance.

### What should a cosmetic surgery clinic track in Google Ads?

Consultation requests, consultations attended and procedures booked with their value. Cost per procedure booked is the measure that shows whether campaigns are profitable.
