Emergency dentist ads win when they match real availability. Someone with toothache or a broken tooth calls the first practice that can see them soon, so run emergency ads only when you can answer the phone and offer an appointment, make calling the main action, keep the location tight, and measure booked emergency appointments rather than calls. An emergency ad that runs at 9pm for a practice that cannot see anyone until next week pays for clicks that cannot become patients.

For the wider picture across emergency, general and implant work, see [Google Ads for dental clinics](/google-ads-for-dentists/).

## What emergency searchers want

People searching for an emergency dentist are usually in pain or worried, and they want three answers fast:

1. **Can you see me today, or soon?**
2. **How do I contact you right now?**
3. **Are you close enough to reach?**

Everything in the campaign should answer those three questions in the ad, before anyone clicks.

## Run ads only when you can help

The schedule is the most important setting in an emergency campaign.

- **Match the ad schedule to the hours someone answers the phone,** not just the hours the practice is open
- **Separate same-day availability from general hours.** If emergency slots are usually gone by mid-morning, reduce bids or pause emergency ads after that point
- **Evenings and weekends:** run them only if you offer appointments or a working out-of-hours line. Otherwise you pay for calls nobody can answer

If you cannot cover evenings, consider ads that clearly offer the next available appointment rather than implying immediate care.

## Make calling the main action

Emergency patients usually want to talk to someone, not fill in a form.

- **Add a call asset** to every emergency ad, showing your number on mobile
- **Put the phone number at the top of the landing page,** so it is visible without scrolling
- **State availability honestly:** same-day appointments if you offer them, or the next available slot
- **Keep the page short.** Opening hours, location, what to do now, and the number

Track missed calls. A missed call from an emergency ad is paid for and almost always lost to the next practice on the page.

## Keep the location tight

Emergency patients will not travel far. A wide radius that suits implant treatment wastes money on emergency searches.

- Target a small area around the practice, based on how far patients actually travel to you
- Use the location option that shows ads only to people in your area. [Google Ads location targeting](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) explains why the default setting often wastes budget
- Add town names you do not serve as negative keywords

## Write ads that answer the urgent questions

Good emergency ad copy is plain and specific:

- **Lead with the problem and availability:** toothache, broken tooth, swelling, with same-day appointments if true
- **Name the area,** so the searcher knows you are close
- **Avoid promises you cannot keep,** such as guaranteed same-day care if slots run out

Health advertising rules apply, so avoid claims about outcomes. [How to write Google Ads headlines](/learn/how-to-write-google-ads-headlines/) covers structure and testing.

## Consider Local Services Ads

In the US and some other markets, dentists can join Local Services Ads. These appear above the normal results with a Google verification badge, show your hours and reviews, and charge per lead. For urgent searches, that visibility and the badge can help. Run them alongside Search where available, and measure the two separately. [Local Services Ads vs Google Ads](/learn/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads/) explains the differences.

## Measure booked emergency appointments

Calls are only the first step. Some callers are existing patients, some want advice only, and some cannot be seen in time.

- **Count calls that lead to a booked emergency appointment,** recorded from the practice system or the call notes
- **Track new versus existing patients,** because an emergency often brings in a new patient who stays for routine care
- **Check missed and very short calls** each week

An emergency appointment that turns into a long-term patient is worth far more than the first visit. Where you can, give new emergency patients a higher conversion value. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) shows how.

## Where emergency budgets leak

- **Home remedy searches:** toothache relief at home, pain relief for toothache
- **Free or emergency care you do not provide,** such as hospital or charity services
- **Searches far outside your area**
- **Calls outside the hours you can answer**

[Negative keywords for dental practices](/learn/dental-negative-keywords/) has a starter list, including emergency-specific terms.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should emergency dentist ads run 24 hours a day?

Only if someone can answer and offer help at those hours. Otherwise the ads pay for calls that cannot become appointments. Match the schedule to real availability.

### Are call-only ads better for emergency dentists?

Calls are usually the most important action, so call assets and a prominent number matter most. Whether to use call-focused ads or standard ads with call assets depends on how your patients prefer to contact you. Test both against booked appointments.

### How far should an emergency dentist target?

A small area around the practice, based on how far patients actually travel for urgent care. Emergency patients rarely travel as far as patients planning treatment.

### What should emergency dental ads be measured on?

Booked emergency appointments, and how many of those patients become regular patients. Calls alone overstate results.

### Can emergency patients become long-term patients?

Often, yes. A patient who is seen quickly in an emergency may stay for routine care, which is why their value is usually higher than the first appointment.
