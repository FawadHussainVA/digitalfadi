Dental implant Google Ads work when they are built for a long, expensive decision. Patients research costs, read about the procedure, compare clinics and think about finance before they book. Give implants their own campaign, answer cost and finance questions honestly, offer a consultation rather than pushing a price, and judge the campaign on consultations that turn into accepted treatment plans, not on form fills. Judged on leads alone, implant campaigns look expensive. Judged on accepted cases, they are often among the most valuable campaigns a practice runs.

For the wider approach across emergency, general and implant work, see [Google Ads for dental clinics](/google-ads-for-dentists/).

## Why implants need their own campaign

Implant searches behave differently from routine dental searches:

- **The value per patient is much higher,** so you can afford a higher cost per lead, but only for the right lead
- **The decision takes longer,** often weeks, with several visits to your website before anyone books
- **Competition is stronger,** because many practices chase the same high-value searches
- **Patients compare,** so trust and clarity matter more than a low headline price

If implant keywords share a campaign with check-ups and hygiene, the budget follows whatever is cheapest to click. Separating them lets you set budgets, bids and ads for the work you actually want more of.

## Organise by how far along the patient is

**Ready to book:** "dental implants near me", "implant dentist in [town]". The clearest intent, and the core of the budget.

**Treatment-specific:** single tooth implants, implant-retained dentures, full arch treatment. Each deserves its own ad group and landing page, because each answers a different situation.

**Cost and finance research:** "dental implant cost", "dental implants on finance". People early in the decision. Worth reaching at lower bids, with an ad that offers a consultation and clear information rather than a number.

Avoid using the brand names of implant systems or treatment concepts in ads unless you are sure you are allowed to. Some are trademarks, and Google's trademark policy can restrict them in ad text.

## Handle cost searches honestly

Cost is the first question most implant patients ask, and the hardest to answer before an assessment. Practices that refuse to say anything lose these searchers to practices that help.

- **Give a realistic range or a starting point** where you can stand behind it, and explain what changes the price
- **Explain what is included:** assessment, scans, the implant, the crown, follow-up
- **Make finance clear,** if you offer it, including that it is subject to approval
- **Offer a consultation** as the next step, so the searcher can get a personal answer

Do not advertise a price that very few patients will actually pay. It brings clicks, but the consultations that follow tend not to accept treatment.

## Build landing pages for a big decision

A single page for "dental implants" rarely converts every kind of implant searcher. A strong implant landing page:

- Explains the treatment in plain language, with the stages and typical timeline
- Shows who carries out the treatment, with qualifications you can verify
- Answers the common questions: cost, pain, recovery, how long implants last, who is suitable
- Uses real photographs of your own work and team, where your regulator and Google's policies allow
- Offers more than one way to start: call, online booking, a consultation request form

Keep claims factual. Health advertising rules apply, and statements such as guaranteed results or a "permanent" fix for everyone are risky.

## Expect a longer path, and plan for it

Many implant patients visit your site several times before booking. You can reach them again, but carefully. Google restricts some personalised advertising around health topics, so check which audiences your account allows before building remarketing lists. Where remarketing is allowed, a reminder of the consultation offer usually works better than repeating the same ad.

The other way to shorten the path is follow-up. Consultations that are confirmed by phone, with a reminder before the appointment, are more likely to happen. A booked consultation that never shows up is a common, invisible leak in implant campaigns.

## Measure every stage

Track the full journey, not just the first step:

1. **Enquiry:** call or form from the implant campaign
2. **Consultation booked**
3. **Consultation attended**
4. **Treatment plan accepted,** with its value

Feed accepted treatment back into Google Ads where you can, using offline conversion import or [enhanced conversions for leads](/learn/google-ads-enhanced-conversions-for-leads-guide/), so bidding learns from cases rather than enquiries. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) covers the setup.

Then calculate cost per accepted case, and compare it with the value of the treatment. That is the number that decides whether implant ads are working.

## Where implant budgets leak

- **Searches from people looking abroad** for cheaper treatment, unless you want them
- **Job and training searches:** implant courses, implantologist jobs
- **DIY and product searches** that have nothing to do with treatment
- **Areas further than patients will travel**, though implant patients often travel further than for routine care

## Frequently asked questions

### Are dental implant Google Ads worth it?

They can be, because a single accepted case can be worth many times the cost of the leads behind it. They are only worth it when the practice tracks accepted treatment, follows up quickly and runs implants in their own campaign.

### Should I show implant prices in my ads?

Show a realistic starting point or range only if most patients pay close to it. Otherwise lead with a consultation offer and explain the price on the landing page, including what affects it.

### How long does it take for implant ads to produce cases?

Often several weeks, because patients research and compare before booking, and treatment is planned after a consultation. Judge the campaign over a longer period than a routine dental campaign.

### What should implant campaigns be measured on?

Consultations booked, consultations attended and treatment plans accepted, with their value. Cost per accepted case is the most useful single number.

### Can I use implant brand names in my ads?

Only if you are sure you have the right to. Brand names of implant systems and treatment concepts can be trademarks, and Google may restrict them in ad text.
