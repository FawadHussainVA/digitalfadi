Google Ads works for optometrists and eye clinics when eye tests, eyewear and specialist treatment such as laser eye surgery are run as separate campaigns. They are three different purchases: a routine appointment, a retail sale and a major medical decision, each with its own value, competition and path to booking.

Track appointments, eyewear sales and consultations separately, so each campaign is judged on what it actually produces. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Three kinds of eye care search

**Eye tests and routine care.** "Eye test near me", "optician in [town]", "children's eye test". Local, steady demand, often booked within days. The value is in the test and the eyewear that often follows.

**Eyewear.** Glasses, designer frames and contact lenses. People compare prices and styles, and some will buy online instead. Practices with a strong range or a specialist service, such as sports or children's eyewear, can stand out here.

**Specialist treatment.** Laser eye surgery, lens replacement, cataract surgery, dry eye clinics. High value, long research and wide travel distances. These deserve their own campaigns and landing pages.

## Eye tests: availability and convenience

- Show next available appointments, opening hours and evening or weekend slots if you offer them
- Keep the location tight, because people choose an optician close to home or work
- Mention eligibility for free or funded eye tests where it applies, if you provide them
- Make online booking the main action, with the phone number beside it

## Eyewear: be specific

Generic "glasses" searches are crowded with large online retailers. Focus on what you do differently:

- Brands you stock, where you are allowed to advertise them
- Specialist services: sports eyewear, children's frames, complex prescriptions
- In-store fitting and aftercare that online sellers cannot offer

If you sell online, Shopping campaigns with a well-built product feed are usually more efficient than text ads for frames. [Google Shopping feed optimisation](/learn/google-shopping-feed-optimisation/) explains what the feed needs.

## Laser eye surgery and specialist treatment

These searches are the most valuable and the most competitive:

- **Lead with a consultation,** not a price, unless most patients pay close to the price you show
- **Explain suitability,** because not everyone is a candidate, and saying so builds trust
- **Answer the common questions** on the landing page: recovery time, risks, aftercare, finance
- **Keep claims accurate.** Health advertising rules apply, and outcome promises such as perfect vision are risky

Patients research for weeks, so measure consultations and treatment accepted over a longer period than eye tests.

## Health advertising rules

Eye care ads follow Google's healthcare policies. Prescription medicine names, such as some eye drops, are restricted in most countries. Google also restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, which limits some remarketing. [What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) covers the rules in more detail.

## Measure each campaign on its own outcome

- **Eye tests:** appointments booked and attended
- **Eyewear:** sales, online or in store, recorded with their source
- **Laser and specialist treatment:** consultations booked, attended and treatment accepted

Mixing these in one conversion count makes the cheap eye test bookings look like the whole story.

## Where eye care budgets leak

- Searches for cheap online glasses and contact lens retailers by name
- Eye symptom and self-diagnosis searches with no intent to book
- Optometry courses, jobs and training
- Free services you do not provide
- Areas too far away for routine eye tests

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for optometrists?

Yes, especially for eye test bookings in the local area and specialist services. They work best when eye tests, eyewear and specialist treatment are separate campaigns, each measured on its own outcome.

### Should optometrists advertise glasses on Google?

Only with a clear angle, such as specialist eyewear, brands you stock or fitting and aftercare, because generic searches are dominated by large online retailers. Shopping campaigns can work well if you sell online.

### How should laser eye surgery clinics run Google Ads?

As a separate, consultation-led campaign, with honest information about suitability, recovery and cost on the landing page. Measure consultations and treatment accepted, not just enquiries.

### Can eye clinics use remarketing?

With care. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, so some remarketing for eye conditions may not be allowed. Check the current policy for your services.

### What should an eye clinic track in Google Ads?

Eye test appointments, eyewear sales and specialist consultations, each as a separate conversion with its own value, so every campaign is judged fairly.
