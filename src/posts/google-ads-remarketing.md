Google Ads remarketing, also called retargeting, shows ads to people who have already visited your website, watched your videos or bought from you. Most visitors leave without buying, so remarketing gives you a second chance to bring them back. It works best with clear audience groups, a message that fits where each person left off, and frequency that does not annoy.

This guide covers how Google Ads remarketing works, where the ads show, the limits you must respect, and how to measure it.

## How Google Ads remarketing works

Google builds audience lists, called your data segments, from people who interacted with you:

- **Website visitors,** recorded by the Google tag, such as everyone who viewed a service page or started checkout
- **YouTube viewers** who watched your videos or visited your channel
- **App users,** if you have an app
- **Customer lists** you upload, known as Customer Match, where your account is eligible

Lists need enough people before they can be used, so very small sites may need time to build them.

## Where remarketing ads show

- **Display:** image ads on websites and apps
- **YouTube and Demand Gen:** video and image ads. See [Demand Gen campaigns](/learn/demand-gen-campaigns/)
- **Search:** remarketing lists for search ads, which let you bid differently or show different ads when past visitors search again
- **Performance Max:** your lists act as signals that guide who the campaign reaches

## Audience groups that work

- **Cart or form abandoners:** closest to buying, worth the strongest message
- **Service or product page visitors:** interested but undecided
- **Past customers:** for repeat purchases, refills or new services
- **Exclude converters** from campaigns meant to win new customers, so you do not pay to reach people who already bought

## Write the right message

Match the ad to where the person left off. Someone who abandoned a quote form needs reassurance, such as reviews, guarantees or a quick call. Someone who bought last year might need a reminder or a new offer. Avoid generic ads that ignore their visit.

## Rules you must respect

- **Sensitive categories.** Google restricts personalised advertising for some areas, such as health conditions, so remarketing may not be allowed for certain services. [What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) covers health rules
- **Consent.** In the UK, the EEA and elsewhere, visitors must consent before their data is used for advertising, and Google requires consent signals for those users
- **Privacy policy.** Your website must explain that you use remarketing
- **Frequency.** Showing the same ad too often wastes budget and irritates people. Set frequency limits where the campaign allows

## How to measure Google Ads remarketing

Remarketing often looks very efficient because it reaches people who were already likely to buy. Check:

- Conversions from remarketing compared with the rest of the account
- Whether total leads or sales rise, not just remarketing's share
- View-through conversions, treated with care, since some of those people would have returned anyway

An [experiment](/learn/google-ads-experiments-ab-testing-guide/) that holds back part of the audience is the most reliable way to see how many extra customers remarketing brings.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Google Ads remarketing?

Google Ads remarketing shows ads to people who already interacted with your business, such as website visitors, YouTube viewers or customers, so you can bring them back to buy or enquire.

### What is the difference between remarketing and retargeting?

They mean the same thing. Google uses the term remarketing; retargeting is the common industry term.

### Do I need the Google tag for remarketing?

For website visitor lists, yes. The Google tag records visits so audience lists can be built. Customer lists and YouTube audiences use other data sources.

### Can every business use Google Ads remarketing?

No. Google restricts personalised advertising for some sensitive categories, such as health conditions, and visitors in many regions must give consent before their data is used.

### Is remarketing worth it for a small budget?

Often, because past visitors are more likely to convert. Keep audiences focused, limit frequency, and check whether it increases total leads or sales rather than taking credit for people who would have returned anyway.
