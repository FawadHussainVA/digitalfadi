Traffic is arriving. The budget is being spent. Nothing is happening at the other end. It is the most common thing we are asked about, and the answer is almost never the one people expect.

Before changing bids or rewriting adverts, work through these in order. The order matters, because each one makes the next one readable.

## Are conversions actually being counted?

Start here. Not because it is likely to be the whole answer, but because every other diagnosis depends on it.

Plenty of accounts are converting fine and reporting nothing. A tag that never fired, a thank-you page that was redesigned and lost its snippet, a form plugin that was updated, a GA4 import that silently stopped. The business sees enquiries in the inbox and zero in Google Ads.

Check it the simple way. Submit your own form, then look at the conversion in Google Ads an hour later. If it is not there, nothing else on this list matters yet.

The opposite problem exists too. Conversions counted twice, or a page view counted as a conversion, so the account looks like it is working while the phone stays quiet. There is more on that in [what we look for first in an existing account](/#findings).

## Is the search intent wrong?

Look at the search terms report, not the keyword list. They are different things, and the gap between them is where most budgets leak.

You might be bidding on "accounting software" and paying for "free accounting software", "accounting software tutorial" and "accounting software jobs". All three are people who will never buy. They click, they bounce, and the account reports a terrible conversion rate for a keyword that was never the problem.

Pull the search terms report as far back as it goes. Read it properly, not the top twenty. Most accounts have whole categories of traffic nobody chose to pay for.

## Does the landing page answer the search?

If somebody searches "emergency plumber Dallas" and lands on your homepage, they have to find the service, confirm you cover Dallas, and work out whether you are open. Most will not bother.

The test is simple. Click your own advert, then ask whether the first screen answers the exact search that produced it. Same words, same service, same city. If the page talks about the company rather than the thing they searched for, the problem is the page.

This is also why high bounce rates on paid traffic are rarely a traffic problem. The traffic was fine. It arrived somewhere that did not answer it.

## Is the offer competitive?

Google Ads puts you next to your competitors in the most direct comparison a buyer ever sees. If three results offer free quotes, next-day availability and visible pricing, and yours offers a contact form, the clicks will still come and the conversions will not.

Nothing in the account fixes this. We can reduce what you pay for each click, but we cannot make a weaker offer win.

Open an incognito window and search your own main keyword. Read the three adverts above and below yours as a buyer would. That is the honest version of a competitor analysis.

## Is the form asking too much?

Every field is a reason to leave. A quote form asking for company size, industry, job title and budget before anybody has agreed to talk will collect far fewer leads than one asking for name, email and what they need.

Qualify after the enquiry, not before it. The information you wanted is available in the first reply, and by then you have their details.

Phone numbers deserve a mention. For services people buy urgently, a visible number converts better than any form, and call tracking makes it measurable.

## Is anybody answering?

This sounds obvious and it is the problem more often than anybody admits.

Enquiries arriving at an address nobody checks. Calls at six in the evening going to voicemail while adverts run until midnight. Form notifications in a spam folder. We have seen accounts with months of leads in a CRM nobody opened.

Before blaming the campaign, check what happens to an enquiry after it is submitted. Send a test through yourself and see how long it takes somebody to respond.

## The order to work through it

1. Verify conversion tracking end to end
2. Read the search terms report in full
3. Click your own advert and judge the landing page
4. Compare your offer against the adverts beside yours
5. Cut every form field that is not essential
6. Test what happens to an enquiry after it arrives

Most accounts have a problem in the first three. Almost none have a bidding problem, which is where most people start.
