Prepare Google Ads for Black Friday in the weeks before the sale, not on the day. Get your product feed and Merchant Center promotions approved early, raise budgets so profitable campaigns are not capped at the busiest moment, use a seasonality adjustment only for the few days when conversion rates jump sharply, and write sale-specific ads in advance. After the sale, let bidding return to normal and judge the event on profit, not revenue.

Most Black Friday problems in Google Ads are timing problems: disapproved products, promotions still under review, or campaigns that run out of budget by lunchtime on the biggest day of the year.

## Two to three weeks before: the product feed and promotions

### Check Merchant Center first

Open the product diagnostics in Merchant Center and fix anything disapproved or limited. A product that is disapproved on Black Friday cannot show at all, however good the offer.

- **Prices and availability** must match the website exactly. Sale prices that change on the day are the most common cause of last-minute disapprovals
- **Use the sale price attribute,** with start and end dates, rather than changing the main price, so the discount can display correctly in Shopping ads
- **Stock:** products likely to sell out should be marked correctly, or they waste clicks once gone

[Google Shopping feed optimisation](/learn/google-shopping-feed-optimisation/) covers the full feed checklist.

### Submit promotions early

Merchant Center promotions add an offer label, such as a percentage off or free delivery, to your Shopping ads. They are reviewed before they run, so submit them well before the sale starts and check they are approved. Add matching promotion assets to your Search campaigns as well, so text ads show the offer too.

## One to two weeks before: budgets and bidding

### Raise budgets on profitable campaigns

Search volume and conversion rates rise sharply around Black Friday and Cyber Monday. A daily budget that is fine in October can run out early on the busiest days, which means your ads stop showing exactly when buyers are ready.

- Increase budgets on campaigns that are already profitable, not on everything
- Check which campaigns already show "Limited by budget" and fix those first
- Remember Google can spend up to twice the average daily budget on a busy day, so set budgets with that in mind. [How much should you spend on Google Ads?](/learn/how-much-should-you-spend-on-google-ads/) explains how daily budgets work across a month

Budget changes do not reset the learning period of automated bidding, so adjust when the business needs it. [Does changing your budget reset learning?](/learn/does-changing-budget-reset-google-ads-learning/) explains why.

### Use a seasonality adjustment, carefully

A seasonality adjustment tells Smart Bidding to expect a higher conversion rate for a set period, so it bids for that in advance rather than learning it after the fact. Google's guidance is clear on when to use it:

- **Only for short events,** ideally 1 to 7 days, such as the Black Friday to Cyber Monday weekend
- **Only for big changes.** Smart Bidding already allows for normal seasonal patterns
- **Not for long periods.** Adjustments may not work as well beyond 14 days
- **No reverse adjustment afterwards.** Bidding returns to normal on its own when the event ends

Base the size of the adjustment on last year's actual conversion rate during the same sale, not on a guess. You set it up under Tools, then Budgets and bidding, then Adjustments.

### Check targets

If you use target ROAS or target CPA, consider whether the targets still make sense during a sale. Discounts lower your margin, so the return you need may be higher than usual. The [break-even ROAS calculator](/tools/break-even-roas-calculator/) shows the minimum ROAS at your sale prices.

## The week before: ads and landing pages

### Write sale-specific ads in advance

- Headlines that state the offer clearly: the discount, free delivery, the end date
- A countdown in the ad text, using ad customizers, to show how long the sale has left
- Separate ads or assets you can switch on and off, rather than rewriting ads on the day, which sends them back through review

[How to write Google Ads headlines that get shown](/learn/how-to-write-google-ads-headlines/) covers headline structure.

### Prepare landing pages

- The sale price, stock levels and delivery cut-off dates must match the ads
- Pages must load quickly on mobile, where most holiday browsing happens
- Test checkout and confirm conversion tracking still fires after any website changes made for the sale

## During the sale

- **Watch budgets daily,** and raise them on campaigns that are profitable and limited by budget
- **Read the search terms report,** because sale periods attract bargain hunters and comparison searches
- **Protect your brand.** Competitors often bid on brand names during sales, so keep a brand campaign running. [Should you bid on your own brand name?](/learn/should-you-bid-on-your-own-brand-name/) explains why
- **Do not rebuild anything.** Big structural changes in the middle of the busiest week make results impossible to read

## After the sale

1. **Remove sale ads and promotions** when the offer ends, so ads never promise a discount that no longer exists
2. **Let bidding return to normal.** A seasonality adjustment ends by itself, with no reverse adjustment needed
3. **Judge on profit, not revenue.** A record revenue weekend can still lose money once discounts, delivery and returns are counted
4. **Record what happened:** conversion rates, cost per sale and which products sold, so next year's adjustment uses real numbers
5. **Plan for returns.** Sales that are refunded later inflate the results you saw on the day

## For service businesses

Black Friday matters for some service businesses too, but often differently: demand may dip during holiday weeks. Check your ad schedule matches the hours you can answer enquiries over the holidays, and do not raise budgets on the assumption that the whole market is busier.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I start preparing Google Ads for Black Friday?

Two to three weeks before the sale for the product feed and promotions, which need approval. Budgets, ads and seasonality adjustments can be set up in the final one to two weeks.

### Should I use a seasonality adjustment for Black Friday?

Only if you expect a big change in conversion rate for a short period, ideally 1 to 7 days, and you have last year's figures to base it on. Smart Bidding already allows for normal seasonal patterns.

### Should I increase my Google Ads budget for Black Friday?

On campaigns that are profitable and likely to be limited by budget, yes. Raising budgets everywhere simply spends more on campaigns that were not profitable before the sale.

### Do I need new campaigns for Black Friday?

Usually not. Sale-specific ads, promotions and assets inside your existing campaigns keep their history and bidding data. New campaigns start with no data in the busiest week of the year.

### What should I do after Black Friday?

Remove sale ads and promotions, let bidding return to normal, and judge the event on profit after discounts and returns rather than revenue alone.
