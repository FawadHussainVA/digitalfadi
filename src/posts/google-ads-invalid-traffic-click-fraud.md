Click fraud in Google Ads is part of what Google calls invalid traffic: clicks and interactions that do not represent genuine interest, such as bots or accidental clicks. Google filters most invalid traffic before you are billed, and you are not charged for it.

Some is found later, and Google credits it back. A new Invalid Traffic card on the Overview page now shows both figures in money terms, so you can see how much protection and how many credits your account received.

## What counts as invalid traffic

- Automated clicks from bots and scripts
- Accidental and repeated clicks
- Clicks intended to drain a competitor's budget
- Other activity that does not represent real interest in your business

## How Google handles it

1. **Before billing.** Google catches most invalid traffic shortly after the click and filters it out. You are not charged for it
2. **After billing.** Some patterns take longer to find. When Google detects them later, it issues credits, which appear as "Invalid activity" under Adjustments in the monthly billing view
3. **Campaign metrics do not change** for past credits, so the credit appears in billing rather than in your campaign reports

## The new Invalid Traffic card

Google began rolling out the Invalid Traffic Overview Card in November 2026. You find it on the Overview page, under the Campaigns tab, and it is rolling out gradually, so not every account has it yet. It shows:

- **Automated protection:** the estimated amount saved by filtering invalid traffic before billing
- **Past invalid activity:** credits issued for invalid activity found after billing

It is a useful transparency tool, but it only shows what Google's systems identified. It cannot show invalid traffic that went undetected.

## Signs that click fraud may be affecting you

- Clicks rise sharply with no matching rise in leads, calls or sales
- Many clicks from one location, time window or placement
- Form submissions with fake details
- Very short visits from paid traffic

Many of these have ordinary causes too, such as broad targeting or a landing page problem, so check those first. [Why are my Google Ads not converting?](/learn/why-are-my-google-ads-not-converting/) covers the common ones.

## What you can do about click fraud in Google Ads

- **Request an investigation** if you suspect invalid traffic that was not filtered. Google accepts investigation requests for activity within the last 60 days
- **Tighten targeting:** location settings set to people in your area, and sensible ad schedules. [The location setting that wastes budget](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) explains the most common leak
- **Review placements** in Display, Demand Gen and Performance Max, and exclude poor ones
- **Protect forms** with spam filtering, so fake submissions do not count as conversions and mislead bidding
- **Track real outcomes,** so automated bidding learns from genuine leads rather than any form fill

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I pay for click fraud in Google Ads?

You are not charged for invalid traffic that Google detects before billing. If it is found later, Google credits it back, shown as "Invalid activity" under Adjustments in your monthly billing view.

### What is the Google Ads Invalid Traffic card?

It is a card on the Overview page, under the Campaigns tab, that shows the estimated savings from invalid traffic filtered before billing and the credits issued for invalid activity found afterwards. It is rolling out gradually.

### How do I report click fraud to Google?

Submit an invalid traffic investigation request from Google Ads. Google accepts requests for activity within the last 60 days.

### Can I block click fraud completely?

No system can block everything. Google filters most invalid traffic, and you can reduce exposure with tight targeting, placement exclusions, form spam protection and tracking that counts real outcomes.

### Why do my clicks not match my website visits?

Invalid clicks filtered by Google, people leaving before the page loads, tracking issues and consent settings can all cause differences. Investigate tracking first before assuming fraud.
