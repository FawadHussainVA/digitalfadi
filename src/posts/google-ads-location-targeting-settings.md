Google Ads location targeting decides where the people who see your ads can be. The setting that matters most is easy to miss: by default, Google shows ads to people in your target area and also to people elsewhere who have shown interest in it. For most local businesses that default wastes money, and the fix is to switch the location option to Presence, so ads only show to people who are in, or regularly in, the places you serve.

## The two location options

In each campaign's settings, under Locations, there is a section called Location options. It offers two choices:

1. **Presence or interest** (the default): people in, regularly in, or who have shown interest in your targeted locations
2. **Presence:** people in or regularly in your targeted locations

The difference is the word interest. Someone in another city who searches for a plumber in your town, reads about your area or plans a trip there can count as interested, even though they are nowhere near it.

## Why the default costs local businesses money

Picture a plumber who targets one town. With Presence or interest, the ads can also show to:

- someone researching the town before moving there next year
- a person in another region searching for prices in different places
- a landlord who lives elsewhere and owns property in the area

Some of these could become customers, but most will not, and each click costs the same as a local customer with a leaking pipe. For any business that serves people in person, such as [trades](/industries/), [dental clinics](/google-ads-for-dentists/), restaurants or local shops, Presence is almost always the better choice. [Why your Google Ads calls are not becoming jobs](/learn/why-google-ads-calls-are-not-becoming-jobs/) shows how often out-of-area calls turn up in local accounts.

## When Presence or interest makes sense

The default is not always wrong. It suits businesses whose customers are often somewhere else when they search:

- hotels, holiday lets and attractions, where people search from home before they travel
- removal companies and estate agents serving people who are moving into the area
- businesses that ship nationally but target specific regions for other reasons

If people genuinely buy from you before arriving, interest is useful. If they need to be there already, it is not.

## Excluding locations

You can also exclude places, such as a neighbouring city you do not cover or a country you cannot ship to. Exclusions have their own option, and the sensible default is to exclude people who are in the excluded location. Excluding a large area you serve part of is a common mistake, so check exclusions against your real service area.

Exclusions and negative keywords work together. A location exclusion stops ads showing to people physically in that place. A negative keyword for a town name stops ads showing for searches that mention it, wherever the searcher is. Local accounts often need both. The [negative keyword generator](/tools/negative-keyword-generator/) has a field for places you do not serve.

## Choosing the area itself

Google Ads lets you target by country, region, city, postcode or a radius around an address.

- **Radius** is quick and suits businesses that travel a set distance from one base
- **Postcodes or cities** suit businesses whose real service area follows roads, rivers or travel time rather than a circle
- **Separate campaigns for core and outer areas** let you spend more where jobs are most profitable and less where travel eats the margin

Draw the area around where you actually want work, not as wide as you are willing to go.

## Bid adjustments by location

With manual bidding you can raise or lower bids for specific locations, for example bidding more close to base and less on the edges. With automated bidding strategies, most of these adjustments are ignored, because the bidding already takes location into account for each search. If you rely on automated bidding, use separate campaigns or tighter targeting instead.

## How to check where your clicks come from

Do not assume the setting is right. Check what actually happened:

1. In Google Ads, open Insights and reports, then the Locations report (under When and where ads showed)
2. Switch the view to see user locations, meaning where people physically were, rather than only the locations you targeted
3. Look for clicks and spend from places you do not serve

If a meaningful share of spend comes from outside your area, the location option, the exclusions or the area itself needs changing. This is one of the first checks in a [Google Ads audit](/google-ads-audit/), because it is often money that can be saved the same day.

## Performance Max and other campaign types

The same Presence or interest choice exists in Performance Max and other campaign types, and it defaults the same way. Check it in every campaign, not just Search.

Local Services Ads work differently: they use the service area set in their own profile, not campaign location options. [Local Services Ads vs Google Ads](/learn/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads/) explains the difference.

## Location targeting is not perfect

Google works out location from signals such as device location, IP address and searches. Most of the time it is accurate, but some clicks will always come from slightly outside the area. A small amount of spill is normal. A steady stream of distant clicks is a setting problem.

## Quick checklist

1. Location option set to **Presence** for any business that serves people in person
2. Exclusions match the places you genuinely do not serve
3. Target area drawn around where you want work, not as wide as possible
4. Town and city names outside the area added as negative keywords
5. The user locations report checked monthly for spend from outside the area
