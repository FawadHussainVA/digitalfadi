To hire a Google Ads expert for account optimisation, decide what "better" means for your business first, then shortlist a few specialists, ask each for a first look at your account, and hire the one whose diagnosis is the most specific about your tracking, your search terms and your margins. Give access through Google Ads' own user permissions rather than sharing a password, and agree how results will be judged before any changes are made.

The step most people skip is the test. A short first look at your real account tells you more about an expert than any sales call, portfolio or review.

## What account optimisation actually involves

Optimisation is not one task. A good expert will usually work through these, in roughly this order:

1. **Conversion tracking.** Checking that what the account counts as a conversion is a real enquiry or sale, counted once
2. **Search terms and negatives.** Finding the searches that spend money without converting and excluding them
3. **Account structure.** Grouping keywords and campaigns so budgets, bids and ads match what people search for
4. **Bidding and budgets.** Choosing a bid strategy that suits your data, and putting budget where it earns most
5. **Ads and landing pages.** Testing headlines, offers and pages that turn clicks into enquiries
6. **Feeds and assets,** for online shops and Performance Max

If someone offers to "optimise" your account without mentioning tracking first, they are starting in the middle. Every later decision depends on the conversion data being right. [What a Google Ads audit should actually check](/learn/what-a-google-ads-audit-should-check/) explains why.

## Step 1: decide what "better" means

Before you contact anyone, write down in one or two sentences what you want the account to do. For example:

- "More enquiries at under 60 each, in the areas we cover"
- "Keep sales the same, but spend 20% less"
- "Profitable Shopping campaigns at a return on ad spend above our break-even"

Give a number wherever you can. An expert can only optimise towards a target they know. If you are not sure what a lead or sale is worth to you, the [metrics calculator](/tools/metrics-calculator/) and [break-even ROAS calculator](/tools/break-even-roas-calculator/) help work it out.

## Step 2: gather what an expert will ask for

Having this ready makes every conversation faster and more useful:

- Your Google Ads customer ID, the 10-digit number at the top of the account
- Monthly ad spend over the last few months
- Roughly how many enquiries or sales the ads produce, from your own records rather than the Google Ads report
- What a typical customer is worth, and your margin if you sell products
- Anything that has already been tried, and what happened

## Step 3: shortlist two or three experts

Look for people who specialise in Google Ads rather than offering it as one of many services. Check that their experience fits your business type: lead generation, local services and online shops are run quite differently.

For where to look and what to pay, see [where to find affordable Google Ads service providers](/learn/where-to-find-affordable-google-ads-services/).

## Step 4: test them on your real account

Ask each shortlisted expert to look at your account before you commit. Some offer a free first look, others charge a small fixed fee for a written review. Either way, this is the most revealing step.

Read what comes back and ask yourself:

- **Did they check tracking first?** A strong expert questions whether the conversions are real before judging anything else
- **Is it specific?** "Your bids are too high" is generic. "These 14 search terms spent 22% of the budget with no enquiries" is specific
- **Did they ask about your business?** Margins, sales process, which jobs or products matter most
- **Do they explain the order of work?** What they would fix first, and why
- **Are they honest about uncertainty?** Nobody can promise results before seeing data over time

The expert with the most impressive presentation is not always the one with the best diagnosis. Hire on the diagnosis.

## Step 5: give access safely

Never share your Google account password. Google Ads has built-in user access for exactly this:

- **Read only** access is enough for a review or audit. Nothing can be changed
- **Standard** access lets an expert make changes to campaigns, which is what ongoing optimisation needs
- **Admin** access should stay with you, the owner of the business

Experts who manage several accounts often ask you to link your account to their manager account. That is normal. What matters is that the account itself stays registered to your business and billed to your own payment method, so the history and data stay with you if you part ways.

## Step 6: agree the first 90 days

Before any changes are made, agree in writing:

1. **The main measure of success,** such as cost per enquiry or return on ad spend, and how it will be checked against your own records
2. **What happens first.** Usually tracking and obvious waste, before any restructuring
3. **How often you will hear from them,** and in what format
4. **When you will judge progress.** Most accounts need several weeks of data before results are a fair test. [How long does it take for Google Ads to work?](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/) explains why

## Signs you hired well

Within the first month or two, a good expert will typically:

- Tell you clearly what they changed and why
- Show you search terms they excluded and the money that was going on them
- Fix or confirm conversion tracking, and tell you if earlier numbers were wrong
- Report brand and non-brand results separately, so easy wins do not hide weak ones
- Ask you about lead quality, not just lead volume

If the reports show only clicks, impressions and click-through rate, ask how those connect to enquiries or sales. [How to tell if your Google Ads agency is doing anything](/learn/is-your-google-ads-agency-doing-anything/) has more checks, and they apply to any expert.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I look for when hiring a Google Ads expert?

Specialisation in Google Ads, experience with businesses like yours, a habit of checking conversion tracking before anything else, and a specific diagnosis of your account rather than a general pitch. Partner status and client reviews are useful supporting evidence.

### Should I give a Google Ads expert admin access?

Usually not. Read only access is enough for an audit, and standard access is enough to manage campaigns. Keep admin access with yourself as the business owner.

### How long does Google Ads optimisation take?

Quick fixes such as excluding wasteful searches can help within days. Structural changes and bidding improvements usually need several weeks of data before you can judge them fairly.

### Can I hire a Google Ads expert just for a one-off optimisation?

Yes. Many specialists offer a one-off audit or optimisation project. It suits accounts that need fixing once and can then be run in-house, though most accounts drift again without ongoing attention.

### How do I know if the optimisation worked?

Compare your main measure, such as cost per enquiry or return on ad spend, against your own sales records over a similar period before and after the changes. Judge on enquiries and sales, not on clicks.
