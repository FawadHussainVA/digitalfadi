In the first 90 days of Google Ads management, the first month should be spent on foundations: understanding your business, checking tracking and fixing the account structure. The second month is a staged launch and early testing; by the third month, decisions should be based on reliable data, with clear reporting on leads or sales and what changes next.

If a manager promises dramatic results in week one, ask what they will check first. Good management starts with measurement, not with spending more.

## Before day one: what you should prepare

- **Google Ads access,** read-only to begin with if you prefer
- **Analytics access,** such as GA4
- **Your numbers:** average order or customer value, and rough margin
- **Your sales process:** what happens after a lead comes in, and who handles it
- **Assets:** images, video, product feed and brand guidelines where you have them
- **The goal:** the one number that would make this a success

Having these ready makes the first week much faster.

## Days 1 to 30: foundations

**Discovery.** The manager learns your offer, margins, sales process and what a customer is worth, then agrees the single measure the account will be judged on, such as cost per lead, cost per booked job or return on ad spend.

**Account analysis.** A full review of the existing account: wasted spend, structure, tracking accuracy and opportunities already sitting in the search terms. [What a Google Ads audit should check](/learn/what-a-google-ads-audit-should-check/) shows what this covers.

**Tracking first.** Conversion tracking is checked end to end and fixed before anything else, because every later decision depends on it.

**Structure and assets.** Campaigns are restructured or rebuilt where needed, exclusions and negatives added, and ad copy and assets prepared.

What you should see: a clear list of what was found, what is being fixed, and in what order.

## Days 31 to 60: staged launch and first tests

**Changes go live in stages,** not all at once, so early data stays readable and automated bidding is not pushed back into its learning period unnecessarily. [Does changing your budget reset learning?](/learn/does-changing-budget-reset-google-ads-learning/) explains why this matters.

**Weekly work begins:** search term reviews, negative keywords, budget checks and the first ad tests.

What you should see: a weekly note on what changed, wasted spend falling, and lead or sale numbers you can match to your own records.

## Days 61 to 90: decisions based on data

**Testing continues** on ads, landing pages and bidding, one change at a time, each documented.

**Budgets move** towards the campaigns producing leads or sales at the best cost, and away from the rest.

**A monthly review** looks at results against the agreed measure, what worked, what did not, and the plan for the next quarter. Budget increases happen only where the numbers support them.

What you should see: a clear trend in your main measure, an account you understand, and a plan for what comes next.

## Realistic expectations

- **Fast wins** often come from stopping wasted spend on irrelevant searches and fixing tracking
- **Bidding improvements** take longer, because automated bidding needs weeks of reliable data
- **High-value or long sales cycles,** such as implants, legal cases or property, take longer to judge
- **Seasonal businesses** need comparisons with the same period last year, not last month

[How long does it take for Google Ads to work?](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/) covers timelines in more detail.

## Warning signs in the first 90 days

- No questions about tracking, margins or your sales process
- Everything changed in the first week, with no record of what or why
- Reports showing clicks and impressions, but not leads, sales or cost per result
- You cannot see the changes in your own account
- Requests to increase budget before tracking is confirmed

## Frequently asked questions

### What should happen in the first month of Google Ads management?

Discovery, a full account analysis, conversion tracking checked and fixed, and the account structure and assets prepared. Major changes should go live in stages, not all at once.

### When should I see results from a new Google Ads manager?

Wasted spend can often fall within the first weeks. Clear improvements in cost per lead or sale usually take one to three months, depending on budget, tracking and how long your customers take to decide.

### Why does good Google Ads management start with tracking?

Because bids, budgets and tests all depend on knowing which clicks became leads or sales. Changing campaigns before fixing tracking means optimising towards the wrong numbers.

### What reports should I get in the first 90 days?

A short weekly note on what changed and a monthly review against the agreed measure, such as cost per lead or return on ad spend, with the plan for the next month.

### What should I do if nothing improves after 90 days?

Ask for the evidence: what was found, what changed and what the data shows. If the explanation is unclear, an independent audit can show whether the account is being worked on properly.
