**If you use images or videos in Google Ads that were created or edited with AI, Google gives you a setting to label them, and in some regions that label now appears on the ad itself.** Campaigns targeting the European Union, India, New York, California, South Korea or Vietnam show a visible AI overlay on ads that use assets you mark as AI created or edited. Everywhere else, the disclosure appears only when someone opens "How this ad was made" in the ad's information panel. Google may label assets made with its own AI tools for you, but assets you made elsewhere are your responsibility. Google also says the setting on its own does not guarantee you meet local law.

## Why this is in the news

In early November 2026, [Search Engine Roundtable reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-expands-ai-content-label-disclosure-42461.html) that Google had added California, South Korea and Vietnam to the list of places where AI labels show on the ad. The list already included the European Union, India and New York. Google updated both its Google Ads help page on [AI content label settings and disclosures](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17140115?hl=en) and the Merchant Center product data specification, so this reaches product images in Shopping feeds too.

The list has now grown since the setting launched in mid 2026, and it is reasonable to expect more places to join it.

## What the label actually does

The label works on two levels.

1. **A disclosure everywhere.** Any ad using an asset you mark as AI created or edited carries a note in the "How this ad was made" section of the ad's My Ad Center panel. This applies globally, but people only see it if they open the panel
2. **A visible overlay in listed regions.** For campaigns targeting the regions above, the ad itself shows an AI label on top of the creative

So the setting does not just tick a compliance box in the background. In the listed regions, the people you are advertising to will see it.

## Which ads it affects

The setting is built for **image and video assets**. That means it matters most in the campaign types that use them: Performance Max, Demand Gen, Display and YouTube, plus product images in Merchant Center. Text-only Search ads are much less affected.

Two sources of AI creative need different handling:

- **Google's own AI tools.** When you use Google's fully automated features to create and serve creatives, Google says assets may be labelled as AI created or edited on your behalf. Google also adds machine-readable metadata to images and videos made with its AI tools
- **Assets you bring in.** An image generated or heavily edited in another tool is up to you to label. Google cannot always tell where it came from

If you let Performance Max generate images or enhance your videos, check which of your assets already carry a label. [Performance Max: what it hides and how to see it](/learn/performance-max-what-it-hides/) covers how to find out what the campaign is actually serving.

## Does every AI-touched asset need a label?

Not necessarily. Google's wording refers to **certain** AI-generated or edited assets, and the rules behind it are local laws that differ in what they cover. Whether a given image needs a label depends on what was done to it, how realistic the result is, and where you are advertising.

That is a legal question, and Google says so itself: using the label setting does not guarantee compliance with local regulations, and it advises getting legal guidance where needed. The practical rule is simple. If an asset was generated by AI, or AI changed what it shows rather than just tidying it up, label it, and take advice if you are unsure.

Merchant Center adds one more consideration: Google notes that labelling might affect whether content appears in some places. That is a reason to make an honest choice per asset, not a reason to avoid labelling.

## What this means for UK advertisers

The UK is not on Google's list. A campaign targeting only the UK will not show the visible overlay under the current list, though the "How this ad was made" disclosure still applies to labelled assets wherever the ad runs.

The catch is targeting. If a campaign also targets Ireland or any other EU country, it falls inside the European Union entry on the list. Check where each campaign that uses AI creative is actually set to show. [Google Ads location targeting](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) explains how to see and tighten this.

## How to label assets in Google Ads

Google prompts you in a few places:

1. **When you add assets.** If an asset in a campaign may need a label, you see a **Review assets** prompt with a table and an empty "AI label" column
2. **In the asset library.** Existing assets have an AI label column. Select the asset, choose **Manage AI label**, then pick **Label this asset as created or edited with AI** or **Don't label this asset**
3. **In bulk.** Select several assets in the asset table and change the AI label for all of them at once
4. **In Google Ads Editor.** The AI label column appears in the data view, with the same two options
5. **In Merchant Center.** For product images, open the image in Manage images and use **Manage AI label** from its menu

You can also put your own visible label on a creative in your design tool before uploading it.

## A practical checklist

- **List every AI-made or AI-edited image and video** in your account and your product feed, including assets Google generated for you
- **Label each one honestly** and record why you did or did not label borderline cases
- **Check the targeting** of every campaign that uses them against the region list, and recheck when Google adds regions
- **Watch performance after labelling** in the listed regions. If a labelled asset falls away, test it against one made without AI rather than removing the label
- **Ask your agency or freelancer** whether they have used AI tools on your creative. If someone else made the assets, they are the one who knows

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to label AI images in Google Ads?

Laws in some places require ads that use certain AI-created or edited assets to say so. Google provides the AI label setting to help. Whether your specific asset needs a label depends on the content and where you advertise, and Google recommends legal advice where you are unsure.

### Where does the AI label show on the ad itself?

On campaigns targeting the European Union, India, New York, California, South Korea and Vietnam, according to Google's help documentation as of November 2026. Elsewhere, the disclosure appears in the "How this ad was made" section of the ad's information panel.

### Does Google label AI assets automatically?

Sometimes. When you use Google's fully automated features to create creatives, assets may be labelled as AI created or edited for you. Assets made in other tools need to be labelled by you.

### Does this apply to UK advertisers?

The UK is not on the list for visible labels, so UK-only campaigns do not show the overlay. Campaigns that also target EU countries, such as Ireland, do. The disclosure in the ad information panel applies to labelled assets everywhere.

### Will an AI label hurt my ad performance?

Google has not published figures on this, and it will vary. Watch labelled assets in the listed regions and compare them with assets made without AI before making changes.
