Cosmetic dentistry Google Ads work best with a separate campaign or ad group for each treatment, because people searching for clear aligners, veneers, whitening or bonding want different things and spend very different amounts. Lead with a consultation rather than a price, show real work and real credentials, be careful with brand names and claims, and measure consultations that become accepted treatment. Cosmetic patients compare carefully and decide slowly, so trust matters more than the cheapest headline.

For the wider dental approach, see [Google Ads for dental clinics](/google-ads-for-dentists/).

## Separate each treatment

Cosmetic searches are specific, and each treatment has its own price, decision time and audience:

- **Teeth whitening:** a lower price point, faster decisions, and strong competition from at-home kits
- **Composite bonding:** a mid-range treatment that many people discover through social media
- **Veneers:** a high-value treatment with long research and careful comparison
- **Clear aligners:** high value, long treatment, often compared against at-home aligner brands
- **Smile makeovers:** combined treatments, usually the highest value and the longest decision

Give each its own ad group and landing page at least, and separate campaigns for the high-value treatments so budget can follow the work you want. A single "cosmetic dentistry" page for every search converts poorly.

## Be careful with brand names

Many clear aligner and treatment names are trademarks. Using them in ad text may be restricted by Google's trademark policy, depending on the brand and whether you are an authorised provider. Before using a brand name:

- Check whether you are listed as an authorised provider, and what the brand allows
- Check whether your ads are disapproved for trademark reasons
- Use generic terms, such as clear aligners or invisible braces, where you are unsure

You can often still bid on brand searches as keywords, but the rules for ad text and keywords are different, so check the current policy for your account.

## Follow health advertising rules

Cosmetic dentistry is still healthcare, and Google's healthcare policies apply, along with the rules of your country and your dental regulator.

- **No guaranteed results.** Every patient is different, and claims such as "perfect smile in days" are risky
- **Describe treatments accurately,** including that suitability is decided at a consultation
- **Before-and-after images:** use only your own patients, with consent, and only where your regulator and Google's policies allow them in ads and on landing pages
- **Reviews and testimonials:** use real ones, and check what your regulator allows in advertising

These rules shape the copy, but they also help. Patients trust calm, specific information more than bold promises.

## Lead with consultations, explain prices on the page

Cosmetic patients want to know the cost, but the right price depends on an assessment. Most practices do best by:

- **Offering a consultation** as the main call to action in the ad
- **Giving a realistic starting price or range** on the landing page, where you can stand behind it
- **Explaining finance,** if you offer it, including that approval is required
- **Answering the common questions:** how long treatment takes, how long results last, maintenance and aftercare

For aligners in particular, explain how in-clinic treatment differs from at-home kits. That answers the comparison many searchers are already making.

## Build trust on the landing page

- Photographs of your own work and team, where allowed
- The clinician's qualifications and experience, stated accurately
- A clear explanation of the process from consultation to result
- Several ways to start: call, online booking, consultation request

## Where cosmetic budgets leak

- **At-home products:** whitening kits, strips, at-home aligners, unless you offer them
- **DIY and information searches:** how to whiten teeth at home, natural whitening
- **Treatments you do not offer**
- **Training searches:** cosmetic dentistry courses, aligner provider training

## Measure accepted treatment

Like implants, cosmetic treatment is a long, considered decision. Track every stage: enquiry, consultation booked, consultation attended and treatment accepted, with its value. Then judge each treatment's campaign on cost per accepted case. [How to track new patients from dental Google Ads](/learn/dental-google-ads-conversion-tracking/) covers the setup.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should cosmetic dentistry ads show prices?

Usually the ad should offer a consultation, with a realistic starting price or range explained on the landing page. Advertise a price only if most patients pay close to it.

### Can I use clear aligner brand names in Google Ads?

It depends on the brand, whether you are an authorised provider and Google's trademark policy. Check before using a brand name in ad text, and use generic terms where you are unsure.

### Are before-and-after photos allowed in dental ads?

Rules vary by country, regulator and Google policy. Use only your own patients' photos with their consent, and check what is allowed in ads and on landing pages before using them.

### Which cosmetic treatment should I advertise first?

The one with the best combination of demand in your area, profit per case and capacity in your diary. Many practices start with one high-value treatment, such as aligners or veneers, and add others once tracking shows what works.

### How do cosmetic dentistry ads compete with at-home kits?

By explaining what in-clinic treatment offers that a kit cannot, such as assessment, supervision and aftercare, and by blocking product searches that come from people who only want a kit.
