Google Ads usually starts showing your ads within a day of launch, once the ads have passed review and billing is set up. Most ads are reviewed within one business day, though some take longer, and a new advertiser may need identity verification first, which can add days. If your ads are approved and running but nothing shows, the cause is usually budget, bids, keywords or targeting, not a delay in Google's systems.

This article is about getting your ads live. For how long results take to become reliable, see [how long it takes for Google Ads to work](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/).

## The steps before the first impression

Your ads go through several stages before anyone sees them:

1. **Account and billing.** A payment method must be added and the account set up to pay. Campaigns cannot serve without it
2. **Advertiser verification.** Google may ask you to confirm who you are and where you operate. This is more common for new accounts and can take a few days
3. **Ad review.** Each ad is checked against Google's policies. Most are reviewed within one business day. Ads that are disapproved, or are limited for a policy reason, will not show until fixed
4. **Eligibility.** The keywords, location and device settings must match real searches. An ad that is approved but targets a tiny audience may show very rarely
5. **Auction.** For each search, Google compares bids and ad quality. An ad only shows if it wins a place

So if nothing has shown, check the stage first. Look at the campaign status, the ad status and the policy column before changing anything else.

## Why ads show later than expected

**The campaign is paused, scheduled or not yet started.** Check the start date and the campaign status. Ads scheduled for next week will not show today.

**The budget is too small for the bids.** If the daily budget is spent on the first clicks, or the bid is too low to win the auction, impressions can stay near zero. Check the impression share and whether the campaign is limited by budget or rank.

**The keywords are too narrow or too rare.** Very specific phrases with little search volume may show a few times a week. This is normal, but it is worth checking that people really search for those terms in your area.

**The location setting is wrong.** A campaign targeting an area where few people search, or using the wrong location option, can show almost nothing. [The location setting that wastes budget](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) explains the options.

**An ad is disapproved or limited.** The ad status column shows the reason. A missing website, a claim that breaks policy or a landing page that does not match the ad can stop it. Fix the problem and the ad goes back into review.

**Automated bidding is new.** A new Smart Bidding strategy needs some conversion data before it bids confidently, so impressions can be lower at first. [Which bid strategy should you use?](/learn/which-google-ads-bid-strategy-should-you-use/) covers when to start with more manual control.

## What is normal in the first week

For a campaign that is approved, funded and eligible for real searches:

- Impressions usually appear within the first day
- Clicks follow once the ads win auctions consistently
- Conversions take longer, because people research before they enquire or buy, and some conversions are recorded days later

Judge the first week on whether ads are showing and clicks are arriving, not on the number of enquiries. Conversion results need a longer window, which the [how long it takes to work](/learn/how-long-does-google-ads-take-to-work/) article covers.

## A quick check when ads are not showing

1. Confirm billing is set up and the account is verified
2. Check the campaign and ad groups are enabled, with a start date that has passed
3. Look at the policy column for disapprovals and limits
4. Check the daily budget is not spent by midday, and the bid is not far below the first-page bid estimate
5. Confirm the location and language settings match where your customers are
6. Search for your own keywords in an incognito window, from the area you target, to see whether your ads appear

If all of these are fine and ads still do not show, an account review can find the cause quickly. The [Google Ads audit checklist](/learn/google-ads-audit-checklist/) lists the settings to check, and the [free Google Ads audit](/#contact) lists the issues in your account.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my Google Ads approved but not showing?

The usual reasons are a campaign that is paused or scheduled for later, a budget that is too small or spent early, a bid too low to win auctions, or a location setting that excludes your customers. Check the impression share and the campaign status first.

### Does Google Ads review take longer on a new account?

It can. New advertisers may need identity verification before ads run, and the first ads in a new account are often reviewed more carefully. Once the account has a good policy history, reviews are usually quicker.

### Can I speed up ad approval?

Make sure ads follow Google's policies, the website is live and matches the ad, and business details are complete. Avoid claims that cannot be supported. A disapproved ad can be fixed and resubmitted for review.

### Will my ads show on the first day?

Usually, if they are approved, billing is set up and the budget and bids allow them to win auctions. Expect a small number of impressions on day one, rising as Google learns which searches your ads suit.

### How long until I get my first enquiry?

It depends on your sales cycle and the search volume for your keywords. Many businesses see first enquiries in the first weeks, but some sell over longer periods. Track enquiries against the date they came in, so you can see the real delay for your business.
