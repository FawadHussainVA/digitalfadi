Google Ads works for therapy and mental health practices when it reaches people looking for ongoing support, and stays well away from searches made in a crisis. Target searches for therapists, counselling and specific therapy types, exclude crisis terms, and make sure every landing page shows where to get urgent help.

Advertise only where your clinicians are licensed, protect privacy carefully, and measure first sessions booked rather than clicks. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Searches to target

- **Practice searches:** "therapist near me", "counselling in [town]", "online therapy"
- **Therapy types:** CBT, EMDR, couples counselling, family therapy, psychiatric assessment
- **Concerns people want help with:** anxiety, depression, stress, grief, where your clinicians specialise
- **Practical searches:** therapists who accept certain insurance, evening appointments, online sessions

Each therapy type and specialism deserves its own ad group and landing page, because people often look for a specific approach or a clinician with specific experience.

## Searches to exclude

Some searches come from people in immediate crisis. A booking page for a future appointment is the wrong response to them, and paying for those clicks helps no one. Add negative keywords for crisis and emergency language, including searches about suicide or self-harm, and review the search terms report regularly for anything that slips through.

Every landing page should still show how to get urgent help, with your country's emergency number and a crisis line, because anyone can arrive from any search.

## Licensing and location

- **Advertise only where clinicians are licensed** to practise, including for online therapy, where licensing often depends on where the client is
- **Set locations to people who are in your area,** not people interested in it, so ads do not reach clients you cannot see. [Google Ads location targeting](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/) explains the setting
- **Insurance:** state which insurers or schemes you work with, only if this is accurate and current

## Health advertising rules

Mental health services fall under Google's healthcare policies:

- **Personalised advertising is restricted.** Google does not allow targeting based on health conditions, so remarketing to people who read about anxiety or depression is generally not allowed
- **Addiction services need certification.** Recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services need Google certification and are limited to certain countries
- **Prescription medicine names** are restricted in most countries, which matters for psychiatric services
- **Claims:** avoid promises of outcomes or timescales for recovery

[What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) covers these rules in more detail.

## Write calm, specific ads

- Describe the service and the clinician's approach, not the problem in alarming terms
- Mention practical details people care about: online or in person, evening sessions, waiting times
- Offer a gentle first step, such as a short introductory call or the first session

## Protect privacy

Therapy enquiries are highly sensitive. Keep booking forms short, never ask for details about someone's mental health on an ad landing page, and agree the tracking approach on booking pages with whoever handles compliance. In some countries, health privacy law, such as HIPAA in the US, affects how tracking tools may be used.

## Measure first sessions and continued care

Track first sessions booked and attended, and whether clients continue. Record the source of each new client in your practice software, using only what you need, and compare cost per client with the value of a course of sessions.

## Where therapy budgets leak

- Free helplines and charity services, where you do not provide them
- Self-help, quiz and information searches with no intent to book
- Counselling courses, qualifications and jobs
- Medication searches
- Locations where your clinicians are not licensed

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for therapists?

Yes, when campaigns target searches for therapists, counselling and specific therapy types, exclude crisis searches, and measure first sessions booked. Many practices find clients through search because people look for help privately.

### Should therapy practices advertise on crisis searches?

No. People in crisis need immediate help, not a booking page. Exclude crisis and emergency terms with negative keywords, and show urgent help information on every landing page.

### Can therapists use remarketing on Google?

Generally not for mental health. Google restricts personalised advertising based on health conditions, so remarketing to people based on mental health content is not allowed.

### Can online therapy practices advertise in other areas?

Only where clinicians are licensed to work with clients in that location. Set location targeting to match licensing, and check the rules for every area you target.

### What should a therapy practice track in Google Ads?

First sessions booked and attended, and whether clients continue. Keep tracking minimal and privacy-safe on booking pages.
