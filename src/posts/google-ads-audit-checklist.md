A Google Ads audit checklist should run in this order: conversion tracking first, then account settings and structure, search terms and keywords, bidding and budgets, ads and assets, landing pages, audiences and targeting, Shopping and Performance Max, and finally reporting. Tracking comes first because every other number in the account depends on it. The 51 checks below follow that order, and each one says where to look in Google Ads.

## How to use this checklist

Work through it with the account open and a date range of at least the last 30 days, or 90 for smaller accounts. For each check, mark it **OK**, **Fix** or **Critical**:

- **OK:** set up correctly
- **Fix:** worth improving, but not losing money today
- **Critical:** costing money right now

Do not skip ahead. A brilliant bidding change on top of broken tracking makes things worse, not better. For the reasoning behind this order, see [what a Google Ads audit should actually check](/learn/what-a-google-ads-audit-should-check/).

## 1. Conversion tracking (8 checks)

Where to look: **Goals, then Conversions, then Summary.**

1. Every primary conversion action is a real lead, call or sale, not a page view, scroll or button click
2. Nothing is counted twice, for example a GA4 import and a Google tag recording the same form
3. Counting is set to **One** for leads and **Every** for purchases
4. Purchase values are passed and roughly match your store or CRM
5. Enhanced conversions are switched on and the diagnostics show no errors
6. Offline or CRM conversions are imported where sales close after the click. See [enhanced conversions for leads](/learn/google-ads-enhanced-conversions-for-leads-guide/)
7. Conversions in Google Ads roughly match your own records for the last 30 days
8. Consent mode is set up for visitors in the UK and EEA

**Critical if:** page views or clicks are primary conversions, or Google Ads reports far more conversions than your business actually received.

## 2. Account settings and structure (7 checks)

Where to look: **Campaign settings** for each campaign, and **Recommendations, then Auto-apply.**

1. Brand and non-brand searches run in separate campaigns
2. Campaigns are split by real differences: service, margin, location or goal
3. The location option is **Presence** for businesses serving people locally. See [the location setting that wastes budget](/learn/google-ads-location-targeting-settings/)
4. Search partners and the Display Network are switched on only where they perform
5. Auto-applied recommendations are reviewed and switched off where unwanted
6. The ad schedule matches the hours you can answer calls and enquiries
7. Campaign goals match the business goal, such as leads rather than page views

## 3. Keywords and search terms (6 checks)

Where to look: **Insights and reports, then Search terms**, and **Tools, then Shared library** for negative keyword lists.

1. Search terms report reviewed for the last 30 to 90 days
2. Search terms with spend and no conversions listed and costed. The [n-gram analyser](/tools/n-gram-analyser/) totals them by repeated words
3. Negative keyword lists exist and are applied to the right campaigns
4. No negative keyword blocks a keyword you are bidding on. The [negative keyword generator](/tools/negative-keyword-generator/) checks for conflicts
5. Match types suit the budget, bidding strategy and conversion volume. See [match types explained](/learn/google-ads-keyword-match-types-explained/)
6. High-spend keywords with a low Quality Score identified. Add the Quality Score columns in the Keywords view

**Critical if:** a large share of spend goes on search terms that have nothing to do with what you sell.

## 4. Bidding and budgets (5 checks)

Where to look: **Campaigns view**, with the bid strategy, **Search lost IS (budget)** and **Search lost IS (rank)** columns added.

1. The bid strategy suits the amount of reliable conversion data. See [which bid strategy to use](/learn/which-google-ads-bid-strategy-should-you-use/)
2. Target CPA or ROAS is realistic against recent actual results
3. Campaigns limited by budget identified, profitable ones first
4. Impression share lost to budget and lost to rank reviewed
5. The budget split follows profitability, not habit. The [break-even ROAS calculator](/tools/break-even-roas-calculator/) shows the minimum return each campaign needs

## 5. Ads and assets (6 checks)

Where to look: **Ads**, **Assets**, and the **policy status** column for disapprovals.

1. Every ad group has a responsive search ad with distinct headlines
2. Headlines include the main search terms and a clear offer. See [how to write headlines that get shown](/learn/how-to-write-google-ads-headlines/)
3. Pinning is used only where it is genuinely needed
4. Sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets and call assets are added
5. No disapproved or limited ads or assets left unresolved
6. Ad strength reviewed as a guide, not chased as a goal

## 6. Landing pages (5 checks)

Where to look: the **Landing pages** report, then the pages themselves on a phone.

1. Each ad group sends traffic to the most relevant page, not the homepage
2. Pages load quickly on a mobile phone
3. The page repeats the promise made in the ad
4. There is a clear next step: visible phone number, short form, obvious button
5. Form and call tracking still fire after a test submission

**Critical if:** the form is broken or the conversion stops recording after a website change.

## 7. Audiences and targeting (4 checks)

Where to look: **Audiences**, the **Devices** segment, and **Insights and reports, then When and where ads showed.**

1. Remarketing and customer lists added, at least in observation mode
2. Performance reviewed by device, with bids or budgets adjusted
3. The user locations report checked for spend outside the service area
4. Demographics reviewed where the policy for your industry allows

## 8. Shopping and Performance Max (6 checks)

Skip this section if you do not run Shopping or Performance Max. Where to look: **Merchant Center product diagnostics** and the **Performance Max campaign reports**.

1. Merchant Center shows no account issues or disapproved products
2. Product titles, GTINs and images are complete and optimised. See [Shopping feed optimisation](/learn/google-shopping-feed-optimisation/)
3. Brand exclusions applied in Performance Max
4. Performance Max channel and search term reports reviewed. See [what Performance Max hides](/learn/performance-max-what-it-hides/)
5. Custom labels separate products by margin or best sellers
6. Asset groups have strong images, headlines and, ideally, video

## 9. Reporting and measurement (4 checks)

Where to look: your **reports**, **Linked accounts**, and **Change history**.

1. Reports show leads or sales and cost per result, not just clicks
2. Brand and non-brand results are reported separately
3. Google Ads is linked to GA4 and auto-tagging is on
4. Change history reviewed for recent large changes

## After the checklist: turn findings into a plan

Once every check is marked:

1. **List every Critical item first,** with a rough monthly cost: spend on wasted search terms, or enquiries being missed
2. **Fix tracking before anything else.** Until conversions are right, every other result is a guess
3. **Remove obvious waste next:** negatives, location settings, broken pages
4. **Then improve:** structure, bidding, ads and landing page tests

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I audit a Google Ads account?

A full audit once a quarter suits most accounts, with a lighter monthly check of search terms, budgets and conversion tracking. Audit straight away after a website change, a tracking change or a sudden drop in results.

### How long does a Google Ads audit take?

For a small or medium account, working through this checklist usually takes a few hours. Large accounts with many campaigns, Shopping feeds or Performance Max take longer, mainly because of the search terms and feed checks.

### What is the most important part of a Google Ads audit?

Conversion tracking. If the account counts page views, button clicks or duplicates as conversions, every report and every automated bidding decision is built on wrong numbers. Check it before anything else.

### Can I audit my own Google Ads account?

Yes. This checklist is written so business owners can work through it themselves. The hardest parts are judging whether tracking is right and estimating what each problem costs, which is where an outside review helps.
