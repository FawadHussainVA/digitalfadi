Calls and emails from Google Ads strategists are usually genuine, but you should verify who is contacting you and treat their advice as suggestions, not instructions. Google's own guidance is to check the identity of anyone claiming to act for Google, and says its representatives have email addresses ending in @xwf.google.com. A strategist can be useful for policy problems or new features, but they see your account rather than your business, so judge every recommendation against your own margins and goals. Never share your password, never let anyone make changes on a call that you do not understand, and ask for recommendations in writing.

## Why this is in the news

In early November 2026, Search Engine Roundtable [reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-strategist-ai-fake-names-photos-42426.html) that an advertiser who declined a call from a Google Ads contractor then received an email from a "Google Ads Strategist" whose name could not be found anywhere and whose profile photo appeared to be AI-generated. The site said it had asked Google about it and had not heard back.

Whatever the explanation turns out to be, the episode is a useful reminder. Advertisers get regular contact from people saying they are from Google, most of it genuine, some of it not, and it is hard to tell from a name and a photo alone.

## Who these strategists are

Google contacts many advertisers through teams of strategists and specialists, many of whom work for Google through its extended workforce rather than as direct employees. That is why their email addresses end in @xwf.google.com rather than plain @google.com.

Their role is to help advertisers use Google Ads features and act on Google's recommendations. Some are experienced and helpful. But a strategist on a short call sees your account settings, not your profit margins, your sales process or which customers are worth having. That is the gap to keep in mind.

## How to check a caller is genuine

1. **Ask them to email you first.** Google's help page on representatives suggests exactly this. A genuine strategist will have no problem sending one
2. **Check the email address.** Google says its representatives' addresses end in @xwf.google.com. Be wary of free email accounts or lookalike domains
3. **Never give your password or a verification code.** Google does not need either. Anyone asking for them is not to be trusted, whoever they claim to be
4. **Do not pay anyone who calls you.** Google Ads is paid through the billing settings inside your own account, not by card over the phone or by bank transfer to a person
5. **If in doubt, end the call.** You can always contact Google Ads support yourself from inside your account

## What a strategist call is good for

Genuine strategists can help with:

- **Policy problems,** such as disapproved ads or a suspended account, where they can sometimes point you to the right team
- **New features and betas** you might not hear about otherwise
- **Technical setup,** such as conversion tracking checks, if you have no specialist of your own

## Where to be careful

Recommendations from Google, whether on a call or in the Recommendations tab, tend to point the same way: broader keywords, more automation, higher budgets, and more of Google's own campaign types. Some of those suit some accounts. None of them is automatically right for yours.

**Do not let anyone make changes live on a screen share** unless you understand each change and could undo it. A common outcome of a rushed call is broad match switched on everywhere, or budgets raised, without anyone checking what happened to cost per enquiry afterwards. [Google Ads match types explained](/learn/google-ads-keyword-match-types-explained/) covers why broad match needs care.

**Check your auto-apply settings.** In the Recommendations section of Google Ads, auto-apply can make changes to your account without asking. Review which types are switched on, and turn off any you would not approve one by one.

**Ask for everything in writing.** A written list of what they suggest, and why, lets you check each item against your own numbers or ask someone independent before acting.

**Watch the change history afterwards.** Under Tools, Change history shows every change and who made it. [How to tell if your Google Ads agency is doing anything](/learn/is-your-google-ads-agency-doing-anything/) explains how to read it.

## If someone already manages your account

If a freelancer or agency manages your Google Ads, pass any call or email from Google to them rather than agreeing to changes yourself. Two people changing the same account without talking to each other is a reliable way to lose track of what worked.

If you do not have anyone, and you are not sure whether a strategist's suggestions are right for you, that is a good moment for an independent second opinion. [What a Google Ads audit should actually check](/learn/what-a-google-ads-audit-should-check/) explains what a good one looks at.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google Ads strategist calls a scam?

Usually not. Most calls and emails come from genuine Google representatives. But scammers do impersonate Google, so verify the caller by asking for an email and checking that the address ends in @xwf.google.com before discussing your account.

### What does @xwf.google.com mean?

It is the email domain Google says its representatives use. The "xwf" refers to Google's extended workforce, people who work for Google through other companies rather than as direct employees.

### Do I have to follow a Google Ads strategist's recommendations?

No. Recommendations are optional. Judge each one against your own costs and goals, and ignore those that would raise spend without a clear reason to expect more enquiries or sales.

### Can a Google Ads strategist make changes to my account?

Only if you give them access or let them guide you through changes on a call. Check what was changed afterwards in the change history, and review your auto-apply recommendation settings so that changes are not made without you.

### How do I stop Google Ads strategists from calling me?

You can ask the caller to stop, and you can review the notification and contact preferences in your Google Ads account to limit what you receive.
