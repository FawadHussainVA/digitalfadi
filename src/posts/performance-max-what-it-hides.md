Performance Max is the campaign type people either defend loudly or distrust completely, usually without data either way. The complaint that it is a black box is half true. It conceals more than Search does. It conceals far less than most advertisers assume, because almost nobody goes looking.

## The thing it hides that costs the most

Performance Max will bid on searches for your own company name unless you stop it.

Brand searches convert cheaply. Somebody typing your name has already decided. So the campaign reports a low cost per acquisition and a high return on ad spend, and the dashboard looks excellent.

None of it is new business. Most of those people would have reached you through the organic listing for nothing. The campaign has not grown anything, it has moved existing revenue into a paid line.

Worse, it takes that traffic from your Search campaigns, which then look like they have deteriorated and often get cut. You end up paying for demand you had and removing the campaign that was finding new demand.

Brand exclusions fix this. Apply them, then re-baseline. The numbers almost always look worse afterwards, and they are finally true.

## What it genuinely does not show by default

- Which channel the spend went to: Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover or Gmail
- Which placements on the Display and video side
- Search terms at the detail Search campaigns give you
- Which individual asset drove which conversion

That is a real loss of control, and it is why Performance Max is not right for every account.

## What can be extracted anyway

Most of the above is reachable if somebody bothers.

**Channel breakdown.** Available through scripts that query the campaign's channel-level performance. This is the first thing to pull, because it tells you whether you bought Search traffic at Display prices.

**Placement reporting.** The placement report shows where impressions ran, including the apps and channels most advertisers would exclude on sight.

**Search categories.** The insights page groups the searches that triggered the campaign. Not as granular as a search terms report, but enough to see whether it is buying brand, category or nonsense.

**Asset performance.** Each asset is rated, which tells you what to replace even if attribution is not exact.

**Listing group performance** for retail, which shows which products carried the campaign.

If your agency says none of this is visible, that is a statement about the agency.

## Asset groups are not optional structure

The most common structural fault is one asset group holding everything.

One asset group means one message for every product or service, and no way to tell which one worked. Performance Max then optimises towards whatever converts most easily, which is usually your cheapest product or your brand.

One asset group per product line or service. It is more work to build and it is the difference between a campaign you can read and one you cannot.

## Audience signals are hints, not targeting

People set audience signals expecting them to restrict who sees the adverts. They do not. They tell the system where to start looking, and it expands from there.

That makes the quality of the signal important. A customer list or a converter audience is a far better starting point than a broad interest category. Feeding it your own first-party data is one of the few genuine levers you have.

## When Performance Max is the wrong answer

- **Too few conversions.** It needs conversion data to learn. A handful a month means it spends while it waits.
- **Tracking is not reliable.** It optimises towards whatever it is told a conversion is. Fix measurement first or it will efficiently buy the wrong thing.
- **Brand is nearly all the demand.** It will mostly rebuy what you already had.
- **You need placement control.** Some regulated categories cannot accept the inventory it uses.

In those cases a properly structured Search campaign usually does more. Saying so costs an agency a retainer, which is why it is rarely said.

## How to judge it in your own account

Three questions, all answerable:

1. Are brand terms excluded? If not, every number from the campaign is inflated.
2. What does the channel breakdown show? If most spend went to Display while the campaign was sold as Shopping, you know what you bought.
3. What happened to Search campaign performance when Performance Max launched? If Search got worse by exactly the amount Performance Max got better, nothing was added.
