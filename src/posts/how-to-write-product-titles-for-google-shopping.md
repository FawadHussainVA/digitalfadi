The product title is the most important field in a Shopping feed and the one most likely to have been left as the manufacturer supplied it.

Google matches searches to products largely through the title. If the words a buyer types are not in it, the product is not eligible, and no bid fixes that.

## The structure that works

There is no single correct formula, but this order holds up across most categories:

**Brand, then product type, then the attributes people search by.**

- Weak: `SKU-4471 Blk 10`
- Better: `Nike Air Zoom Pegasus Running Shoes`
- Best: `Nike Air Zoom Pegasus 40 Men's Running Shoes Black Size 10`

The third version is eligible for searches on brand, model, category, colour, gender and size. The first is eligible for almost nothing.

## Front-load what matters

Google truncates titles in the display, and the beginning carries more weight in matching. Put the words people search first and the specifications last.

If a buyer types "waterproof hiking boots", the word waterproof should not be sitting in position fourteen behind a warranty note.

## Match the order people type

Watch how buyers actually phrase it in your category. For apparel it is usually brand, product, colour, size. For electronics it is brand, model number, key specification. For parts it is often the compatibility first: "fits Toyota Corolla 2015".

The search terms report on your Shopping campaigns and your own site search are the best sources for this. You are not guessing, the data is already there.

## Category by category

- **Apparel:** brand, gender, product type, colour, size, material
- **Electronics:** brand, model, product type, key spec such as capacity or screen size
- **Parts and accessories:** brand, product, then compatibility
- **Consumables:** brand, product, variant, size or count
- **Furniture:** brand, product type, material, colour, dimensions

## Mistakes that remove products from the auction

**Promotional text.** "Free shipping", "best seller", "50% off" and "sale" in a title breach policy and can get the item disapproved.

**All capitals.** Treated as promotional. Use normal capitalisation.

**Keyword stuffing.** Repeating terms to catch more searches reads badly and hurts both relevance and click-through.

**Your internal codes.** An SKU in the title helps your warehouse and nobody else.

**Missing the obvious word.** Catalogues frequently omit the plain category word. A product called "Pegasus 40" without "running shoes" in it misses every category search.

## Length

Google allows 150 characters and displays far fewer, often around 70 on mobile.

Write for both. The first 70 characters should stand alone and make sense. The rest carries the attributes that help matching even when they are not shown.

Do not pad to reach the limit. A clear 80 character title beats a stuffed 150.

## Test rather than assume

Titles are not a one-time job.

1. Change titles for one product group, leave the rest alone
2. Wait for enough impression data, usually two to four weeks
3. Compare impressions and click-through rate for that group against the control
4. Roll out what worked

Impressions are the signal that matters here. More impressions means more searches you became eligible for, which is what titles control.

## Where this fits

Titles are the single biggest lever in Shopping, but they sit alongside attributes, identifiers and availability accuracy. A title cannot rescue a product that is disapproved for a missing GTIN.
