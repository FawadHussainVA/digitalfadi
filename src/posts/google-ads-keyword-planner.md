Google Ads Keyword Planner is a free tool inside your Google Ads account that suggests keywords, shows how often they are searched, and estimates what clicks may cost. Use it to plan which searches to target before you spend money. Treat its numbers as guidance rather than guarantees.

This guide shows how to use Google Ads Keyword Planner step by step, how to read its numbers, and the mistakes that lead to wasted budget.

## Where to find Google Ads Keyword Planner

Sign in to Google Ads, open **Tools**, then **Planning**, then **Keyword Planner**. You need a Google Ads account, but you do not need a running campaign to use it. Accounts with little or no ad spend may see search volumes shown as ranges rather than exact figures.

## The two main tools

**Discover new keywords.** Enter words that describe your products or services, or a website, and Keyword Planner suggests related searches with volumes and bid estimates.

**Get search volume and forecasts.** Paste a list of keywords you already have to see their volumes, trends and a forecast of clicks and cost at a given budget.

## How to use Google Ads Keyword Planner step by step

1. **Start with what customers type,** not your internal product names. "Emergency plumber" beats "24-hour plumbing solutions"
2. **Set the right location and language.** Volumes and costs change a lot by country and city
3. **Review the suggestions** and keep only searches that show intent to buy or book
4. **Group keywords by theme,** such as each service, so each group can have its own ads and landing page
5. **Check top of page bid ranges** to see what clicks may cost in your market
6. **Use forecasts** to test whether your budget can buy enough clicks to learn from

## How to read the numbers

- **Average monthly searches:** a rough guide to demand, often rounded and grouped
- **Three-month and year-on-year change:** whether interest is rising or falling
- **Competition:** how many advertisers bid on the keyword, not how hard it is to rank organically
- **Top of page bid (low and high range):** what advertisers have historically paid to show near the top. Your real cost per click depends on your quality and competition at the time

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing keywords only by volume.** High volume, vague searches often waste budget. Specific, lower volume searches usually convert better
- **Ignoring intent.** "How to fix a boiler" and "boiler repair near me" have very different value
- **Trusting forecasts as promises.** They are estimates based on past data
- **Forgetting negatives.** Keyword Planner shows what to target; you still need [negative keywords](/learn/how-to-find-negative-keywords/) to block what you do not want
- **Skipping match types.** How a keyword matches searches matters as much as the keyword. [Google Ads match types explained](/learn/google-ads-keyword-match-types-explained/) covers the options

## After Keyword Planner

The best keyword data comes from your own account once ads run. The search terms report shows what people actually searched before clicking, which is more reliable than any planning tool. Use Google Ads Keyword Planner to start, then let real search terms guide your changes. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Ads Keyword Planner free?

Yes. Keyword Planner is free to use inside a Google Ads account. You do not need to run ads to access it, though accounts with little spend may see search volumes as ranges.

### Why does Keyword Planner show ranges instead of exact search volumes?

Accounts with little or no active ad spend often see volumes as ranges. Accounts that run campaigns usually see more precise figures.

### Are Keyword Planner search volumes accurate?

They are estimates, often rounded and grouped with similar searches. Use them to compare keywords and spot trends, not as exact counts.

### What does competition mean in Keyword Planner?

It shows how competitive advertiser bidding is for that keyword in Google Ads. It does not measure how hard it is to rank in organic search results.

### Can Keyword Planner tell me my exact cost per click?

No. It shows historical top of page bid ranges. Your actual cost per click depends on your bids, ad quality, landing page and competition at the time.
