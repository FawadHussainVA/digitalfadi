Google Ads billing depends on the payment setting you choose: automatic payments charge you after you advertise, manual payments let you add money first, and monthly invoicing, for eligible businesses, sends you an invoice to pay later. Invoices and statements are in the Billing section of your account. Most confusion comes from payment thresholds and surcharges.

This guide explains how Google Ads billing works, where to find documents, and why charges sometimes look unexpected.

## The three payment settings

**Automatic payments.** You are charged after your ads run, either when your costs reach a payment threshold or a set number of days after your last charge, whichever comes first. The threshold usually starts low and rises over time as you pay successfully. This is the most common setting.

**Manual payments.** You add money to the account first, and costs are deducted as ads run. Ads stop when the balance runs out, so top up in time.

**Monthly invoicing.** Eligible businesses receive a line of credit and a monthly invoice to pay by bank transfer or other methods. You need to apply and meet Google's requirements.

## Where to find invoices and statements

Open **Billing**, then **Documents**. Depending on your setting and country, you will find monthly statements, payment receipts and tax invoices. Make sure your business name, address and tax details are correct in your payments profile, because invoices use them.

## Why Google Ads billing can look confusing

- **Charges do not match daily budgets.** Google can spend up to twice your average daily budget on a single day, while keeping the monthly total within the limit
- **Several charges in a month.** With automatic payments, each time you reach the threshold you are charged
- **Credits and adjustments.** Promotional credit and invalid traffic credits appear as adjustments. [Click fraud in Google Ads](/learn/google-ads-invalid-traffic-click-fraud/) explains invalid activity credits
- **Taxes and surcharges** added on top of ad costs

## Surcharges changing in 2027

In some countries Google adds a regulatory operating cost surcharge to ads served there, billed on top of your budget. Google has announced that the surcharges for ads served in France and Italy rise to 3% on 1 January 2027, while the UK rate stays at 2%. If you advertise in those countries, include the surcharge in your cost planning.

## Avoid billing problems

- Keep a backup payment method, so ads do not stop if a card fails
- Settle failed payments quickly. Unpaid balances can lead to suspension. [Google Ads account suspended](/learn/google-ads-account-suspended/) covers what to do if that happens
- Use payment details that match your business and advertiser verification
- Check that the person managing your ads cannot change billing without your knowledge. Your ad spend should be paid directly to Google from your own account

## Freelancer and agency fees are separate

Google Ads billing covers only what Google charges for ads. If someone manages your account, their fee is separate and should not be mixed into your Google invoice. [Google Ads management cost](/learn/google-ads-management-cost/) explains how management fees work.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does Google Ads billing work?

It depends on your payment setting. Automatic payments charge you after ads run, manual payments use money you add first, and monthly invoicing sends eligible businesses an invoice to pay later.

### Where can I download my Google Ads invoice?

In your Google Ads account, open Billing, then Documents. You will find statements, receipts and tax invoices depending on your country and payment setting.

### Why was I charged more than my daily budget?

Google can spend up to twice your average daily budget on a single day when there are more opportunities, while keeping monthly spend within your overall limit.

### What is a Google Ads payment threshold?

It is the amount of costs that triggers an automatic charge. It usually starts low and increases as you make successful payments.

### What happens if a Google Ads payment fails?

Your ads can stop running, and unpaid balances can lead to account suspension. Add a backup payment method and settle failed payments quickly.
