Google Ads works for dermatology clinics when medical and cosmetic skin care are run as separate campaigns. Someone worried about a changing mole and someone comparing laser treatments are searching for different reasons, with different urgency, value and advertising rules.

Advertise consultations rather than medicines, keep claims accurate, and judge every campaign on consultations booked and treatment accepted. For the wider approach across every kind of clinic, see [Google Ads for clinics](/google-ads-for-clinics/).

## Medical and cosmetic are two different businesses

**Medical dermatology.** Mole checks, skin cancer screening, acne, eczema, psoriasis, hair loss and rashes. People want a specialist they can trust, often soon. The value is in the consultation and any follow-up care.

**Cosmetic dermatology.** Laser treatments, skin resurfacing, pigmentation, scar treatment and anti-ageing procedures. People compare clinics, prices and results over weeks. Higher value per patient, more competition, and stricter rules on claims and images.

When both share one campaign, the budget follows whichever is cheaper to click, which is rarely the work the clinic wants most.

## Build campaigns around concerns and treatments

- **Urgent medical concerns:** mole checks and skin cancer screening, with availability in the ad
- **Chronic conditions:** acne, eczema, psoriasis and hair loss, where patients often want a long-term plan
- **Each cosmetic treatment** in its own ad group with its own landing page
- **Brand searches** for the clinic's name, kept separate so they do not flatter the account

People usually travel further for a dermatologist than for a GP, so a wider location can work, especially for cosmetic treatment.

## Rules that catch skin clinics out

- **Prescription medicine names.** In most countries, Google does not allow prescription drug terms in ads, keywords or landing pages without certification. Several common skin and anti-ageing treatments involve prescription products, so advertise the consultation or treatment type, not the medicine
- **Claims.** Avoid promises such as clear skin guaranteed or permanent results
- **Before-and-after images.** Use only your own patients, with consent, and only where your regulator and Google's policies allow them
- **Personalised advertising.** Google restricts targeting based on health conditions, so remarketing to people who read about a skin condition may not be allowed

[What clinics can and cannot advertise on Google Ads](/learn/healthcare-google-ads-policy-for-clinics/) explains these rules in more detail.

## Landing pages that earn the booking

- **Medical pages:** the concern in the headline, who will see the patient and their qualifications, how soon appointments are available, and how referral or self-pay works
- **Cosmetic pages:** what the treatment involves, how many sessions, recovery time, a realistic price range where you can publish one, and a consultation as the next step

Both need booking and a phone number at the top. [Dental landing pages that turn ad clicks into bookings](/learn/dental-landing-pages/) covers the same principles for another kind of clinic.

## Measure consultations and treatment

Track consultations booked and attended, and for cosmetic work, treatment plans accepted with their value. A cosmetic campaign with a high cost per consultation can still be the most profitable in the account if enough consultations become courses of treatment.

## Where skin clinic budgets leak

- Skincare products, creams and at-home device searches
- Home remedy and DIY searches
- Dermatology training, jobs and courses
- Free public services, where you do not provide them
- Searches for treatments you do not offer

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Google Ads work for dermatology clinics?

Yes, when medical and cosmetic care are separate campaigns, ads stay within health advertising rules, and the clinic tracks consultations and accepted treatment rather than clicks.

### Can dermatology clinics advertise treatment names?

Treatment types, such as laser resurfacing or mole checks, can usually be advertised. Prescription medicine names are restricted in most countries, so advertise the consultation or treatment type instead of the product.

### Should medical and cosmetic dermatology share a campaign?

No. They differ in urgency, value, competition and rules. Separate campaigns let each have its own budget, ads and landing pages.

### Can skin clinics use before-and-after photos in ads?

Only where your regulator and Google's policies allow them, with patient consent and your own patients' images. Rules vary by country, so check before using them.

### What should a dermatology clinic track in Google Ads?

Consultations booked and attended, and for cosmetic treatment, plans accepted and their value. Cost per accepted treatment shows profitability better than cost per click.
