To track new patients from dental Google Ads, count the actions that become appointments, not every click or call. Set up conversions for booked appointments and qualified calls, record where each new patient came from in your practice system, and send booked or accepted treatment back to Google Ads where you can. Keep patient privacy in mind throughout: collect only what you need, and check which health privacy rules apply to tracking on your website.

Without this, Google Ads reports clicks and calls that look busy but cannot show which campaigns bring patients. Bidding then learns to buy more calls, not more patients.

## Decide what counts as a conversion

Not every action is worth the same. Separate them:

| Action | Usually a primary conversion? |
|---|---|
| Online appointment booked | Yes |
| Call from an ad, long enough to be a real enquiry | Yes, once you have checked call quality |
| Contact form submitted | Yes |
| Click on the phone number on the website | No, it may not lead to a call |
| Visit to the contact page | No |
| Treatment plan accepted | Yes, as an offline conversion with its value |

Only primary conversions should guide bidding. Keep the others as secondary, for reporting only. [Why are my Google Ads not converting?](/learn/why-are-my-google-ads-not-converting/) covers how wrong conversion settings mislead automated bidding.

## Track calls properly

Most dental enquiries start with a phone call, so call tracking matters more than in most industries.

- **Call assets in ads** can be tracked directly, with a minimum call length you choose
- **Calls from the website** need a forwarding number or call tracking on the site, so Google Ads can tell which ad led to the call
- **Choose a sensible call length.** Very short calls are often wrong numbers. Too long a threshold misses quick bookings. Listen to a sample of calls to set it

Then check quality. Compare the number of tracked calls with the appointments your reception team actually booked from them.

## Track online booking

If patients book online, the booking confirmation is usually the best conversion you have.

- Fire the conversion on the confirmation step, not when the booking page opens
- If the booking system is on another domain, check the tracking still works after the patient returns or confirms, and test it with a real booking
- Retest after any change to the website or booking system, because these often break tracking silently

## Record the source in the practice

Online data only goes so far. Ask the reception team to record how each new patient found the practice, and keep the source in the practice management system. Then you can compare:

- New patients from Google Ads each month
- Which treatments they booked
- The value of their first treatment, and their longer-term value

This is the number that tells you whether advertising pays.

## Send real outcomes back to Google Ads

Google Ads learns faster from outcomes than from enquiries. Where you can, import later stages back into the account:

- **Appointments attended**
- **Treatment accepted,** with its value, especially for implants and cosmetic work

Offline conversion import and [enhanced conversions for leads](/learn/google-ads-enhanced-conversions-for-leads-guide/) connect an enquiry to its outcome. Give high-value treatment a higher conversion value, so bidding favours the searches that bring it. [Dental implant Google Ads](/learn/google-ads-for-dental-implants/) shows why this matters for implant campaigns in particular.

## Patient privacy comes first

Dental enquiries can include health information, so tracking needs care:

- **Collect only what you need** in forms, and avoid asking for medical details in an ad landing page form
- **Check which privacy rules apply.** In some countries, health privacy law covers how tracking tools may be used on pages where patients book or share health details. In the US, for example, HIPAA can apply. Agree the approach with whoever handles compliance for the practice
- **Respect consent.** Where cookie consent is required, consent mode should be set up so tracking follows the visitor's choice
- **Be careful with remarketing.** Google limits some personalised advertising around health, so check which audiences your account allows

## A quick tracking check

1. Make a test booking and confirm the conversion records once, not twice
2. Call from a test ad click and confirm the call is tracked
3. Compare last month's Google Ads conversions with appointments booked from Google Ads in your practice system
4. Check the conversion settings: primary for real enquiries, secondary for everything else

If the numbers are far apart, fix tracking before changing bids or budgets.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a dental practice count as a conversion?

Booked appointments, qualified calls and form submissions as primary conversions, and accepted treatment as an offline conversion with its value. Page visits and phone number clicks should be secondary at most.

### How do I know which Google Ads calls became patients?

Compare tracked calls with appointments booked by reception, and record the source of each new patient in the practice system. Listening to a sample of calls also shows which ones were real enquiries.

### Can I send booked appointments back to Google Ads?

Yes, through offline conversion import or enhanced conversions for leads. Matching depends on capturing the right details at the time of enquiry, so plan this when you set up forms and call handling.

### Does health privacy law affect dental Google Ads tracking?

It can. Rules vary by country, and some health privacy laws cover tracking on pages where patients book or share health details. Check with whoever handles compliance before adding tracking to those pages.

### Why do Google Ads conversions not match my bookings?

Common causes are duplicate conversions, counting phone number clicks or page visits as conversions, calls too short to be real, or tracking broken by a website change. Test each conversion with a real action to find the gap.
